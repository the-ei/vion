# Scene Plan: The Solstice Noon - Ruth

## Summary

Ruth Abramson, alone in her DC apartment on the winter solstice, writes. Not legal opinions anymore - that work is behind her - but something else: an essay, a letter, a document for her children about what she has learned. She thinks about death: Susan's death six years ago, which broke her; her colleagues and contemporaries dying over the years; eventually her own. The congressional commission has released its report, incorporating her testimony, recommending reforms that might matter. Or might not. Ruth no longer needs certainty about outcomes. She is 65, maybe with time remaining, maybe not. She writes about institutional faith and its limits, about what persists when structures fail: human connection, specific care, the small scale where love is possible. She writes anyway, whether anyone reads it or not.

## Key Elements

- Writing as Ruth's mode of reckoning completion
- The document for her children: transmitting what she's learned
- Thinking about death: Susan's, others', eventually her own
- The commission report released: her testimony made public record
- The uncertainty about outcomes: reforms might matter, might not
- Ruth's acceptance of uncertainty as wisdom
- What persists when institutions fail: connection, care, scale
- Writing as act: meaningful regardless of audience

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson** (65): POV character completing her reckoning through writing
- **Susan Abramson** (memory): Ruth's late wife, whose presence persists in the apartment
- **David and Rebecca** (referenced): Her children, the intended audience for what she writes
- **Elena Varga** (referenced): The nurse Ruth thinks of, whose work embodies what law couldn't achieve
- **Congressional commission members** (referenced): The institution that received and processed her testimony

## Setting

- Ruth's Dupont Circle apartment in Washington DC
- Her study, where she has always written, legal texts lining the walls
- Susan's presence in objects: the piano, the art, the books
- The winter solstice noon: brief daylight, the shortest day
- The view from her window: the street, the city, the nation's structures visible and distant
- The document she writes: paper, pen, the old technology of transmission

## Timeline

- December 2038, winter solstice day
- Scene spans approximately four hours: 10 AM through 2 PM
- The congressional commission report released earlier this week
- Susan died six years ago; the anniversary is near

## Page Target

5-6 pages

## Stylistic Notes

- Open with Ruth at her desk: the specific posture of writing, the light from the window
- The document emerging in fragments: what she wants David and Rebecca to know
- Susan's presence in the apartment rendered through objects, not narration
- The commission report mentioned but not detailed: outcomes uncertain, that's the point
- Knausgaard interiority about death: not morbid but clear-eyed
- The writing itself appearing on the page: what she believes, what she doubts, what persists
- End with Ruth still writing, the day's brief light beginning to fade
