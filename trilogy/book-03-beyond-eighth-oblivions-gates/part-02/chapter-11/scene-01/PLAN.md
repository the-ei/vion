# Scene Plan: The Cascade

## Summary

Yusuf Hassan is on a delivery shift in Minneapolis when his phone begins pinging with notifications that will alter his life's trajectory. A debt from the crisis years - money borrowed to help his family survive Book 2's economic collapse - has been sold to a collections agency. His credit score, already damaged, drops further. An apartment application he submitted last week is denied. The algorithmic systems governing gig work begin their quiet punishment: his ratings drop, premium shifts become unavailable, the invisible hierarchy of gig economy labor pushes him down. He finishes the shift because he needs the money. Afterward, sitting in his aging Honda in a parking lot, he tallies the cascade and makes a call he hasn't made in years - to Kevin Zhou, the tech engineer whose path crossed his during the crisis, who once offered help that Yusuf was too proud to accept.

## Key Elements

- The delivery shift as frame: the mundane work that defines Yusuf's days
- The cascade of notifications, each one compounding the previous
- The algorithmic punishment made visible: lower ratings, fewer shifts, systemic exclusion
- Yusuf's calculation of the damage: specific numbers, specific debts
- The memory of how this debt was incurred during Book 2's crisis
- His decision to call Kevin Zhou - a bridge across class that Yusuf rejected before
- The parking lot as space of reckoning: between deliveries, between identities
- Pride as both protection and prison

## Characters

- **Yusuf Hassan** (27): POV character facing the accumulated consequences of crisis-era choices; his anger and intelligence trapped by systemic constraint
- **Kevin Zhou** (32): Voice on phone at scene's end, the unexpected connection from Book 2 offering something Yusuf can't yet name
- **Collections agent** (phone notification): The impersonal voice of debt systems
- **Gig app algorithms** (presence): The invisible arbiters of Yusuf's economic life
- **Jamal** (memory): Friend from crisis years, dead from overdose - one trajectory Yusuf avoided
- **Ahmad** (memory): Another crisis-era contact, currently incarcerated - another trajectory avoided

## Setting

- Minneapolis streets, late afternoon, March 2038
- Yusuf's car, a 2012 Honda Civic with 180,000 miles, the mobile office of gig work
- The delivery destinations: suburban houses, apartment complexes, the economic geography of service
- The parking lot of a strip mall where Yusuf stops to process the notifications
- His phone screen, the portal through which algorithmic judgment arrives

## Timeline

- Spring 2038, continuing Part 2's chronology
- Scene spans approximately five hours: 3 PM shift start through 8 PM parking lot call
- The debt dates to 2035-2036, the crisis years of Book 2
- Kevin Zhou hasn't heard from Yusuf in nearly three years

## Page Target

6-7 pages

## Stylistic Notes

- Open with the rhythm of delivery work: addresses, packages, the bodily routine
- The notifications should interrupt the rhythm, Carson-style fragments breaking Knausgaard flow
- Interior monologue calculating the cascade: specific numbers, specific consequences
- Memory of Jamal and Ahmad as brief, sharp intrusions - the alternative paths
- The call to Kevin Zhou should feel reluctant, each digit of the number a small defeat of pride
- End with the conversation beginning, not concluded - what Kevin offers remains unclear
