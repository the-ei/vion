# Scene Plan: The Numbers

## Summary

Elena Varga sits in an examination room at the community health clinic where she has worked for nearly a decade - but today she sits on the patient side. Dr. Reyes, a former colleague who now serves as her physician, delivers the results of tests ordered after Elena reported persistent fatigue, joint pain, and heart palpitations. The numbers are abnormal: elevated thyroid antibodies, inflammatory markers, patterns consistent with autoimmune thyroiditis. Not cancer - Elena's worst fear - but something that requires further investigation and will not simply resolve. As Dr. Reyes explains the implications, Elena's clinical knowledge becomes both asset and curse. She knows too much to be falsely reassured, too much to panic cleanly. Driving home through Phoenix afternoon traffic, her body feels suddenly foreign. Alone in her empty house, she confronts the accumulated cost of years spent caring for everyone but herself.

## Key Elements

- Elena as patient rather than provider, role reversal that destabilizes identity
- Dr. Reyes delivering results with the careful precision Elena herself uses with patients
- The medical language Elena knows intimately now applied to her own body
- Autoimmune implications: the body attacking itself as metaphor for caregiving burnout
- The drive home through Phoenix traffic, ordinary city indifferent to personal crisis
- The empty house: abuela at a neighbor's, children at school, space for reckoning
- Elena sitting with the results, having nowhere to direct her care but inward
- The years of medicating anxiety, skipping meals, surrendering sleep now presenting their bill

## Characters

- **Elena Varga** (37): POV character receiving diagnosis, confronting the body she has instrumentalized for others' care
- **Dr. Reyes**: Elena's physician, a former colleague who understands both the medical facts and the personal weight; careful, compassionate, direct
- **Elena's body** (as presence): Her heartbeat, her exhaustion, the physical reality she has ignored now demanding attention
- **Abuela, Sofia, Mateo** (absence): Their temporary absence creates the solitary space needed for this confrontation

## Setting

- Community health clinic examination room, the same fluorescent-lit space where Elena has delivered countless diagnoses
- Dr. Reyes's office for the detailed conversation
- Elena's car, the 2019 Honda she drives to and from work daily, now a space of containment
- The Phoenix streets, February afternoon, winter sun low and golden
- Elena and Daniel's house, quiet, the accumulated objects of family life surrounding her absence of focus

## Timeline

- The children return from school at 3:30, picked up by abuela; Elena is alone from 4-6 PM

## Stylistic Notes

- Begin with clinical precision: the examination room, the paper gown, the familiar equipment now alien
- Dr. Reyes's delivery should be professional but warm, recognizing Elena as colleague-turned-patient
- Elena's interior response should layer medical knowledge with emotional processing
- The drive home in Knausgaard mode: attention to every traffic light, every billboard, the strangeness of continuation
- At home alone, shift toward Carson compression: fragments, gaps, the inadequacy of language for body-shock
- End not with despair but with the beginning of inward attention
