# Scene Plan: Daniel Comes Home

## Summary

Daniel Varga drives from a construction job in Tucson when Elena calls with the news. Three hours later, he walks through their front door, and the couple confronts something they have been avoiding: the state of their marriage, the accumulated distance of logistics and exhaustion, the question of whether they can be present for each other when presence is finally required. The scene moves from awkward reunion through Elena's breakdown - not just about the diagnosis but about everything she has carried - to Daniel's admission that his absence has been partly escape. They fight, then stop fighting, then sit together in the silence that marriage sometimes requires. The children need dinner; life continues around the crisis. But something has shifted between them.

## Key Elements

- Daniel's three-hour drive as commitment made visible
- The awkward reunion of people who love each other but have become strangers to intimacy
- Elena's breakdown: not the diagnosis alone but the accumulation of years
- Daniel's confession: his absences weren't only economic necessity
- The fight that surfaces long-buried resentments
- The resolution that isn't resolution but willingness to stay in the room
- Sofia and Mateo's presence demanding that parents function despite crisis
- The marriage tested and, tentatively, holding

## Characters

- **Elena Varga** (37): POV character, breaking down in ways she hasn't allowed herself, needing Daniel to see her
- **Daniel Varga**: Husband, construction worker, driving back from Tucson; carries his own guilt about absence, his own fear about what Elena's health means
- **Sofia Varga** (9): Eldest child, present in the house, sensing something wrong, needing reassurance
- **Mateo Varga** (6): Younger child, aware of tension but not its source, needing normalcy
- **Abuela** (present but peripheral): Seeing the couple's struggle, holding the household together as she always has

## Setting

- The Varga home in Phoenix, early evening
- The master bedroom where the private conversation happens
- The kitchen where dinner must still be made
- The living room where family life continues
- Daniel's truck in the driveway, dusty from the construction site
- The February evening, cool for Phoenix, darkness falling early

## Timeline

- Early 2038, same day as the diagnosis (Scene 1)
- Scene spans evening: 6 PM (Daniel's arrival) through 10 PM (children asleep, couple still awake)
- Daniel left the job site at 3 PM, drove three hours
- The conversation unfolds in stolen moments between parenting duties

## Page Target

7-8 pages

## Stylistic Notes

- Open with Daniel's arrival: the truck, his boots, the physical presence of a man who works with his hands
- The initial conversation stilted, both wanting to discuss and unable to
- Elena's breakdown should be Knausgaard-detailed: the specific words, the physical sensations of crying
- Daniel's confession delivered plainly, without elaborate language - a man who doesn't have sophisticated vocabulary for emotion but has the emotion
- The children's interruptions providing structure: crisis punctuated by "what's for dinner?"
- End with the couple together, not resolved but committed to the work of resolution
