# Scene Plan: Preparation

## Summary

Ruth Abramson meets with congressional commission staffers in her Washington DC apartment - the same Dupont Circle space she has lived in for thirty years, now too large since Susan's death. They are preparing her for testimony before the commission reviewing the Eighth Oblivion response. The staffers want sanitized answers: What regulatory frameworks existed before the crisis? Why did they fail? What should have been different? But Ruth finds herself unable to give the institutional language they expect. She keeps thinking of Susan - her late wife would have cut through the careful legalese to ask the only question that mattered: Did people suffer less because of you? The preparation session becomes a confrontation with Ruth's own doubts. She begins drafting testimony more honest than anyone expects.

## Key Elements

- The DC apartment as repository of Ruth and Susan's life together
- Young commission staffers representing institutional continuity
- The gap between what they want Ruth to say and what she needs to say
- Susan's memory as moral compass, cutting through institutional language
- Ruth's review of her own legal career in preparation for testimony
- The question crystallizing: did her work matter?
- The drafting of honest testimony as act of courage or self-destruction
- Ruth's age and mortality as context for assessment

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson** (64): POV character preparing to testify about whether her life's work mattered
- **Jennifer Park** (30): Lead commission staffer, sharp, respectful, wanting Ruth to be useful without being dangerous
- **Michael Okafor** (28): Junior staffer, taking notes, his youth making Ruth feel the distance of generations
- **Susan Abramson** (memory): Ruth's late wife, a physician, whose voice Ruth still hears asking the essential questions
- **David and Rebecca** (referenced): Ruth's children, whose different responses to systemic failure she carries

## Setting

- Ruth's Dupont Circle apartment, fourth floor of a pre-war building
- The living room where Susan's presence persists: her books, her art, the piano she played
- Ruth's study where the preparation happens, legal texts lining walls
- The view of the tree-lined street, spring beginning
- Washington beyond the windows: the city of institutions Ruth has served and questioned

## Timeline

- Summer 2038, Part 2's midpoint
- Scene spans one afternoon: 2 PM (staffers arrive) through 6 PM (they leave)
- The testimony is scheduled for three days hence
- Flashbacks to Susan's illness and death six years prior

## Page Target

6-7 pages

## Stylistic Notes

- Open with the apartment: Knausgaard attention to objects that carry Susan's presence
- The staffers' arrival shifts to dialogue, their professional language contrasting with the personal space
- Ruth's interior response to their prepared questions: legal analysis mixed with memory
- Susan's voice in Ruth's head rendered without quotation marks, a presence rather than a character
- The shift to honesty should feel like risk: Ruth knows the consequences but proceeds
- End with Ruth alone, beginning to write what she actually believes
