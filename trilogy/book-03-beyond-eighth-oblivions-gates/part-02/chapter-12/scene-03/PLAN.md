# Scene Plan: David and Rebecca

## Summary

After the testimony, Ruth has dinner with both her children - a rare gathering. David, who works in finance, is concerned about her candor: it could damage her legacy, invite lawsuits, make their family name controversial. Rebecca, a social worker, is moved but worried about her mother's emotional state: this felt like grief made public, like Susan's death finally finding expression. The dinner becomes about more than the testimony - about what each of them has chosen, about Susan's absence that shapes every family gathering, about what Ruth hoped to give her children and what she actually could. The chapter ends with Ruth alone, writing a letter to Elena Varga - someone she barely knows but whose work represents what Ruth's legal frameworks couldn't achieve.

## Key Elements

- The rare gathering of mother and children, charged by the day's events
- David's pragmatic concern: legacy, lawsuits, family reputation
- Rebecca's emotional concern: her mother's grief, her mother's isolation
- The contrast between David (accommodation to power) and Rebecca (ground-level care)
- Susan's absence as the unnamed presence at the table
- What Ruth wanted to transmit to her children versus what she actually did
- The letter to Elena as reaching toward connection outside family
- Ruth alone at night, writing what she knows while she still can

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson** (64): POV character processing the day's testimony through her children's responses
- **David Abramson** (40): Ruth's son, finance professional in New York, whose career represents working within systems Ruth critiqued today
- **Rebecca Abramson** (38): Ruth's daughter, social worker in Boston, whose career represents ground-level response to systemic failure
- **Susan Abramson** (memory): The wife and mother whose absence shapes every family gathering
- **Elena Varga** (referenced): The nurse practitioner Ruth thinks of, whose work embodies what law couldn't achieve

## Setting

- A restaurant in DC, chosen for neutrality - not Ruth's apartment, not weighted with Susan
- The table where Ruth faces both children, their different lives visible in their clothes, their phones, their postures
- Later, Ruth's apartment, alone at her desk
- The letter she writes to Elena: physical paper, handwritten, the intimacy of old technology
- Night falling over DC, the city's monuments visible from Ruth's window

## Timeline

- Summer 2038, evening of testimony day
- Scene spans approximately five hours: 6 PM (dinner) through 11 PM (Ruth alone, writing)
- David took the Acela from New York; Rebecca flew from Boston
- Both will leave tomorrow morning
- The letter to Elena may never be sent, but writing it matters

## Stylistic Notes

- The restaurant scene in dialogue-heavy mode: the children's different concerns voiced clearly
- Ruth's interior response to each child: what she hears beneath their words
- Susan's absence rendered through specific details: the dish Susan would have ordered, the joke Susan would have made
- The shift to Ruth alone at home: Knausgaard solitude, the weight of empty rooms
- The letter to Elena in fragments: Ruth writing, crossing out, trying again
- End with Ruth still writing, the content less important than the act of reaching toward someone who understands the gap between systems and people
