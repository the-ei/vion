# Scene Plan: The Testimony

## Summary

Ruth Abramson sits before the congressional commission in a wood-paneled hearing room, cameras present, the formal architecture of democracy surrounding her. The questions begin as softball: her credentials, her experience, her expertise. But Senator Morrison, pursuing his own political agenda, asks a sharp question that cuts through the ritual: "Judge Abramson, do you believe the legal frameworks you helped develop made any material difference in outcomes for ordinary Americans during the Eighth Oblivion?" Ruth's prepared answer dissolves. She speaks instead about what she actually saw: the elegant legal structures, the careful opinions, the measured language - all of it ultimately unable to prevent power from doing what power does. The room falls quiet. She names specific failures, including her own. This is not the testimony anyone expected.

## Key Elements

- The congressional hearing room as theater of democratic accountability
- The formal ritual of testimony: oath, credentials, opening statement
- Senator Morrison's political motivation producing an honest question
- Ruth's prepared answer dissolving, replaced by truth
- The specific failures she names: her own opinions that were circumvented, frameworks that were ignored
- The gap between legal theory and lived reality made explicit
- The room's silence as response to unexpected honesty
- The cameras capturing a moment that will be replayed

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson** (64): POV character delivering testimony that exceeds what institutions want to hear
- **Senator Morrison**: Commission chair, Republican from Missouri, pursuing political points but accidentally creating space for truth
- **Senator Williams**: Ranking member, Democrat from Michigan, uncomfortable with Ruth's candor
- **Commission members**: A row of faces, some sympathetic, some hostile, most calculating political implications
- **Jennifer Park** (commission staff): Watching from behind the dais, horrified and impressed
- **Cameras and reporters**: The mechanism by which this moment will propagate

## Stylistic Notes

- Open with the room itself: the wood paneling, the flags, the formal architecture of power
- The ritual of oath-taking in precise detail
- Softball questions in summary; Morrison's question in full
- The pivot to honesty rendered in first person, Ruth's voice breaking through legal register
- Carson-style compression for the room's silence after her key admission
- Other senators' reactions observed from Ruth's perspective: the political calculations visible in their faces
- End with the session's conclusion, Ruth walking out past cameras, the consequences beginning
