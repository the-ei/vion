# Scene Plan: The House

## Summary

After the cremation, Ananya returns to her parents' house - the place that was home before America, before Prometheus, before the ethical battles that defined her career. She moves through rooms that hold her childhood like sediment, finding artifacts she had forgotten: school notebooks, photographs, objects that trigger memory cascades. Her father moves through the space with the particular silence of new widowhood, his presence both comforting and painful. Text messages from Delphine in Los Angeles arrive like dispatches from another life, the American existence calling. Priya explores the house with the curiosity of someone meeting her mother's ghost.

## Key Elements

- The family home as repository of memory and artifact
- Ananya sorting through belongings, the archaeology of a life and a childhood
- Her father's silence and grief, the surviving spouse's particular devastation
- Objects from Ananya's childhood: notebooks, photographs, letters, small possessions
- Memory triggered by physical things - the Proustian archaeology
- Priya discovering artifacts of her mother's childhood, seeing Ananya newly
- Text messages from Delphine, checking in, the American life asserting itself
- The question of what to do with the house, with the belongings, with her father
- The Eighth Oblivion's absence here - life that continued regardless of systemic crisis

## Characters

- **Ananya Ramaswamy** (47): POV character, daughter returning to childhood home

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes** (44): Present through text messages, the American friendship

## Setting

- The Ramaswamy family home in Chennai
- November 2041, evening after cremation
- Specific rooms: kitchen, Ananya's childhood bedroom, parents' room, sitting room
- The domestic geography of memory

## Timeline

- Memory time: the decades between Ananya's childhood and now
