# Scene Plan: Morning Preparation

## Summary

Ruth Abramson prepares for her final public lecture in a hotel room at Yale, the institution where she trained decades ago. At 68-69, her body reminds her constantly of its limits - the small accommodations she now makes, the morning rituals that take longer than they once did. She reviews her notes on law, technology, and human dignity, deciding what to say and what to leave unsaid. Susan, her late wife, is present through the internal conversation Ruth still has with the dead. The young Ruth who attended this law school couldn't have imagined becoming this - an elder, summoned to pronounce, aware that pronouncement changes nothing.

## Key Elements

- Ruth in her hotel room at Yale, preparing for the lecture
- Physical details of aging: how her body has changed, accommodations she makes
- Memory of being a student here, the young Ruth who couldn't imagine this
- Reviewing notes: law, technology, human dignity - what to say, what to omit
- Susan's imagined commentary, the conversation with the dead continuing
- The gap between what Ruth knows and what she can say
- Her mortality present: this is likely her last major public address
- The weight of being summoned to pronounce when pronouncement feels inadequate
- Yale itself as palimpsest: her past self beneath her present one
