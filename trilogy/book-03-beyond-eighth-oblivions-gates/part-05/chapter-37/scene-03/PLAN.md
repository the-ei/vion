# Scene Plan: The Vigil

## Summary

The long hours of waiting define this scene - the suspended time when death is coming but has not arrived. The family trades stories about the past, about what's inherited, about who they are because of this woman who made them. Jerome and Denise's marriage is examined through glances, through decades of understanding compressed into small gestures. DeShawn asks about his father's investigations with genuine curiosity, not the skepticism of earlier years. Jerome's mother drifts, the lucidity fading and returning in smaller windows. The moment of death itself arrives - quiet, expected, and still a rupture.

## Key Elements

- The suspended time of vigil, waiting for death
- Family conversations about the past, about inheritance
- Jerome and Denise's marriage examined in the small moments of waiting
- DeShawn's genuine curiosity about his father's work, a bridge forming
- The grandmother drifting in and out, each lucid moment smaller
- What the family talks about when they're waiting together
- The physical experience of vigil: exhaustion, coffee, the room's air
- The moment of death: quiet, medical, the cessation observed
- The rupture that even expected death creates

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington** (59): POV character, son watching, father being watched
- **Jerome's mother** (late 80s): Dying, then dead, the transition witnessed
- **Denise Washington**: Beside Jerome, the marriage present
- **DeShawn Cole** (24-25): Present, connecting with his father across usual distance
- **Jerome's sister**: Keeping vigil, her grief her own
- **Hospice staff**: Professional presence at the moment of death

## Setting

- Late December 2041 / early January 2042

- Time: night into early morning, the death-hour
