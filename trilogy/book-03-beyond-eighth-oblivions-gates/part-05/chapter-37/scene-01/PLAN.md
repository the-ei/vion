# Scene Plan: The Lucid Window

## Summary

Jerome Washington sits alone with his mother when an unexpected clarity returns to her dementia-clouded mind. For the first time in years, she recognizes him fully, speaks coherently, asks about his life with the directness only a mother can. In this window of lucidity, Jerome finds himself explaining his career in terms she can understand - not the awards, not the investigations, but what he tried to do and whether it worked. Her questions cut through his professional self-justification. The Baltimore of her memory emerges as she speaks, the place that shaped them both. Jerome pays exhaustive attention to the medical setting, her body's fragility, the borrowed time this lucidity represents.

## Key Elements

- Jerome's mother's unexpected return to clarity
- The medical setting: hospice room, monitors, the apparatus of dying
- Her direct questions about his life, his work, his family
- Jerome's attempt to explain journalism in terms she can grasp
- Her memories of raising him, what she hoped for him
- The Baltimore she remembers vs. the Baltimore that is
- The gap between professional achievement and simple maternal questions
- The fragility of this window - they both know it will close
- Knausgaard attention to the body in the medical bed, the details of decline

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington** (59): POV character, journalist, son at his mother's side
- **Jerome's mother** (late 80s): Dying, but lucid in this window, the mother returned
- **Hospice staff** (background): The professional caregivers, present but peripheral

## Setting

- Hospice facility or hospital room, East Coast (Baltimore area)
- December 2041 / January 2042
- The specific geography of a dying room: bed, monitors, chairs, windows
- Time: evening, the quiet hours when lucidity sometimes returns

## Timeline

- Scene spans 2-3 hours, the duration of the lucid window
- Memory time: Jerome's career (2000s-2040s), his mother's life
