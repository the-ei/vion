# Scene Plan: The Work

## Summary

After Theo leaves for school in an autonomous vehicle - a departure Delphine watches with residual unease despite three years of practice - she enters her home office to begin her workday. A video call with a foundation program officer about upcoming project funding reveals the landscape of post-crisis "social impact" content: who funds what, what narratives are sanctioned, what remains unspeakable. Delphine then reviews footage from a documentary about climate migration that she's producing, sitting with the gap between the work's importance and its apparent futility. The scene establishes her professional context and the particular flavor of post-crisis complicity.

## Key Elements

- Theo's departure in the autonomous vehicle - the strange intimacy of watching your child driven away by algorithm
- The home office space: professional but domestic, the hybrid workplace of 2037
- Video call with Miranda Chen, foundation program officer - the language of impact philanthropy
- The funding landscape post-crisis: acceptable narratives, forbidden connections
- Review of documentary footage: climate migrants at the Arizona-Sonora border
- The gap between meaningful work and measurable impact
- Delphine's internal accounting: is this better than what she did before?
- Jessie's absence felt through domestic details - her coffee mug, her books, her pillow still dented

## Characters

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes** (POV, 41): Navigating the "social impact" production world
- **Miranda Chen** (via video, 45): Foundation program officer, sympathetic but constrained by institutional pressures
- **Jessie Barnes** (absent): Her presence felt through absence and domestic traces
