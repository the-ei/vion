# Scene Plan: Morning Architecture

## Summary

Delphine wakes in her Los Angeles home to the ambient hum of domestic AI systems already managing the morning. The scene follows her through the intimate ritual of waking, breakfast preparation, and getting seven-year-old Theo ready for school. Through these mundane activities, we establish the texture of 2037 domestic life: the seamless AI integration, the climate adaptations (air quality notifications, water conservation routines), and the persistent undertow of anxiety that has become background noise. The scene culminates in Theo asking a question about his mother's documentary work that cuts uncomfortably close to the heart of her complicity.

## Key Elements

- Opening image: Delphine's consciousness emerging into the house's ambient awareness
- The domestic AI as presence rather than personality - helpful, invisible, slightly unsettling
- Morning light in Los Angeles filtered through climate-adaptive windows
- Theo at seven: curious, matter-of-fact about technologies that still unsettle Delphine
- Breakfast conversation revealing Theo's questions about what his mother does for work
- The kitchen as site of negotiation between technology and human touch
- Delphine's internal monologue about the three years since the crisis
- The culminating moment: Theo asking "Why do your movies make people sad?"

## Characters

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes** (POV, 41): Creative director turned documentary producer, navigating motherhood in the changed world
- **Theo Okafor-Barnes** (7): Growing up in post-crisis reality, accepting as natural what his mother still finds strange

## Setting

- Delphine and Jessie's home in Los Angeles (Silver Lake or Echo Park area)
- Open-plan kitchen and living space with large windows
- Time: Early morning, approximately 6:30-8:00 AM
- Season: Late winter/early spring 2037

## Timeline

- Approximately 90 minutes of story time
- Single continuous morning sequence
- Three years after Book 2's crisis

## Page Target

6-7 pages

## Stylistic Notes

- Open with Knausgaard-style immersion in the sensory details of waking
- The domestic AI should be rendered through effects rather than dialogue
- Theo's questions should land with Carson-like compression amid the flow
- Balance between the comfort of routine and the strangeness of the familiar-made-unfamiliar
- The final question should shift the register, creating a pivot point
