# Scene Plan: The Waiting Room

## Summary

Jerome and Denise at Johns Hopkins for her oncology appointment following the breast cancer diagnosis. The waiting room becomes a study in American healthcare 2037: anxious families, efficient intake systems, the strange democracy of illness. Denise holds his hand. The doctor delivers news that is better than feared but still terrifying - early stage, excellent treatment options, high survival rates. But "survival rates" means death is in the room now, a presence that won't leave. They drive home mostly silent. In the car, Denise finally cries. Jerome feels his world reorder around this new fact, all his concerns about journalism and legacy suddenly distant against the immediacy of his wife's mortality.

## Key Elements

- Johns Hopkins oncology center: the institutional space of serious medicine
- The waiting room as American microcosm: who waits, who fears, who hopes
- The intake process: efficient, algorithmic, impersonal at the edges
- Denise's hand in his: the physical anchor of partnership
- Dr. Adaora Okonkwo: the oncologist delivering complicated news competently
- The statistics: early stage, 93% five-year survival, "excellent prognosis"
- The way good news still contains the shadow of the alternative
- The drive home: silence that isn't empty but full
- Denise crying in the car: the release she couldn't have in the office
- Jerome's world reordering: everything that seemed important recategorized

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington** (POV, 55): Journalist confronting his wife's illness
- **Denise Washington** (54): The patient, receiving news, maintaining composure then releasing
- **Dr. Adaora Okonkwo** (45): Oncologist, competent, overworked, compassionate within constraints
- **Other patients and families** (observed): The waiting room population
