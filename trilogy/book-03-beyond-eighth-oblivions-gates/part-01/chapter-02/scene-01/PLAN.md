# Scene Plan: Morning Duties

## Summary

Jerome arrives at his sister Grace's house in Baltimore where their mother Evelyn, 86, now lives with advanced dementia. The scene follows the morning routine of caregiving - the repetitions, the patience required, the small moments of unexpected clarity that break through the fog. His mother asks about his father as if he's still alive (he died twelve years ago), and Jerome must navigate the particular grief of being unrecognized by someone who made you. Grace, exhausted from primary caregiving, updates him on medical appointments and logistics. The scene establishes Jerome's capacity for intimate, sustained attention even as his public work has become distant and abstract.

## Key Elements

- The drive from Jerome's home to Grace's house through Baltimore neighborhoods
- Grace's house: modest, adapted for elder care, full of their mother's relocated possessions
- The morning routine: medication management, breakfast assistance, the careful dance of dementia care
- Evelyn's moments of clarity interspersed with confusion - asking for Jerome's father as if alive
- The specific grief of caring for a parent who sometimes doesn't know you
- Grace's exhaustion and the sibling negotiation of caregiving burden
- Medical appointment logistics: the bureaucracy of aging in 2037
- Jerome inhabiting tenderness that contrasts with his public persona as relentless investigator

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington** (POV, 55): Investigative journalist caring for his aging mother
- **Evelyn Washington** (86): Jerome's mother, dementia progressed, lucid moments increasingly rare
- **Grace Washington** (58): Jerome's older sister, manages primary care, stretched thin
- **Jerome's father** (deceased): Present through Evelyn's confused questions, through memory

## Setting

- Grace's house in a Baltimore neighborhood (Northwood or Morgan Park area)
- Single-story home adapted for elder mobility
- Time: Early morning, approximately 7:00-10:00 AM
- Late winter 2037, gray Baltimore light

## Timeline

## Stylistic Notes

- Deep Knausgaard immersion in the physical details of care work
- The small gestures of assistance rendered with full attention
- Evelyn's confusion should be handled with dignity, not pathos
- The sibling dynamic economically established through small exchanges
- A moment of Carson-like compression when Evelyn briefly recognizes Jerome, then loses him again
- The weight of accumulated family history present in objects and gestures
