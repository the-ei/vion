# Scene Plan: The Position

## Summary

The op-ed. Ruth has been asked to write for a major publication, staking her position clearly on the AI governance question. She drafts it in her study at home, Susan's photo on the desk. The writing forces clarity - she must say what she actually believes about institutions, about law, about power. The piece becomes more radical than she intended: a careful argument that the post-crisis frameworks were designed to fail, that true reform requires structural change the current system cannot deliver. Before publishing, she calls Rebecca (who applauds the stance) and David (who warns of consequences). She publishes anyway. The chapter ends with Ruth awaiting the response, having crossed a line she cannot uncross.

## Key Elements

- Ruth's home study: the private space of thought
- Susan's photo on the desk: the imagined counsel Ruth can't have
- The op-ed assignment: a major publication wants her voice
- The writing process: clarity forcing commitment
- The argument that emerges: more radical than intended
- Post-crisis frameworks designed to fail: specific critique
- Structural change required: Ruth saying what she believes
- Rebecca's call: daughter applauding, pushing further
- David's call: son warning, worried about consequences
- The decision to publish: irreversible commitment
- Awaiting response: the uncertainty of having acted
