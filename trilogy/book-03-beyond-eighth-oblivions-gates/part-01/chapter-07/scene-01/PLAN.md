# Scene Plan: The Brief

## Summary

Ruth in her Berkeley office, working on an amicus brief with a team of law professors. The case centers on whether AI companies can be held liable for algorithmic harms under the post-crisis governance frameworks - the answer will determine whether the reforms were real or theater. The legal work is absorbing, familiar, alive in a way that teaching alone isn't. Ruth finds purpose in the precision of argument. A call from her son David interrupts: his financial firm has clients on the wrong side of this case, and her involvement is already being noted in certain circles. Ruth tells him she's an independent scholar; he tells her there's no such thing. She returns to the brief with something hardened in her, understanding that this is no longer academic.

## Key Elements

- Berkeley Law office: the workspace of academic legal practice
- The amicus brief: supporting plaintiffs in a major AI liability case
- The legal question: algorithmic harm liability under post-crisis frameworks
- Ruth's team: three other law professors, collaborative legal work
- The work itself: satisfying in ways teaching isn't
- David's call: warning from the finance world
- The conflict: Ruth's independence versus the web of interests
- David's clients have stakes; Ruth's involvement creates awkwardness
- Ruth's response: defiance tempered by awareness of cost
- The hardening: this is not academic anymore
