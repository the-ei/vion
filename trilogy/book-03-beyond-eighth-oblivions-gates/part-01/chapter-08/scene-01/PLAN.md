# Scene Plan: The Offer

## Summary

Kevin Zhou receives a message from DeShawn, excited: Prometheus wants to talk about acquiring his startup. To DeShawn, this is validation, success, the dream. To Kevin Zhou, who knows what Prometheus absorbs and what it destroys, this is a trap. He knows the pattern: promising startups acquired, founders retained briefly then marginalized, technology integrated into systems that serve consolidation. A tense video call where Kevin Zhou tries to warn without crushing ambition - he knows how he would have responded to such warnings at 20. DeShawn is respectful but unconvinced; the generational and experiential gap feels vast. Kevin Zhou realizes that warning isn't enough; he needs to try something else.

## Key Elements

- DeShawn's message: Prometheus acquisition interest, excitement palpable
- Kevin Zhou's immediate recognition: the pattern, the trap
- What Kevin Zhou knows about Prometheus: from Books 1-2 and from industry knowledge
- The video call setup: two screens, two perspectives
- Kevin Zhou's attempt to warn: the difficulty of transmitting experience
- DeShawn's position: validation feels good, opportunity feels real
- The generational gap: what Kevin Zhou at 20 could hear, couldn't hear
- DeShawn's respectful dismissal: he's hearing fear, not wisdom
- Kevin Zhou's realization: warning isn't working, he needs another approach
- The decision forming: who might actually reach DeShawn?
