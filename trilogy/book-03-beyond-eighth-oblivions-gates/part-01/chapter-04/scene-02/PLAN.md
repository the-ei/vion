# Scene Plan: Walking Through

## Summary

Afternoon. Kevin Zhou walks through Oakland neighborhoods - a practice he started in Berlin during his wandering years and has maintained since returning. The walk becomes meditation and observation: through Chinatown, Temescal, along Lake Merritt. Oakland in 2037 shows the marks of climate and economy - the adaptations visible in architecture and faces, the mix of resilience and strain. He thinks about his parents in Shenzhen, increasingly unreachable as US-China relations have complicated travel and communication. A brief video call with his mother - the connection poor, the love clear, the distance vast. She asks when he's coming home; he doesn't have an answer, and they both know why.

## Key Elements

- The practice of walking: how Kevin Zhou learned to occupy space differently
- Oakland neighborhoods as text: what the city reveals about 2037
- Chinatown: familiar and foreign, connecting Kevin Zhou to a heritage he left
- Lake Merritt: the walking loop, other walkers, the persistence of public space
- Climate adaptations visible: drought-resistant landscaping, cooling stations, air quality signs
- Economic stratification visible: who has resources to adapt, who doesn't
- The video call with his mother: technological connection across political distance
- US-China tensions rendering family relationships difficult to maintain
- The question of home: where it is, whether return is possible, what was lost

## Characters

- **Kevin Zhou** (POV, 31): Walking through the city, processing distance and belonging
- **Kevin Zhou's mother** (via video, 62): In Shenzhen, aging, worried, proud, far away
- **Oakland residents** (observed): The city's population as presence, not speaking characters
