# Scene Plan: The Oracle

## Summary

Wednesday. Ruth delivers a lecture at Stanford on "Constitutional Resilience in the Post-Oblivion Era" to an audience of tech executives, policy advisors, journalists, and students. She speaks carefully, her public voice more modulated than the classroom honesty that surprised her Monday. Yet her argument contains radical doubt disguised as moderation. The Q&A reveals the tech sector's preferred narrative - the system held, the crisis was managed, innovation continues - and Ruth's gentle pushback draws blood without seeming aggressive. Afterward, alone in her hotel room in Palo Alto, she calls her daughter Rebecca. They debate whether teaching is enough, whether Ruth should move toward more direct action. Ruth defends the long game while suspecting she no longer believes in it.

## Key Elements

- Stanford campus: the architecture of tech-adjacent prestige
- The lecture hall: mixed audience with competing investments in the narrative
- Ruth's carefully constructed argument that sounds moderate but isn't
- Q&A as intellectual combat disguised as civil exchange
- Specific questioners: the tech executive seeking validation, the student seeking clarity
- The hotel room afterward: Ruth's solitary processing
- Phone call with Rebecca: mother-daughter dynamic, different theories of change
- Ruth's internal debate: institutional work versus direct action
- The image of Susan: what would she counsel?

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson** (POV, 64): Public intellectual performing certainty she no longer feels
- **Tech executives** (audience): Invested in the "system worked" narrative
- **Policy advisors and journalists** (audience): Various positions and agendas
