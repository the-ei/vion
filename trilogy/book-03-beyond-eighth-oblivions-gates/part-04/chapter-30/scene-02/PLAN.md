# Scene Plan: What He Built

## Summary

Kevin Zhou revisits his trajectory through memory: the MIT prodigy who published at 20, the Prometheus years where he built without questioning, the startup that was implicated in Book 2's crisis, the testimony that followed, the reckoning that has not completed. He examines these moments not to judge himself but to understand what drove him - the need to matter, to prove he was real, to build something that would outlast him. The rivalry with Ananya Ramaswamy surfaces: her ethics position that he dismissed as theater, his own ethical blindness that she perhaps saw clearly. Both accusations may be true. The scene is an archaeology of motivation, not confession.

## Key Elements

- MIT prodigy years: the early brilliance
- Prometheus: building without questioning
- The startup and Book 2's crisis: technology implicated
- Testimony and public reckoning
- The Ananya rivalry: her ethics theater vs. his ethical blindness
- What drove him: need for significance, fear of irrelevance
- Memory not as confession but as understanding
- The trajectory from builder to questioner incomplete
