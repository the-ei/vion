# Scene Plan: The Vigil

## Summary

Elena Varga sits beside her abuela's bed in the hospice. The room is simultaneously familiar and strange - Elena knows these spaces professionally from years of nursing work, but inhabiting one as family transforms every surface, every beep of a monitor, every soft footfall of staff. The scene unfolds in extended attention to abuela's breathing, the small adjustments of a body preparing to stop. Woven through the present vigil are memories triggered by abuela's hands: hands making tortillas in a kitchen from Elena's childhood, hands checking her forehead for fever, hands holding newborn Sofia. The hands rest on the hospital sheets now, gnarled from decades of labor, still except for the involuntary twitches of sleep. Elena's double vision - family member and healthcare professional - creates a particular anguish: she knows too much about what is happening, cannot take refuge in ignorance.

## Key Elements

- The hospice room: clinical space made intimate by vigil
- Abuela's breathing: the rhythm of departure
- Elena's double vision: professional knowledge meeting personal grief
- Memory sequences centered on abuela's hands through the years
- The particular quality of waiting for death
- Phoenix light through the hospice window
- Monitors and equipment Elena knows too well
- Physical details of the dying body without clinical distance
- The silence broken by machinery

- Time marked by breath and light rather than clock

- The body's vocabulary: medical detail held at emotional distance
- Hands as recurring image organizing memory and present
- Silence as presence, not absence
