# Scene Plan: The Crossing

## Summary

Abuela dies in the early morning. Elena is there, holding the hands she has known all her life. The actual moment is both enormous and ordinary - a last breath that is not different from the breaths before it, except that no breath follows. The scene moves into Carson compression: fragmentary, almost elliptical, white space around the facts of death. Elena's threshold is not grief but integration. The rage she has carried at the system and the care she has provided within it reveal themselves as one force, not two. After the death, after the procedures, Elena walks outside into the Phoenix morning. The chapter ends not with resolution but with quiet clarity - the knowledge of what she has always been doing, finally unified.

## Key Elements

- The moment of death: vast and small simultaneously
- Holding abuela's hands through the crossing
- The pause when breathing stops
- Procedural aftermath: what happens after death in a hospice
- Elena's emotional integration: rage and care as one force
- Walking outside into Phoenix morning
- The body left behind, the hands released
- Grief as backdrop to clarity
- Light and heat as Elena exits the building
