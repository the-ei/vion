# Scene Plan: Sofia and Mateo

## Summary

The children come to the hospice. Sofia at 14 wants to appear strong but keeps looking at Elena for cues on how to behave, what expression to wear, whether it is permitted to cry. Mateo at 11 asks when bisabuela will wake up, and Elena must find words that don't lie but don't terrify - the impossible language of preparing children for death. The three of them arrange themselves around the bed: generations present to witness the departure of the oldest. Elena sees in her children what she must have looked like to abuela decades ago: raw, unfinished, desperately loved. The scene is about transmission - what passes between generations in these threshold moments, what the children will carry from this room into their lives.

## Key Elements

- Sofia (14): Performing maturity, seeking permission to feel
- Mateo (11): The questions that cut through adult evasion
- Three generations around the bed: geometric arrangement
- Elena finding language for death that serves her children
- What children absorb in these moments
- Abuela's continuing presence: still breathing, still here
- The contrast between adolescent and pre-adolescent processing
- Elena's memory of herself as a child facing loss
- Physical gestures: holding hands, touching the bed

## Characters

- **Elena Varga** (43): POV character, mother translating death for her children
- **Sofia Varga** (14): First encounter with death of someone beloved
- **Mateo Varga** (11): The questions adults cannot answer
- **Abuela** (late 80s): Present but unconscious, the still center
