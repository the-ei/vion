# Scene Plan: The Performance

## Summary

Evening, the space filled with community members, old and young. Yusuf performs three songs he wrote years ago and one he wrote for tonight, specifically for this space, for his father. The music carries everything he couldn't articulate - his father's thwarted ambitions, his own years of survival, the precarity that shaped them both, something like hope. The audience includes people who remember the accident, people from the gig economy organizing Yusuf was part of, and strangers who came for the arts space opening. Amina records on her phone. When Yusuf finishes, he steps offstage shaking, having given something away that he didn't know he'd been holding.

## Key Elements

- The performance space filled: maybe 150 people, folding chairs and standing room
- Yusuf taking the stage, the terror and the necessity
- The first three songs: written years ago, about precarity, about waiting, about fathers
- The fourth song: new, written for tonight, specifically about this space's transformation
- What the music carries: grief, anger, hope, the unsayable made sound
- The audience's range: old neighbors, fellow organizers, strangers, families
- Specific faces Yusuf sees: Mr. Tadesse, a woman he delivered groceries to, a city councilwoman
- Amina recording, her face as she hears her brother do what he's avoided
- The applause, the aftermath, stepping offstage
- The physical release: shaking, tears, something emptied and filled

## Characters

- **Yusuf Hassan (29-30):** POV character, performing what he's carried
- **Amina Hassan (21-22):** Witness, recorder, moved sibling
- **The audience:** Collective character, bearing witness
- **Mr. Tadesse:** Visible in the crowd, hearing his dead friend's son
- **Yusuf's father (in the music):** Present through what the songs carry

## Stylistic Notes

The performance rendered differently: lyrics might appear (italicized or offset), but the focus is on Yusuf's embodied experience of playing, the sound in the room, the faces he glimpses. Knausgaard-mode for the detailed physical experience of performing - hands on strings, breath into microphone, the space between songs. Carson-mode for the aftermath: shaking offstage, Amina finding him, few words exchanged. The final paragraphs should feel like release, like something completed but also just begun.
