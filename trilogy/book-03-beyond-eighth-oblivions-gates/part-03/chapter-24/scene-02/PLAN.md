# Scene Plan: Discussion

## Summary

Q&A with the film students. Their questions are sharp and sometimes naive. They see "The Invisible Shift" as a historical document, reading meanings Delphine didn't consciously intend. A student asks about the silhouetted workers image - the one Delphine fought to keep - and reveals she wrote a paper about it, arguing it's a critique of surveillance even though the film was commissioned by a company implicated in surveillance. Professor Reyes offers a theory about crisis-era aesthetics that makes Delphine uncomfortable in its accuracy. Delphine tries to answer honestly about the commission, the constraints, what she was and wasn't able to say.

## Key Elements

- The Q&A format: students with prepared questions, some spontaneous
- A question about the commission: "Did you feel compromised making this?"
- Delphine's honest answer: yes, but also no, but also yes
- The student with the paper: analyzing the silhouette image
- The gap between artist's intent and critical reception
- Professor Reyes's theory: crisis-era content makers performed complicity as critique
- Delphine's discomfort: is that what she did? Did she mean to?
- Other questions: about the gig workers she interviewed, what happened to them
- The question she can't answer: did this film change anything?
- The students as inheritors: they'll teach this film, write about it, remember it differently

## Characters

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes (42-43):** POV character, trying to answer honestly
- **Film students:** Sharp questioners, some naive, all serious
- **Student with paper (Marisol):** Represents the next generation's interpretation
- **Professor Adelina Reyes:** Academic theorist, offering frameworks Delphine didn't have

## Setting

- Same screening room, lights up
- Post-screening discussion format
- The film's final image still on screen behind Delphine

## Timeline

- Present-time action rendered closely
- References to the film's production and aftermath

## Stylistic Notes

Carson-mode: fragmentary dialogue, the gap between question and answer. Short exchanges with silences where Delphine searches for honesty. The student's paper argument rendered in her words, Delphine's response inadequate to the complexity. Professor Reyes's theory in more formal language, academic register intruding. Some Knausgaard expansion when Delphine processes internally - the gap between what she meant and what they see. The scene should feel like interrogation without hostility, interpretation without permission.
