# Scene Plan: What Survives

## Summary

Evening, alone in her hotel room near campus, processing. Delphine calls Jessie, who is both proud of her and weary of her ongoing reckoning. Theo gets on the phone, asking if Mom is famous now. The question cuts through the adult complexity. Delphine thinks about what survives of any creative life - the compromised work and the pure work judged by different criteria, by people who weren't there. She looks up other crisis-era content creators, sees whose work endured and whose disappeared, finds no clear pattern. The chapter ends with Delphine accepting that the archive will preserve what it preserves regardless of her intentions - and that this might be okay.

## Key Elements

- The hotel room: generic, quiet, the day's intensity settling
- The call to Jessie: processing the screening, the questions
- Jessie's reaction: pride, weariness, ongoing complexity of their marriage
- Theo's interruption: "Are you famous, Mom?"
- The simplicity of that question against all the adult complexity
- Looking up other crisis-era creators: whose work survived?
- No clear pattern: some compromised work endured, some pure work disappeared
- The randomness of cultural survival
- A specific creator who did better work than Delphine whose name is forgotten
- Ending on acceptance: the archive has its own logic, and fighting it is futile

## Stylistic Notes

Blended mode: the phone call with dialogue in Carson compression - short exchanges, Theo's interruption. The research section in Knausgaard expansion - specific creators, their work, what happened to them. The final reflection returns to compression - shorter sentences, acceptance rendered simply. The scene should feel like exhaling, like letting go of control over meaning. Theo's question - "Are you famous?" - echoes differently than expected, revealing Delphine's real concern isn't fame but worth.
