# Scene Plan: The Screening

## Summary

Delphine arrives at UCLA's School of Theater, Film and Television for a screening and discussion of her crisis-era short film "The Invisible Shift." She's been told the film is now part of the curriculum - taught as a significant cultural artifact of the Eighth Oblivion period. The film was something she made under commission, for a company that wanted to seem compassionate about gig workers. She's always dismissed it as compromised work. But now, watching it with students who were children during the crisis, she sees both the compromises and something genuine underneath that she didn't know she'd put there.

## Key Elements

- Arrival at UCLA: the film school building, the professor who invited her
- The screening room filling with students, approximately 40
- Watching "The Invisible Shift" - a 15-minute documentary about gig workers
- Remembering the production: what she was asked to include, what she fought to keep
- What she cut: the more damning material, the system critique
- What she didn't cut: a specific image of workers silhouetted against screens
- The students watching: their faces, their attention, their youth
- Delphine's doubled vision: seeing the film and seeing herself making it
- The ending credits rolling, the room lights coming up
- The weight of being studied, of her compromises becoming history

## Characters

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes (42-43):** POV character, watching her own work historicized
- **Professor Adelina Reyes:** The academic who has studied Delphine's work
- **Film students (approximately 40):** Young people inheriting crisis-era culture
- **Delphine's past self (in memory):** The 35-year-old making the film

## Setting

- UCLA School of Theater, Film and Television
- A screening room, tiered seating, professional projection
- Summer 2040, late afternoon
- The institutional architecture of film education

## Timeline

- The screening itself spans approximately 25 minutes (film plus settling)
- Present-time action dilates this through Delphine's experience
- Flashbacks to 2035, when the film was made

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard-mode: the film rendered in detail, Delphine's memory of making it layered over the watching. Long paragraphs about specific shots, specific choices, what they meant then and what they seem to mean now. The physical experience of watching yourself from years ago, with strangers who see differently. The students as witnesses whose reactions Delphine can't read. The scene should feel like confrontation with a past self who made choices the present self must now account for.
