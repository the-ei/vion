# Scene Plan: Confrontation

## Summary

Yusuf meets with his organizing community - the gig workers he's been advocating alongside for years. He tells them about the job offer, expecting congratulations. Some are happy for him. Others challenge him: "So you're out now? Moving on?" A fellow organizer named Darius, someone Yusuf respected, accuses him of abandonment. The cooperative exists because of their collective struggle, and now Yusuf is joining management while others remain precarious. Yusuf defends himself but hears the accusation's partial truth. This is a meeting Amina couldn't understand - she escaped through academia, a path that doesn't carry this particular guilt.

## Key Elements

- The meeting space: a community center basement, folding chairs, fluorescent lights
- The organizers gathered: approximately 15 people, mostly gig workers
- Yusuf's announcement: expecting support, receiving complexity
- Those who are happy: they understand what stability means for his family
- Those who challenge: Darius especially, articulate and furious
- The accusation: "You built this with us and now you're leaving us behind"
- Yusuf's defense: "The cooperative exists because of this work"
- The partial truth he can't deny: he IS leaving, in some sense
- His music as similar question: is stability death or possibility?
- The meeting ending unresolved, relationships strained

## Characters

- **Yusuf Hassan (30):** POV character, facing judgment from comrades
- **Darius Washington (mid-40s):** Fellow organizer, the one who challenges him most sharply
- **Other organizers:** A range of responses, supportive and skeptical
- **Amina (absent but referenced):** Her path as contrast, different escape route

## Setting

- Community center basement, Phillips neighborhood, Minneapolis
- Folding chairs in a circle, coffee in paper cups
- A Saturday afternoon
- The space where organizing has happened for years

## Timeline

- Saturday afternoon, approximately 2pm to 4pm
- The meeting spans 2 hours
- References to years of organizing together

## Stylistic Notes

Carson-mode: the confrontation rendered in quick exchanges, gaps where Yusuf can't respond. Darius's accusation given full weight, not dismissed. Short paragraphs of dialogue, then silence. What's said and what can't be said about solidarity, survival, and individual escape. Some Knausgaard expansion when Yusuf processes internally - the history he has with these people, what they've built together, what he's leaving. The scene should feel like rupture, like something tearing that can't be fully mended.
