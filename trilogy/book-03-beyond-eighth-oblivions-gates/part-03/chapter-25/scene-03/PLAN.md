# Scene Plan: Decision

## Summary

Yusuf walks through the neighborhood where he grew up, where his father walked. He thinks about patterns - the precarity that shaped his father, that shaped him, that he might finally break. He calls Amina, who makes the case for taking the job: "Dad would have wanted this for you. You can still organize, still make music. You'll just have time." He thinks about his music - whether stability would enable it or kill it, whether the material of his songs depends on the precarity he's lived. Finally, he calls the Cooperative Logistics Network. The chapter ends not with the answer itself but with Yusuf understanding that either choice carries loss - there's no breaking the pattern without something tearing.

## Key Elements

- Walking the old neighborhood: the streets, the corner where he used to wait for his father
- The physical landscape of precarity: payday loans, check cashing, the businesses that profit from it
- Calling Amina: her argument, clear and practical
- "Dad would have wanted this" - the weight of that claim
- His music: the songs are about precarity, will stability make them false?
- The question of whether artists need suffering (Yusuf rejects this but wonders)
- The call to the cooperative, the answer he gives
- What he's choosing TO is clear; what he's choosing AWAY FROM is also clear
- The acceptance that loss is built in either way
- Ending on the walk home, the decision made, the pattern changed or unchanged

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard-mode dominates: the neighborhood rendered in exhaustive detail, each block carrying memory. Long paragraphs about specific corners, stores, the changes over years. His father's ghost in specific places - the corner where he waited for the bus, the store where he bought lottery tickets. The phone calls provide dialogue breaks, shorter sections. The decision itself understated - not a triumphant moment but a quiet one. The ending should feel like threshold crossed, loss accepted, future uncertain but different.
