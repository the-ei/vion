# Scene Plan: The Exhibition

## Summary

Delphine walks through the gallery space of the Los Angeles Museum of Contemporary Art with Theo, her eight-year-old son. The exhibition she's curating - "After the Wave: Culture and Crisis 2032-2037" - is half-installed, objects arriving in cases, screens being mounted, walls of explanatory text being painted. Each section represents a different aspect of the Eighth Oblivion era: the technology, the economics, the human stories, the cultural responses. Theo asks what things mean, and Delphine finds herself struggling to translate catastrophe into language a child can hold. In the "Media Response" section, she sees a piece she produced during the crisis - an explainer video that went viral - now treated as historical artifact.

## Key Elements

- The gallery space in process: workers, crates, dust sheets, the exhibition becoming
- Each section of the exhibition requiring Delphine to re-encounter the era she helped narrate
- Theo's questions: "What's that?" "Why did that happen?" "Were you scared?"
- The artifacts: screenshots, objects, documents, artworks - each curated with intention
- Delphine's own work displayed as artifact: the disorientation of becoming historical
- A young museum staffer who recognizes Delphine, admires her crisis-era content
- The tension between memory and curation - what gets included, excluded, framed
- Theo wanting to touch things, the physicality of his presence against abstraction
- Delphine's internal reckoning with each curatorial choice as a form of content strategy

## Characters

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes (42):** POV character, curator confronting her own era
- **Theo (8-9):** Her son, asking questions that reveal the inadequacy of adult explanations
- **Young museum staffer (early 20s):** Someone who grew up with Delphine's content
- **Installation workers:** Background presence, building the frame

## Setting

- Los Angeles Museum of Contemporary Art, gallery space
- Summer 2039, mid-morning
- The exhibition half-installed, scaffolding and cases
- Climate-controlled institutional space, outside heat visible through windows

## Timeline

- Two weeks before exhibition opening
- Present-time action spans approximately 3 hours (10am to 1pm)
- Flashbacks to Delphine's crisis-era work throughout

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard-mode: exhaustive attention to the museum space, each artifact described with its curatorial context. Delphine's mental commentary on the choices made - what's included and why. Theo's presence keeps interrupting the adult analysis with simpler, more direct questions. Long paragraphs about specific objects (a protest sign, a server blade, a child's drawing from a shelter). The scene should feel like walking through crystallized memory, the exhibition as attempt to freeze meaning.
