# Scene Plan: Transmission

## Summary

Evening, Theo in bed. Delphine calls her mother in London, seeking a different perspective on legacy and transmission. They discuss what survives - her father's legacy (he was an academic, died two years ago), the stories the Okafor-Barnes family tells, how each generation inherits differently. Her mother asks about the exhibition with genuine curiosity, not the skepticism Jessie brought. The conversation reveals how Delphine's anxieties about legacy echo her mother's own - both women who shaped narratives, who worry about what they've actually transmitted versus what they intended. The chapter ends with Delphine looking at the exhibition catalogue proof, adding a note that acknowledges her complicity more directly.

## Key Elements

- The video call to London: time zone difference, her mother's flat visible behind her
- Her father's death two years prior, what he left (books, ideas, unfinished projects)
- Her mother's perspective on narrative work - she was a teacher, also a shaper of minds
- The family stories: what gets repeated, what gets revised, what disappears
- How Delphine's Nigerian-Welsh heritage shapes her understanding of transmission
- Her mother's question: "Who are you making this exhibition for?"
- The parallel between Delphine's anxieties and her mother's - inheritance of worry itself
- The catalogue proof: text she wrote, now seeming insufficient
- The revision she makes: acknowledging she was part of what she's curating
- Ending on the act of revision, the ongoing nature of reckoning

## Characters

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes (42):** POV character processing the day's confrontation
- **Delphine's mother (late 60s):** Voice from London, different generation's perspective
- **Delphine's father (deceased, in memory):** What he left, what survived
- **Theo (sleeping):** Present as weight, as who Delphine is transmitting to

## Setting

- Delphine's Los Angeles home, evening
- The study where she works, catalogue proofs spread on desk
- Late evening LA time, early morning London time
- The screen connecting two worlds, two generations

## Timeline

- Same day, late evening (10pm LA, 6am London)
- The call spans approximately 45 minutes
- The revision work another hour after
- References to family history across decades

## Stylistic Notes

Blended mode: the video call dialogue has Carson spareness, but Delphine's thoughts between exchanges expand in Knausgaard fashion - memories of her father, her understanding of her mother's parallel anxieties. The revision at the end is rendered in detail: what she changes, what she adds, the physical act of marking the proof. The scene should feel like settling after turbulence, Jessie's critique processed through a gentler lens. The mother's question - "Who is this for?" - reverberates without simple answer.
