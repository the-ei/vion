# Scene Plan: The Confrontation

## Summary

Jessie arrives at the museum cafe to pick up Theo. A tense conversation unfolds while Theo is distracted by a tablet loaded with games. Jessie has seen the exhibition plans, knows what Delphine is building, and challenges whether this project represents genuine reckoning or just repackaging complicity in a more respectable form. "You're still telling people how to feel about things. That's what you do." Delphine defends the work - museums are different from content farms, curation can be critique - but feels the accusation's accuracy. Their marriage surfaces here: accumulated tensions about Delphine's ongoing need to shape narrative, Jessie's weariness with her self-examination.

## Key Elements

- The museum cafe: institutional food, other families, Theo absorbed in the tablet
- Jessie's critique: is this exhibition different from the content work Delphine renounced?
- Delphine's defense: museums create context, slow consideration, not virality
- The underlying marital tension: Jessie has watched years of Delphine's reckoning
- Specific reference to the viral video in the exhibition - Jessie saw how it was made
- The question of whether expertise in manipulation can be deployed for critique
- Theo's periodic interruptions breaking the adult conversation
- What Jessie's own work (TV writing) says about complicity and narrative
- The gap between what Delphine says and what she fears might be true
- No resolution - the confrontation surfaces the question without answering it

## Stylistic Notes

Carson-mode: spare dialogue, long silences, the marriage's accumulated tensions surfacing in fragments. Short exchanges with weight in the pauses. Delphine's interior responses not always matching her spoken words. The confrontation rendered through rhythm - quick volleys, then silence, then Theo's interruption resetting. Some Knausgaard expansion in Delphine's internal defenses, but the dominant mode is compression, ellipsis, what's not said louder than what is.
