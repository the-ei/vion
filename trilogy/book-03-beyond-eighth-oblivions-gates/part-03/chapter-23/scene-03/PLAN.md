# Scene Plan: Reckoning

## Summary

Father and son finally talk, in a quiet corner away from the conference networking. DeShawn explains what he's trying to build - accountability that doesn't depend on exposure but is embedded in systems. Jerome asks questions, genuine ones, not challenges. DeShawn admits that Jerome's work shaped him even as he rejected its methods. "I'm not trying to tell the truth to people. I'm trying to make it harder for systems to lie." That night, Jerome calls Denise, trying to articulate what shifted. The chapter ends with a tentative new understanding - not resolution, not agreement, but possibility where there had been only judgment.

## Key Elements

- The quiet corner: away from booths, a hallway, two chairs
- DeShawn's explanation: structural accountability versus exposure accountability
- Jerome's questions: genuine inquiry, not cross-examination
- The acknowledgment: DeShawn admitting Jerome's influence
- The key line: "I'm trying to make it harder for systems to lie"
- What this means for Jerome's legacy: transformed but not erased
- The limits of understanding: they still see the world differently
- The call to Denise that night: Jerome processing aloud
- Denise's perspective: she's watched both of them for years
- Ending on possibility, not resolution - the relationship changed, not healed

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (57-58):** POV character, learning to see his son differently
- **DeShawn Cole (22-23):** Explaining himself, acknowledging his father's influence
- **Denise Cole (phone):** Jerome's wife, offering perspective on both men
- **Kevin Zhou (referenced):** The other influence on DeShawn, acknowledged

- Then: Jerome's hotel room that night, the phone call
- Austin, the conference world still present outside

## Timeline

## Stylistic Notes

Blended mode: the father-son conversation in a mix of dialogue and interior response. Knausgaard expansion for the key moments of recognition - when DeShawn explains his methodology, when he acknowledges Jerome's influence. Carson compression for the call to Denise - shorter exchanges, the exhaustion of the day. The ending should feel tentative, not triumphant: something opened, not concluded. The relationship changed by a single conversation but not transformed entirely.
