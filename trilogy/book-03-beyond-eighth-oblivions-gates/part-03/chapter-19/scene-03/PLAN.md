# Scene Plan: Night Understanding

## Summary

Late evening. DeShawn has gone back to his hotel. Jerome sits alone with his mother, who sleeps fitfully. In the quiet of the institutional night, Jerome thinks about what he actually wanted to pass on - not the awards, not even the stories, but something harder to name. A belief in witness, in the obligation to see clearly. He wonders if DeShawn might carry this in his own way, transformed into something Jerome wouldn't recognize. The chapter ends with his mother waking briefly, recognizing Jerome fully for one moment, calling him by name, asking if he's still writing.

## Key Elements

- Jerome alone with his sleeping mother, the facility at night
- The argument with DeShawn replaying, Jerome finding the parts he couldn't answer
- His own father's judgment of his journalism career - the pattern repeating
- What he wanted to transmit: not truth-telling but truth-seeing
- The possibility that DeShawn absorbed it and transformed it beyond recognition
- His mother's sleep sounds, her fragility visible in rest
- The Pulitzer on the bedside table - its meaning shifting through the chapter
- Jerome's realization that legacy isn't received, it's taken and remade
- His mother's moment of clarity: waking, knowing him, asking the right question
- The tenderness of being seen fully, even briefly, by a fading witness

## Stylistic Notes

Blended mode: Knausgaard interiority for Jerome's recursive thinking about legacy, witness, transmission. The long paragraph of his thoughts about what truth-seeing means. Carson compression for the ending - his mother's waking, the exchange between them, spare and devastating. The chapter should end on a note of possibility, not resolution - his mother's recognition as gift, not guarantee. The moment of being seen is enough, and also not enough.
