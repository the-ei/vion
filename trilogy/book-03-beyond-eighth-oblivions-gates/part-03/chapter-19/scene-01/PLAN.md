# Scene Plan: The Visit

## Summary

Jerome Washington arrives at Morningside Memory Care in Baltimore with two banker's boxes of his journalism archive. He's come to show his mother his life's work, hoping the physical artifacts might trigger recognition that conversation no longer reliably can. The facility is familiar to him now - the same fluorescent light, the same antiseptic overlay on decay, the same aides who know him by name. His mother's responses to the clippings, photographs, and the framed Pulitzer certificate vary unpredictably: sometimes she knows exactly who he is and glows with pride, sometimes she thinks he's her late husband Arthur, sometimes she asks why this stranger is showing her these papers.

## Key Elements

- Jerome entering the facility with boxes - the weight of them, the ridiculousness of bringing paper artifacts
- The facility's texture: sounds from other rooms, the aide's greeting, the slow elevator
- His mother's room, how she's arranged today, her lucidity flickering
- Showing her specific pieces: his first byline, the financial crisis investigation, the Pulitzer
- Her responses - recognition, confusion, moments of clarity that cut through
- Denise in the room, watching Jerome's need and his mother's inability to reliably meet it
- Other patients glimpsed - the community of the fading
- Jerome's internal negotiation: knowing this is for him more than for her
- A moment where his mother calls him Arthur and tells him she's proud of their son

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (57):** POV character, bringing his archive to a failing witness
- **Jerome's mother (82):** Dementia advanced, her recognition a slot machine
- **Denise Cole (55):** Jerome's wife, grounding presence, watching both of them
- **Facility aides:** Familiar figures, part of the landscape

## Setting

- Morningside Memory Care, Baltimore
- His mother's room - single occupancy, window overlooking parking lot
- Spring 2039, late morning
- The facility as institution: schedules, meals, the rhythm of decline

## Timeline

- Day 1 of visit, late morning to late afternoon
- Present-time action spans approximately 5-6 hours
- Flashbacks to Jerome's career and his mother's role in supporting it

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard-mode: exhaustive attention to the facility environment, the physical objects of Jerome's archive, his mother's room and her body. Long sentences that accumulate institutional detail. The showing of the clippings becomes ritualistic, each one with its history. Dialogue emerges in pockets - his mother's responses unpredictable, Denise's observations spare. The scene should feel like ceremony, like witness requiring a witness, even when the witness can no longer hold what they see.
