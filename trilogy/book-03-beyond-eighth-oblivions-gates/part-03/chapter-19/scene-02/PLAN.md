# Scene Plan: The Argument

## Summary

DeShawn arrives the next morning, successful in his tech career, wearing clothes that cost more than Jerome's monthly budget. The visit to grandmother becomes a confrontation between father and son about legacy and relevance. DeShawn questions whether Jerome's truth-telling ever mattered - whether exposure changes systems or just provides catharsis for journalists. Jerome defends his work but hears himself sounding defensive, circular. His mother, present for part of this exchange, interjects with a comment that cuts through both their positions - a moment of devastating clarity from her dementia-fragmented mind.

## Key Elements

- DeShawn's arrival: the car he drove, the confidence he carries
- The generational tension visible in clothing, language, assumptions about the future
- DeShawn's argument: exposure doesn't change systems, it just makes journalists feel righteous
- Jerome's defense: witness matters even when change doesn't follow
- Kevin Zhou's influence on DeShawn mentioned - the mentorship that shaped his career
- The grandmother's interjection - something neither expected, a truth from the broken place
- Denise's role as witness, her own perspective on both men
- The facility as backdrop - other families visiting, the rhythms of institutional care
- What Jerome wanted to pass on versus what DeShawn actually received
- The Pulitzer's meaning contested - trophy or evidence?

## Stylistic Notes

Carson-mode dominates: fragmented dialogue, gaps where understanding fails. The argument rendered in quick exchanges with silences between. Jerome's interior responses sometimes don't match his spoken words. The grandmother's interjection breaks the pattern - a longer, Knausgaard passage for that moment of unexpected clarity. The tension between father and son conveyed through rhythm, through what's not said, through the physical space between them.
