# Scene Plan: Sorting

## Summary

Morning in the Phoenix house that Elena has inherited from her abuela. Elena and Sofia methodically work through abuela's bedroom, sorting belongings into categories: keep, donate, discard. The physical objects - clothes still carrying her scent, photographs with no one left to identify the faces, medical bills revealing debts Elena never knew about - become vessels for memory and meaning. Sofia asks questions that force Elena to articulate truths she's never put into words: about death, about what families keep, about why some things matter and others don't.

## Key Elements

- The physical texture of abuela's room: the cedar chest, the crucifix, the medication bottles
- Sofia's questions punctuating the work, each one requiring Elena to translate grief into language a ten-year-old can hold
- Unlabeled photographs from Mexico, faces no one can name anymore
- Medical bills stuffed in a drawer, revealing the scope of what abuela hid
- Elena discovering objects from her own childhood that abuela saved
- The scent of abuela on clothes, still present, making absence more acute
- Mateo drifting in and out, processing differently - quieter, more physical
- Daniel's absence (away for construction work) creating space but also silence
- Elena's internal cataloging of what her body remembers from this room

## Characters

- **Elena Varga (39):** POV character, navigating the archaeology of family
- **Sofia Varga (10):** Present and asking questions, forcing articulation
- **Mateo Varga (8):** In and out of the room, processing through movement
- **Elena's abuela (deceased):** Present through objects, scent, the shape of absence

## Setting

- Phoenix, Arizona
- The house Elena has inherited, specifically abuela's bedroom
- Early morning light, February 2039
- Three weeks after abuela's death

## Timeline

- Early February 2039
- Morning, approximately 7am to 12pm
- Five hours of present-time action with memory flashbacks

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard-mode dominates: exhaustive attention to objects and their weight, the physical labor of sorting as meditation, long sentences that accumulate detail. Elena's interiority expands around mundane actions. Sofia's questions arrive as interruptions that redirect attention, moments of dialogue surfacing in the flow of description. Some Carson compression when grief becomes too acute - shorter fragments, white space, pauses.
