# Scene Plan: The Letter

## Summary

Elena discovers a letter in abuela's handwriting, addressed to her but never sent - or perhaps never finished. The letter, found tucked inside a prayer book, speaks of Elena's mother, of the family's crossing into the United States, of sacrifices made and prices paid that Elena never knew. But the letter is incomplete, fragmentary - gaps and crossed-out passages that mirror the gaps in family knowledge. Sofia watches Elena read, wanting to know what it says, and Elena must decide what to share and what to protect her daughter from.

## Key Elements

- The discovery: the letter tucked in abuela's prayer book, envelope yellowed
- The handwriting - shaky in places, firm in others, spanning years of composition
- Content fragments: references to Elena's mother's choices, the crossing, debts owed
- What's crossed out and illegible, the deliberate erasures
- Elena's physical reaction to reading - hands shaking, breath catching
- Sofia's persistence: "What does it say, Mom?"
- The decision of how much to translate for a child
- A revelation about Elena's mother that reframes Elena's understanding
- The letter's incompleteness as metaphor - what can never be fully transmitted
- Elena reading aloud some passages, keeping others silent

## Characters

- **Elena Varga (39):** POV character, receiving words from beyond death
- **Sofia Varga (10):** Witness to her mother's reaction, wanting access to the story
- **Elena's abuela (through the letter):** Speaking across time, incomplete
- **Elena's mother (referenced):** A figure in the letter, revealed differently

## Setting

- Same room, afternoon light now
- The letter as focal object
- The prayer book (San Judas Tadeo on the cover)
- Elena seated on abuela's bed, Sofia beside her

## Timeline

- Same day, afternoon (around 2pm)
- The reading spans perhaps 30 minutes but dilates in Elena's experience
- The letter itself references events from decades prior

## Page Target

7 pages (pages 8-14 of chapter)

## Stylistic Notes

Carson-mode dominates: the letter's fragments reproduced with white space, gaps visible on the page. Elena's reading interrupted by her own reactions, by Sofia's questions. Shorter paragraphs, more dialogue. The fragmentary nature of the letter reflected in the prose structure. Some Knausgaard expansion when Elena flashes back to memories the letter evokes, but the overall mode is compression, ellipsis, the unsaid hovering. The letter text itself formatted differently - perhaps italicized or offset.
