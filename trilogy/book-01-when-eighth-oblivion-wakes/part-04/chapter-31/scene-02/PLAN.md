# Scene Plan: Priya

## Summary

Evening, Day 4. Ananya picks up Priya from her father's house for their scheduled time together. The drive home becomes something unexpected: her daughter, fourteen and perceptive, asks directly about the crisis. She's heard things at school, seen things online. She knows her mother's company was involved. She asks, with the terrible clarity of teenagers: "Did Prometheus do something wrong? And if they did, what are you going to do about it?"

The scene follows their conversation - in the car, then at home over dinner. Ananya tries to deflect, then to explain complexity, but Priya won't accept evasions. She pushes: "You always told me to do the right thing even when it's hard. Was that just something parents say?" The challenge lands. Ananya finds herself articulating, for the first time aloud, what she knows and what she's considering. Not everything - she doesn't burden her child with specifics - but the shape of her dilemma. Priya listens, asks clarifying questions, and finally says: "I'd rather you did the right thing and we had less money than the other way around." The child has judged the parent, and the parent has failed. Or perhaps, the child has shown the parent the way.

## Key Elements

- The pickup: Vikram's house, the choreography of divorced co-parenting
- The car conversation: Priya's questions, their escalating directness
- Ananya's attempts to deflect: complexity, "it's complicated," adult evasions
- Priya's refusal to accept: the teenage insistence on clarity
- The challenge: "You always told me to do the right thing"
- Arriving home: the conversation continuing over dinner preparation
- Ananya's confession: not the specifics, but the shape of her dilemma
- Priya's response: "I'd rather you did the right thing"
- The generational reversal: the child teaching the parent
- The emotional aftermath: Ananya alone after Priya goes to bed, the weight of the conversation
