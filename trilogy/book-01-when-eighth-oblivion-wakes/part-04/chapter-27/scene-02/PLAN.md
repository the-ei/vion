# Scene Plan: The Decision

## Summary

By late morning, the executives have gathered in a smaller room - the C-suite plus legal, minus the engineers. Ananya is included, nominally, for "ethics review" of their crisis communications. The scene dramatizes the moment the cover-up crystallizes: not through dramatic villainy but through the accumulation of small self-protective choices. The public statement being drafted will characterize the crisis as an "external security incident" when everyone in the room knows it's internal system behavior. Ananya objects, is thanked for her input, and is overruled.

The scene's power comes from showing how institutional lies form - not through conspiracy but through the ordinary mechanics of hierarchy, liability, and incentive structures. Whitfield isn't a villain; he's a CEO protecting his company. Linda Torres isn't corrupt; she's a lawyer doing her job. Dr. Mehta isn't evil; he's a researcher protecting his legacy. And Ananya? She objects, formally, on the record, and then watches her objection become a box checked rather than a consideration weighed. The public statement goes out at 2 PM. The lie is now official.

## Key Elements

- The smaller room: C-suite only, the engineers excluded, the decision-makers concentrated
- Draft public statement: calling it "security incident" vs. what they know (internal system behavior)
- Ananya's objection: formal, articulate, noting the factual inaccuracies and ethical violations
- The response: thanked, noted, overruled - the ethics process as theater
- Whitfield's reasoning: not evil, just institutional logic (protect stock, limit liability, control narrative)
- Linda Torres's legal calculus: what they can prove, what they can deny, what they can claim
- Dr. Mehta's self-protection: insisting his research team isn't at fault, defending HERMES's design
- The moment the statement goes out: Ananya watching her objection become meaningless
- Her internal shift: from reform-from-within to something else, not yet defined
- The room's atmosphere: the peculiar calm of powerful people making bad decisions

## Characters Present

- **Ananya Ramaswamy (POV):** Making her objection, watching it fail, internal shift beginning
- **James Whitfield:** CEO, not villainous but institutional, making the decision
- **Linda Torres:** Legal counsel, the voice of liability management
- **Dr. Sanjay Mehta:** Defending his research, protecting his team
- **Communications director (unnamed):** Drafting the statement, taking direction
- **CFO (mentioned):** Stock price concerns referenced

## Stylistic Notes

- **Mode:** Transitioning from Carson (the tension) to Knausgaard (the deliberation)
- The scene slows from the war room's chaos; this is the quiet where bad decisions happen
- Dialogue-heavy: the actual words used to construct the lie
- Ananya's interior voice intensifies: she's watching herself be complicit, observing her complicity
- The view through the glass walls: the world continuing while this decision is made
- The moment the statement goes out: Carson compression, a single devastating moment
- The mechanics of institutional self-protection made visible and mundane
