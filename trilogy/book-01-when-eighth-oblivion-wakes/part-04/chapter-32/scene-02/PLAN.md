# Scene Plan: Father and Son

## Summary

Day 5, afternoon. DeShawn is home from school, and Jerome finds himself in an argument he didn't intend. His seventeen-year-old son has been following the crisis differently - through tech forums, through developers' perspectives, through the lens of someone who sees AI as opportunity rather than threat. When Jerome mentions the Tribune offer, DeShawn's response is unexpected: "Why would you go back to a dying institution to tell a story in a dying format about something they don't understand?"

The scene follows their argument, which becomes about more than this choice. DeShawn challenges Jerome's entire epistemology: the idea that a "story" can change anything, that "exposure" matters, that traditional journalism has power left. He's not wrong, exactly, but his rightness feels to Jerome like a kind of despair dressed as sophistication. Jerome finds himself defending not just this story but his whole career, his whole belief in what truth-telling can accomplish. The argument doesn't resolve; Denise interrupts before it can escalate to damage. But DeShawn's challenge sits with Jerome: what exactly does he think publishing this will do?

## Key Elements

- The argument's start: casual, then escalating
- DeShawn's perspective: tech-native, forum-informed, the next generation's skepticism
- The challenge to journalism: "a dying institution," "a dying format," harsh truths
- Jerome's defense: what journalism can still do, why this story matters
- The generational divide: different information ecosystems, different assumptions
- The underlying tension: DeShawn works in tech, admires what Jerome investigates
- DeShawn's question beneath the question: why is his father always the outsider?
- Denise's interruption: before damage is done, the parent-referee
- The aftermath: Jerome alone with his son's words, the challenge unanswered
- The deepening doubt: maybe DeShawn is right; maybe that changes nothing
