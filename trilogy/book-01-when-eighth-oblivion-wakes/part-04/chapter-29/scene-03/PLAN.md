# Scene Plan: The Death

## Summary

Early evening. Systems have been partially restored, but the damage is done. Elena is treating Halima Hassan, an older Somali woman with diabetes and heart complications, whose condition has deteriorated because her continuous glucose monitor was offline for twelve hours. The scene traces Elena's attempt to stabilize her, the cascade of complications, and the ultimate failure. Halima dies - not dramatically, not peacefully, but in the ordinary awful way that preventable deaths happen in under-resourced hospitals. The AI system that should have flagged her condition hours ago is back online now, blinking green, oblivious.

The death is the chapter's turning point. Elena has seen patients die before - it's part of the job - but this death is different. This patient died because a system designed to help her failed, and because the system's failures were probably known and accepted somewhere in a conference room. Elena's professional anger becomes personal fury. She looks at Halima's body and knows that this woman's death is evidence. Someone should be held accountable. And if no one else will document it, Elena will.

## Key Elements

- Halima Hassan: her arrival, her history, her condition worsening
- The treatment attempt: Elena working to stabilize her, the medications, the interventions
- The cascade: how diabetes complications compound without proper monitoring
- The failure: Halima's heart gives out, resuscitation attempts fail
- The moment of death: neither dramatic nor peaceful, just terrible
- The AI system: back online now, showing green status, a bitter irony
- Elena's reaction: professional composure, internal fury
- The death notification: contacting family (Yusuf, though Elena doesn't know his name yet)
- The aftermath: paperwork, the body, the routine of death in hospitals
- Elena's decision crystallizing: this death is evidence, someone must document it
- Daniel's call: her husband checking in, Elena barely able to speak

## Characters Present

- **Elena Varga (POV):** Treating the patient, witnessing the death, fury crystallizing
- **Halima Hassan:** The patient, dying, a human being with a specific history
- **Miguel Santos:** Assisting Elena, affected by the death
- **Dr. Patricia Okonkwo:** Making the call, confirming death, sharing Elena's anger
- **Daniel Varga (via phone):** Brief call after, Elena's voice breaking
- **Hassan family (via notification):** Yusuf or Amina reached by phone, the terrible news delivered

- **Atmosphere:** The specific atmosphere of medical emergency, then the particular quiet after death

## Timeline

## Stylistic Notes

- **Mode:** Carson dominant for the medical emergency - clipped, sensory, urgent; Knausgaard for the aftermath
- Medical procedure rendered in real time: the specific interventions, the specific failures
- Halima as person, not case: her face, her hands, her responses, her stillness
- The death: neither melodramatic nor clinical, the ordinary awfulness of it
- The AI system's green light: a single image that carries enormous weight
- Elena's professionalism: she continues functioning, but we see the cracks
- The phone calls: the death notification as routine made terrible; Daniel's call as personal intrusion
- The body: how a person becomes a body, the specific moment
