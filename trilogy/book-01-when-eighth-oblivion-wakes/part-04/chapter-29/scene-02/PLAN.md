# Scene Plan: The Interview

## Summary

Jerome Washington's interview request arrives while Elena is between patients. She's exhausted, skeptical of journalists, and has no time for this - but something in his message is different. He's asking about specific cases, not seeking soundbites about "the crisis." He mentions seeing her earlier posts about AI diagnostic problems, before the crisis. He seems to understand that this is about patients, not technology. Elena takes the call in the break room, sitting on a worn couch surrounded by vending machines and old magazines, while Jerome appears on her screen from a coffee shop across the country.

The scene shows the interview from Elena's side - her wariness gradually shifting as Jerome proves he's listening, not just extracting quotes. She describes what she's seeing: the specific patients, the specific failures, the gap between what the systems promised and what they delivered. For the first time, she's articulating something she's felt for years: that the technology was never about helping patients, it was about efficiency metrics that looked like caring. Jerome asks follow-up questions that show he understands. By scene's end, she's agreed to stay in touch, to document what she can, to be part of a record that might matter.

## Key Elements

- The message arrival: Jerome's interview request, specific enough to interest her
- Elena's wariness: journalists have burned her before, used her words out of context
- The break room: the worn couch, the vending machines, the space between patients
- The video call: Jerome on screen, clearly also exhausted, clearly also committed
- Elena's testimony: the cases she's seen today, the patterns she's recognized
- The deeper truth: technology as efficiency theater, not genuine care improvement
- Jerome's questions: specific, informed, focused on people
- The mutual recognition: two people who take their work seriously finding each other
- Her request: if he writes this, center the patients, not the technology
- The agreement: she'll document, she'll stay in touch, this conversation might matter
- The return to work: the interview ends, patients still waiting

## Characters Present

- **Elena Varga (POV):** Giving the interview, finding her voice as witness
- **Jerome Washington (via video):** Conducting the interview, asking the right questions
- **Other staff (peripheral):** Glimpsed through the break room door, the clinic still in crisis
- **The patients (mentioned):** Their cases are Elena's evidence

## Setting

- **Location:** Break room at the community health center - worn furniture, vending machines, fluorescent lights
- **Time of Day:** 2:00 PM to 3:30 PM (Arizona time); Jerome is calling from Baltimore/DC
- **Atmosphere:** The exhausted calm of a break room during crisis, the strange intimacy of video calls with strangers

## Timeline

- Duration: Approximately 1.5 hours including the initial message and the call itself
- Mid-afternoon of the crisis day

## Page Target

5 pages (~1,375 words)

## Stylistic Notes

- **Mode:** Balanced - Knausgaard for the depth of conversation, Carson for the medical details
- Dialogue-heavy: the interview as the scene's engine
- The video screen: the technological frame through which connection happens
- Elena finding her voice: she's articulating things she's known but never said
- Jerome as listener: his responses minimal but precise, drawing her out
- The break room details: the small sadnesses of underfunded healthcare spaces
- The agreement: not triumphant, just practical - two tired people choosing to work together
- The interruption: the break must end, patients are waiting
