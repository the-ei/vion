The app's voice had been giving directions for thirty minutes when it started to malfunction.

"In three hundred feet, turn right onto - turn right onto - " The voice stuttered, repeated itself, then fell silent.

Yusuf looked at his phone, mounted on the dashboard of his ten-year-old Civic. The screen showed the map frozen, the route line flickering, the delivery address disappearing and reappearing as if the system couldn't decide whether it existed.

"Come on," he said. "Not now."

Three customers were waiting for these groceries. He had the bags organized in his back seat with the precision that three years of algorithmic training had instilled - Mrs. Patterson's organic produce, carefully separated from anything that might bruise it; the Singh family's bulk rice and lentils, double-bagged for weight; a single person's worth of frozen meals for an address in Uptown, tucked against the cooler bag's ice packs. Each delivery meant points toward his hourly bonus. Each delay meant falling behind the algorithm's expectations, and the algorithm never forgot, never forgave, never failed to adjust his future opportunities based on today's performance.

The screen flickered one more time and went dark.

Yusuf pulled over to the curb and tried to restart the app. The loading wheel spun endlessly. He force-closed it, reopened it, watched the same wheel spin the same direction toward the same nowhere.

His phone buzzed with a notification from a different app - his news feed, running in the background.

"MAJOR TECH OUTAGES REPORTED NATIONWIDE - DEVELOPING STORY"

He opened the feed and scrolled through fragments: AI systems failing, healthcare disruptions, logistics networks down across the country. Prometheus Systems - the name sounded familiar, something from the news, one of those tech companies that made the invisible infrastructure everyone depended on without knowing they were depending on anything at all. The kind of company whose products disappeared into the background of life until the moment they stopped working.

The groceries in his back seat were becoming a problem.
