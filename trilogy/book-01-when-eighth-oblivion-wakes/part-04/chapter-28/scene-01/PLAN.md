# Scene Plan: Breakfast with Mother

## Summary

Jerome is at his mother Dorothy's house in Baltimore, making breakfast, when the news breaks. The scene opens with the mundane textures of caregiving - finding the eggs, her confusion about the day of the week, the patient repetition of morning routines - before his phone begins its cascade of alerts. The juxtaposition is deliberate: the intimacy of caring for someone with dementia against the abstraction of technological crisis. Dorothy doesn't understand what's happening, asks why he keeps looking at his phone, and Jerome must choose between being a son and being a journalist.

The scene establishes Jerome's split loyalties and the particular clarity his mother's disconnection provides. While the world panics, Dorothy asks if he'd like more coffee. While financial systems tremble, she tells him a story about his father that she's told a hundred times. The crisis feels both urgent and absurd from her kitchen table. By scene's end, Jerome has kissed his mother goodbye, promised to return soon, and is already on the phone with his first source, the journalist emerging from the caregiver.

## Key Elements

- The morning routine: making breakfast, Dorothy's confusion, the weight of caregiving
- Dorothy Cole: her dementia, her lucid moments, her stories about Jerome's father
- The phone cascade: alerts beginning, Jerome checking, then checking again
- Initial reports: fragments of the crisis, conflicting information, the fog of breaking news
- The split attention: being present with his mother vs. the pull of the story
- Dorothy's questions: "What's wrong with the phone?" - her confusion as perspective
- The decision to leave: guilt, duty, the journalist winning over the son
- His sister Lorraine (mentioned): she'll check on Dorothy, the family coverage arranged
- First source call: made while leaving the house, the work beginning
- The strange peace: Dorothy's kitchen as refuge from catastrophe
