# Scene Plan: Information Warfare

## Summary

Jerome has left his mother's house and is now in full journalist mode, but the information environment is a nightmare. The scene tracks him through mid-day as he tries to triangulate truth from a chaos of sources: official statements that contradict each other, anonymous tips that could be genuine or planted, social media awash with speculation, and the "Eighth Oblivion" concept suddenly trending as people seek explanatory frameworks. His investigation from Parts 2-3 gave him context, but the real-time crisis is moving faster than verification allows.

The scene dramatizes what it means to practice journalism in 2033. Jerome is on calls with sources who can't go on record, scanning social media for patterns, cross-referencing official statements with leaked documents, all while his independent platform gives him freedom but no institutional protection. His son DeShawn texts, excited by the tech implications; Jerome finds his son's enthusiasm disturbing. His wife Denise calls from DC, reporting school closures and neighborhood chaos. The outside world keeps intruding on his attempt to find the story.

## Key Elements

- The information cascade: multiple sources, contradictory claims, the fog of real-time crisis
- Jerome's methodology: how he verifies in 2033, the tools and their limits
- Source cultivation: unnamed contacts, the ethics of anonymity, the risk of manipulation
- Social media landscape: the "Eighth Oblivion" trending, competing narratives, viral misinformation
- DeShawn's texts: his son's tech-native perspective, seeing opportunity where Jerome sees threat
- Denise's call: school closures, neighborhood impact, the practical chaos at home
- The AI systems: Jerome realizing his investigation from earlier parts connects to this crisis
- Official statements: Prometheus's lie (from Chapter 27), other companies' denials
- The specific misinformation: at least one false narrative Jerome must track and debunk
- The journalist's frustration: truth fragmenting faster than it can be assembled

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** In journalist mode, fighting the information environment
- **DeShawn Cole (son, via text):** Seventeen, excited about the tech implications
- **Denise Cole (wife, via call):** Managing home chaos, reporting from DC
- **Sources (various, via phone/message):** Fragments of information, reliability uncertain
- **Social media voices:** The chorus of the crisis, named accounts and anonymous speculation

## Setting

- **Location:** Initially in transit (car, then train station), then settling in a Baltimore coffee shop to work
- **Time of Day:** 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM
- **Atmosphere:** The portable office of a modern journalist - laptop, phone, headphones, the noise of public spaces; the displacement of working without an institution

- **Mode:** Carson dominant - fragmented, rapid, overwhelming

- Screen time: actual texts, tweets, headlines woven into the prose
- Jerome's professional voice: skeptical, methodical, fighting chaos with process

- The marriage as anchor: Denise's call brings practical reality, grounds him
- Physical displacement: working in transit, no home base, the precarity of independent journalism
- Information overload rendered viscerally: the assault on attention
