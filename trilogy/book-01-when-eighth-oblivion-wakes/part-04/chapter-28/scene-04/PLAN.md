# Scene Plan: The Decision

## Summary

Evening. Jerome is on the train from Baltimore to DC, the familiar route between his mother and his life. The day's accumulation weighs on him: the information chaos, Elena's testimony, his sources' fragments. He knows the story's shape now - AI systems failing in ways that hurt people, companies likely covering up what they knew - but he needs inside confirmation. He has a contact at Prometheus, someone he's cultivated over his investigation: Ananya Ramaswamy, the Chief Ethics Officer who always seemed troubled by her own company.

The scene follows Jerome's decision to reach out to Ananya directly. It's a risk: she might refuse, might report him, might be loyal to her employer. But Elena's testimony has made clear the human stakes, and Jerome can't write "sources say" without the inside view. He drafts and redrafts a message - too aggressive, too vague, too revealing of what he knows. Finally, he sends something that acknowledges they need to talk without specifying about what. The train arrives in DC as his phone shows Ananya's read receipt. Then, just as he steps onto the platform, her reply arrives. The chapter ends with Jerome reading her brief message, knowing the story is possible now.

## Key Elements

- The train journey: the liminal space between Baltimore (mother, past) and DC (family, work, future)
- The day's synthesis: what Jerome has learned, what he still needs
- The decision to contact Ananya: the risk, the necessity
- Drafting the message: multiple attempts, each revealing different approaches
- What he knows about Ananya: her public profile, her reputation for integrity, the tension in her role
- The send: the commitment made, the irreversibility
- The wait: the train moving, time passing, the read receipt
- The reply: brief but unmistakable - she's willing to talk
- The DC platform: stepping off, phone in hand, the story crystallizing
- The "Eighth Oblivion" still trending: the public discourse Jerome is about to enter
