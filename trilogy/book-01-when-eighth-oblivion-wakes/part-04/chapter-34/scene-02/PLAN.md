# Scene Plan: Family

## Summary

After the initial shock, Yusuf and Amina sit down to process together. They talk about their mother - not the case, but the person. Halima Hassan: what she loved, what she worried about, how she prayed, what she said to them. The conversation becomes a kind of private memorial, a reclaiming of the person from the evidence.

But Amina's perspective surprises Yusuf. At sixteen, she's more digitally native than he is, and she sees something he initially missed: their mother's story, made public, might actually help other people. The same systems that killed Halima are still running; other families will face what they faced. If Halima's case becomes part of why that changes, isn't that meaningful? Amina isn't dismissing Yusuf's rage - she shares it - but she's also thinking strategically. What if, instead of being victims of this story, they became participants in it? What if Yusuf spoke, not as a grieving son (though he is), but as someone who understands how these systems work from below?

## Key Elements

- The sibling conversation: reclaiming Halima as person, not case
- Memories of Halima: specific, loving, the mother they knew
- Her death: how they experienced it, the hospital, the aftermath
- Amina's perspective: the strategic view, how the documentation might help others
- Her digital fluency: she understands the discourse in ways Yusuf doesn't
- Her argument: victim vs. participant, silence vs. voice
- Yusuf's resistance: he doesn't want to perform his grief
- Amina's counter: it's not performing, it's testifying
- The tension: valid points on both sides, no easy resolution
- The direction forming: Yusuf considering that he might have something to say

## Characters Present

- **Amina Hassan:** The younger sister with the strategic mind
- **Halima Hassan (in memory):** The mother, reclaimed from the documentation
