# Scene Plan: Signals

## Summary

Multiple threads interwoven across several days. First: the video call with Kevin Zhou's parents in Shenzhen. The connection is poor - whether for technical reasons or political censorship is ambiguous. They speak carefully, avoiding topics that might draw algorithmic attention. His mother mentions that travel restrictions are tightening; the implication is that visits in either direction may become difficult or impossible. His father asks about work in a way that suggests awareness of surveillance. The call is fifteen minutes of love expressed through what can't be said. After hanging up, Kevin Zhou sits with the particular loneliness of chosen distance.

Second: an evening in the gaming community where Kevin Zhou is "Wei_37." The usual raid, the usual banter, the social space where Kevin Zhou is most fluent. But Atlas - a player he's known online for two years but never met - is asking pointed questions. What company does Kevin Zhou work for? What specifically does he build? The questions could be innocent curiosity or could be probing. Kevin Zhou deflects, makes jokes, but the conversation unsettles. Who is Atlas? What does the question mean?

Third: the first date with Sara, who matched with him on an app. She's a graphic designer who works remotely, lives in the Mission, has interests that overlap with Kevin Zhou's non-work life (gaming, speculative fiction, arguments about technology). Their conversation surprises him - she asks questions that aren't small talk, disagrees with him thoughtfully, doesn't seem impressed by his job title. He likes her. This is unfamiliar. They make plans for a second date.

## Key Elements

- The parents' call: poor connection, careful words, love beneath
- Political danger in China: implied, not explicit
- The loneliness of chosen distance: what Kevin Zhou has traded
- The gaming session: the social space, the familiar fluency
- Atlas's questions: innocent or probing, uncertainty
- The deflection: Kevin Zhou not wanting to answer
- The date with Sara: unexpected genuine connection
- Sara's questions: substantive, challenging, interesting
- The second date planned: something new, maybe

## Stylistic Notes

**Mode:** Shifting between registers - Carson-like compression for the parents' call, Knausgaard flow for gaming, something more immediate for the date

**Sentence rhythm:** Halting during the video call; fluid in the game; searching during the date

**Key technique:** Three modes of connection: family (constrained), community (fluid but limited), romantic (uncertain but promising)

**Emotional register:** Loss with parents, comfort in gaming, surprise with Sara
