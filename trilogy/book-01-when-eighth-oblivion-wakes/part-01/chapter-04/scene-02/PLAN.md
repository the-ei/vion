# Scene Plan: The Home

## Summary

Evening at Delphine and Jessie's home in Silver Lake. Theo, four years old, runs to greet Delphine at the door - the physical specificity of a small child's affection cutting through everything complicated. The evening unfolds in the rhythms of family with a young child: dinner preparation as negotiation, the TV playing something Theo likes while the parents half-watch and half-talk, the bath time and bedtime rituals that mark the day's end.

Jessie, a TV writer, shares the weight of her own workday. Her show has been renewed but with compromises: a new showrunner who wants changes the original writers resist, a character arc that troubles Jessie deeply. Their conversation moves through layers: venting, defending, acknowledging the similar contradictions they both navigate. "We tell ourselves stories about what we're making," Jessie says at one point. "I don't know if the stories help or hurt." They're mirror images, this couple, both skilled at work they're not certain serves good ends.

The scene builds to Theo's bedtime. Delphine sits with him in the dark after the story and the songs, feeling his small presence, his questions that pierce through irony: "Why do people take videos?" Her answer is easier than the truth. She stays until he sleeps, the room dark, her professional self temporarily held in suspension by something simpler and realer.

## Key Elements

- Theo's greeting: the physical reality of a four-year-old's love
- Evening routines: dinner, TV, the choreography of family time
- Jessie's work troubles: the show renewal, the compromises required
- The couple's mutual recognition: both skilled at questionable work
- The conversation about complicity: "We tell ourselves stories..."
- Bath time and bedtime rituals: the specific details of parenting
- Theo's question: "Why do people take videos?" - the child's X-ray vision
- Delphine alone with sleeping Theo: the room dark, the thoughts circling

## Stylistic Notes

**Mode:** Knausgaard - the texture of parenting, the specific weight of family

**Sentence rhythm:** Shorter during action with Theo (his energy shaping the prose); longer during adult conversation; slowing toward stillness at bedtime

**Key technique:** Theo as specific person, not symbolic device - his presence matters because he's real

**Dialogue approach:** The couple's conversation as genuine exchange, not position-taking

**Emotional register:** Love and exhaustion, refuge and incompleteness
