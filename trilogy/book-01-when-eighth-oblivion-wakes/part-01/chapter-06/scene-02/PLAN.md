# Scene Plan: Two Conversations

## Summary

Two parallel conversations, intercut. The first: Jerome attempts to talk to DeShawn about the Prometheus interview, trying to explain his concerns without dismissing his son's aspirations. The conversation goes wrong almost immediately. Jerome sounds paranoid, paternal, out of touch. His concerns about surveillance capitalism, about the ethics of AI development, about what it means to build tools for prediction and control - all of it comes out as Dad being Dad, fighting battles DeShawn doesn't recognize as relevant.

DeShawn's rebuttals are sharp and partially right: the tech industry isn't what it was in Jerome's formative years; many companies are doing important work; criticism from the outside is easier than change from within. He mentions people his age who are using their skills to address problems - climate, healthcare, access. The argument doesn't resolve; it escalates into territory both regret, and DeShawn retreats to his room with the particular finality of a teenager ending a conversation.

The second conversation: Jerome and Denise, after DeShawn's door slams. Denise is exhausted - teaching is brutal this year, her students more anxious and distracted than she's ever seen, the infrastructure of public education crumbling around her. She doesn't have the energy to be neutral. "You're not wrong about the industry," she says. "You're wrong about your son." Jerome doesn't know how to hear this. They go to bed in silence, the familiar geography of marriage after an unresolved fight.

## Key Elements

- The DeShawn conversation: Jerome's concerns, their poor delivery
- DeShawn's rebuttals: sharp, contemporary, partially valid
- The generational divide: not about facts but about frame
- The escalation: the moment it goes too far
- DeShawn's retreat: the door, the silence
- The Denise conversation: her exhaustion, her clarity
- Her teaching burdens: the context she carries
- Her observation: "You're wrong about your son"
- The silent retreat to bed: unresolved, geographic
