# Scene Plan: The Visit

## Summary

Chicago. Jerome's mother in the assisted living facility - a good facility, expensive, the cost split with Yvonne who earns less but lives closer and visits more often. The scene follows Jerome through the visit: the arrival at the facility with its curated homelikeness, the walk to his mother's room, the uncertain minutes before recognition settles on her face.

Her condition has good days and bad; today is in between. She knows him, mostly. Their conversation drifts through time - she talks about his father (dead fifteen years), about Jerome's childhood (moments she remembers accurately, others revised by the disease), about the house in Baltimore she still believes she lives in. A lucid interval comes unexpectedly: she asks about his work, about DeShawn, about whether he's happy. Jerome doesn't know how to answer. The question hangs as she drifts back toward confusion.

Sister Yvonne arrives; the siblings navigate their dynamics - the guilt Jerome carries for living far away, the weight Yvonne carries for being the local one, the love that coexists with the resentment neither will name. They share a meal at a restaurant their mother used to take them to as children; now she can't follow the menu, orders by pointing at pictures. The weight of watching a parent diminish. The flight home, Jerome looking down at Chicago receding, feeling something shift in him - not resolution, but a softening that the argument with DeShawn had hardened.

## Key Elements

- The assisted living facility: its designed comfort, its underlying purpose
- Jerome's mother: the recognition, the drift, the lucid moments

- The question: "Are you happy?" - unanswerable, essential
- Yvonne's arrival: sibling dynamics, shared burden, unspoken grievances
- The restaurant meal: the childhood place, the changed mother
- The flight home: geographic and emotional return
