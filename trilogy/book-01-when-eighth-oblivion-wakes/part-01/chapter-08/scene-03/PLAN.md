# Scene Plan: The Return

## Summary

London for her mother's seventieth birthday. The scene begins with the flight - the transatlantic distance, the time zone shift, the crossing from one self to another. Delphine's British self emerges as she lands: the accent shifting back to its origins, the references her LA friends wouldn't catch, the person she was before she became who she is.

The birthday gathering at her mother's Brixton apartment. Family and old friends, the faces that knew her at seventeen, at twenty-two, at the wedding, at the funeral two years ago. The celebration is warm but haunted: her father's absence fills the spaces between people. His chair is still in the living room; no one sits in it. Her mother is doing well - active, social, aging with grace that Delphine admires - but the birthday dinner is shadowed by the person who should be there.

After the party, Delphine alone in her childhood bedroom (now a guest room, remade but recognizable). Old questions surface: Why did she move so far away? What kind of daughter lives an ocean from her aging mother? The room holds the ghost of who she was - the books she read, the posters she chose, the person before Los Angeles and content and creative direction. The trip ends with an airport goodbye, her mother's face visible through the glass, the particular pain of chosen distance. She returns to LA changed in ways she can't yet name.

## Key Elements

- The flight: crossing distance, crossing selves
- The British self: accent, references, the earlier version
- The birthday gathering: family, friends, celebration
- The father's absence: the chair, the shadow, the grief
- Her mother at seventy: grace, resilience, loneliness
- The childhood bedroom: ghosts of earlier self
- The airport goodbye: the mother's face, the glass, the distance
- The return changed: something shifted, not yet named
