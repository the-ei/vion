# Scene Plan: The Interface

## Summary

Saturday. Kevin Zhou's attempt at normal life beyond the screen. A dating app match - a woman in marketing whose profile suggested shared interests - leads to drinks at a bar in Hayes Valley that tries too hard to seem effortless. The conversation is friendly enough but reveals the gaps in Kevin Zhou's social toolkit. His work is classified enough to be vague about; his personal life is sparse enough to be embarrassing. She asks what he does for fun; he realizes he doesn't have an answer. The date ends early, mutual relief disguised as busy schedules.

Home to his apartment, to the gaming community where he's "Wei_37" - a handle, a character, a version of himself with different rules. The scene shifts as Kevin Zhou logs in, joining a raid with people he's known online for years but never met. In the game, he's confident, social, funny even - a leader whose strategic calls are respected, whose banter flows naturally. The contrast with the failed date is stark but not moralized. The scene shows him more alive in the virtual space than the physical one, but also shows the limits: when he logs off, the apartment is still empty, the city outside still indifferent, the loneliness still real.

## Key Elements

- The dating app as interface: the profile, the match, the algorithm of connection
- The bar date: the small talk that reveals the big gaps
- Kevin Zhou's social limitations: not performed, simply true
- The mutual relief of ending: neither cruel nor kind
- The gaming community: "Wei_37" as alternate identity
- The raid as social space: strategy, banter, belonging
- The contrast without judgment: where Kevin Zhou is alive, and what's missing
- The log-off: the apartment unchanged, the loneliness acknowledged
