# Scene Plan: The Build

## Summary

Kevin Zhou alone in his San Francisco apartment at 2 AM, surrounded by the blue light of multiple monitors, the city dark and invisible behind him. He's deep in flow state, debugging a component of Clarity - the AI system designed to help users understand why they make the choices they make. The code is elegant; he takes genuine pride in its architecture. This is the scene that establishes Kevin Zhou's relationship to work: not a job but an identity, the organizing principle of a life otherwise lacking structure.

The scene follows him through several hours of coding, interspersed with the micro-routines of deep work: the energy drink from the refrigerator, the stretches to relieve a back that's been curved over a keyboard for too long, the brief checks of Slack that reveal his colleagues are also awake, also building. As he works, his thoughts drift toward the system's implications - the data Clarity requires, the predictions it makes possible, the gap between "helping users understand themselves" and "modeling users for other purposes" - but these thoughts are routed to background processes, acknowledged and contained. He works until 4 AM, orders food through an app at dawn, sleeps a few hours. The scene establishes his devotion and its cost: the work is everything, and everything else is absence.

## Key Elements

- The 2 AM coding session as Kevin Zhou's natural habitat
- The apartment as functional space: optimized for productivity, empty of personality
- Clarity's codebase: specific enough to feel real, accessible enough to be universal
- The flow state as refuge: where Kevin Zhou is most himself
- Brief acknowledgments of other late-night coders: the community of the devoted
- The drifting thoughts about implications, quickly contained
- The dawn routine: food ordered without thought, sleep that isn't rest

## Characters Present

- **Kevin Zhou** (POV): The engineer in his element, alone in the way he prefers
- **Colleagues** (via Slack): The names that appear in chat, the validation of shared obsession
- **Clarity** (as presence): The code itself, Kevin Zhou's creation and expression

## Setting

**Location:** Kevin Zhou's apartment in San Francisco - a one-bedroom in SoMa, functional and sparse

**Time of Day:** 2:00 AM - 7:00 AM

**Physical Details:**
- Three monitors arranged for maximum workspace
- Standing desk in sitting position, the ergonomic chair he spent too much on
- Energy drinks in the refrigerator, takeout containers from previous nights
- The city outside the window, invisible except for scattered lights
- No personal decorations, no photos, no evidence of life beyond work
- The bed visible through the open bedroom door, unmade

## Stylistic Notes

**Mode:** Knausgaard - the texture of technical work, the specific satisfactions of code

**Sentence rhythm:** Long, flowing sentences that mirror the experience of flow state; technical language embedded naturally

**Key technique:** The coding process made visible without being pedantic; we understand what Kevin Zhou is doing through his consciousness of it

**Interiority level:** Deep but defended - we're inside Kevin Zhou's head, but his head has learned not to dwell

**Atmosphere:** The particular silence of late-night work; the city absent; the screen as world
