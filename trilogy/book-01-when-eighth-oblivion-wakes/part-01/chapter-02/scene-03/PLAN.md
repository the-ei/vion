# Scene Plan: The Distance

## Summary

Daytime, mid-week. Jerome takes a phone call from his sister Yvonne in Chicago about their mother, whose early-stage dementia is progressing. The conversation navigates the logistics of long-distance caregiving: the assisted living facility wants to discuss next steps, there are forms to sign, decisions to make. Beneath the logistics: Yvonne has been carrying more of the weight, lives closer, visits more often. Jerome knows this and resists knowing it. The guilt is there but unnamed, managed through practicality.

The call ends with a plan - Jerome will visit next month - but Yvonne's doubt is audible. He's promised before. After hanging up, he sits with the weight, tries to write, can't. Instead, he goes for a walk through the Baltimore neighborhood, seeing the houses he's been writing about - the algorithmic pricing, the invisible discrimination, the patterns that shape who lives where. The walk is both research and meditation, an attempt to transmute personal helplessness into professional purpose. He returns without resolution but with something shifted: the weight of care, the distance from his mother, the parallel distances in his own household.

## Key Elements

- The phone call with Yvonne: logistics as language, guilt beneath
- The caregiving divide: Yvonne closer, Jerome contributing money more than presence
- The assisted living facility: good care at significant cost, the economics of aging
- Jerome's guilt: managed but not processed
- The neighborhood walk: research and escape, the housing story made visible
- The parallel to DeShawn: Jerome distant from his mother as his son is distant from him
- The return home: no resolution, but weight acknowledged

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington** (POV): Son navigating distance, guilt, the limits of presence
- **Yvonne Washington** (via phone): Sister who's been carrying more, measured but clear
- **Their mother** (in conversation): The woman she was, the person she's becoming
- **The neighborhood** (as presence): The Baltimore streets that embody his investigation

## Setting

**Location:** Jerome's home office, then walking through the Baltimore neighborhood

**Time of Day:** 11:00 AM - 2:00 PM, weekday

**Physical Details:**
- The office where the call happens: the work interrupted
- The phone held to his ear, Yvonne's voice across the miles
- The streets of Baltimore in autumn: row houses, some renovated, some struggling
- The For Sale signs, the rental listings, the physical evidence of his story
- The corner store where he buys coffee, the faces that recognize him
- The walk back, the house approached from the street

## Stylistic Notes

**Mode:** Transitioning toward Carson - the fragmentary weight of loss, the compression of grief

**Sentence rhythm:** Shorter during the phone call, the information exchanged; expanding during the walk as thoughts have room

**Key technique:** The neighborhood walk as external correlative to internal state; the housing story as container for uncontainable feeling

**Emotional register:** Grief that can't be spoken, guilt that can't be relieved, love that can't be adequately expressed

**Atmosphere:** Autumn light in Baltimore, the particular quality that makes familiar streets feel elegiac
