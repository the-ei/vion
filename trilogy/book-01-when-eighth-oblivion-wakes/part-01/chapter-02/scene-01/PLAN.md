# Scene Plan: The Booth

## Summary

Jerome Washington in his home office in Baltimore - a converted spare bedroom that has become his professional universe since leaving traditional media four years ago. The scene opens mid-week, early morning, as Jerome prepares to record a podcast episode about digital currency and financial surveillance. The space itself tells his story: acoustic panels he installed himself, a professional microphone on a secondhand boom arm, books and files stacked with the controlled chaos of active research.

The recording session becomes a window into Jerome's method and voice. He's articulating the case against central bank digital currencies, tracking how they enable financial surveillance at scale. The argument is solid, the delivery practiced - and partway through, he stumbles, loses the thread, stops. In the silence that follows, his thoughts spiral: four years since he left The Journal, the investigation they killed under corporate pressure, the audience he's gained and the influence he's lost. The Pulitzer on his office shelf is fourteen years old. He wonders if it represents his peak. Then he restarts the recording, gets it right, the small victory of finishing. The scene ends with him uploading the episode, alone in the room, the craft sustained by will.

## Key Elements

- The home office as metaphor: independence and isolation, freedom and marginalization
- The recording ritual: microphone check, water at hand, the discipline of performance
- Jerome's voice and method: the careful building of argument, the faith that evidence matters
- The stumble as revelation: the doubt beneath the discipline
- The Pulitzer on the shelf: achievement aging into memory
- The four-year wound: the killed investigation, the corporate pressure
- The recovery: completing the recording, uploading, the persistence that defines him

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington** (POV): Journalist in the middle of his craft, confronting its costs
- **His work** (as presence): The accumulated research, the evidence, the unwritten stories
- **The Journal** (in memory): The institution that broke faith, the story they killed

## Stylistic Notes

**Mode:** Knausgaard - the texture of daily work, the specific satisfactions and griefs of creative labor

**Sentence rhythm:** Long sentences that build argument, mirroring Jerome's journalistic method; fragments during the stumble and doubt

**Key technique:** The work itself as window into character; we learn who Jerome is by watching him do what he does

**Interiority level:** Deep - we are inside the process, experiencing the recording through his consciousness

**Atmosphere:** Solitude that is both chosen and imposed; the dignity of the work and its precariousness
