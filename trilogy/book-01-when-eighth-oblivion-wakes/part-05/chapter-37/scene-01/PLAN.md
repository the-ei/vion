# Scene Plan: Media Appearance

## Summary

Jerome Washington on a video interview for a major news podcast - performing expertise, compressing complex truth into digestible soundbites, watching himself become content. The scene captures both the interview itself and its immediate aftermath: Jerome scrolling through reactions, the mix of validation and attack, the strange sensation of having his work seen by millions while sensing that attention is not the same as impact.

The interview focuses on his reporting about the crisis and the Eighth Oblivion concept. The host wants clear narratives; Jerome wants to preserve complexity. The negotiation between these impulses is exhausting. Afterward, alone in his Baltimore home office, Jerome watches the clip circulate - shared, praised, attacked, memed. The gap between the conversation he wanted to have and the conversation that aired becomes achingly clear. Being right feels hollow when rightness changes nothing.

## Key Elements

- The interview itself: the performance of expertise, the pressure to simplify
- The host's framing: seeking drama, conflict, clear villains - not the systemic analysis Jerome offers
- Physical setup: Jerome in his home office, professional background, ring light, the apparatus of remote media
- The immediate aftermath: scrolling reactions, engagement metrics, the dopamine/disappointment cycle
- Validation and attack simultaneously: supporters and denialists in the same feed
- The gap between attention and impact: millions see it, systems continue unchanged
- Physical exhaustion: the performance drains; Jerome is tired in a way sleep doesn't fix
- The question emerging: Is this helping, or is he just content now?

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Performing public expertise while privately doubting its efficacy
- **Podcast host (voice/video):** Professional, friendly, seeking the compelling narrative over the complex truth
- **Commenters (text/abstract):** The disembodied voices of reaction - praise, attack, engagement
