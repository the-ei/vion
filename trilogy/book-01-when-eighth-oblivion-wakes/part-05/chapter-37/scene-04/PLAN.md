# Scene Plan: Late Night Alone

## Summary

The house asleep, Jerome in his home office with his work. This is where he's most himself - not performing for cameras, not negotiating family dynamics, not weighing offers and threats. Just Jerome and the story, the ongoing investigation that didn't end when the crisis peaked.

He works on follow-up pieces, checking sources, tracking developments. He calls Ananya Ramaswamy - late enough that it's an imposition, but she answers anyway. Their conversation is collegial, almost intimate: two people who've been through something together comparing notes on the aftermath. She tells him she's on administrative leave, uncertain of her path; he tells her about the offers and threats. Neither has answers, but the connection matters. The chapter ends with Jerome at his desk, uncertainty not resolved but accepted. He'll continue the work - not because it will change everything, but because it's what he does.

## Key Elements

- The solitary work: journalism as practice, as discipline, as what Jerome does
- Follow-up investigation: the crisis didn't end the story; new threads to pursue
- The Ananya call: source relationship evolved into friendship; mutual reckoning
- Shared uncertainty: neither knows what impact their work will have
- The decision not-quite-made: Jerome will continue, but without the old certainty
- Night as refuge: the quiet hours when work can happen without interruption
- His mother as background concern: checking in on her care situation
- Writing as meaning: the act itself valuable, independent of outcome
