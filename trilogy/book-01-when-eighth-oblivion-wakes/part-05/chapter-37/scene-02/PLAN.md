# Scene Plan: Home Evening

## Summary

The Washington-Cole household in evening: Jerome home from his media performance, Denise home from teaching, DeShawn present but withdrawn. This scene establishes the domestic context of Jerome's public work - the family that bears the cost of his calling, the generational tension that the crisis has sharpened rather than resolved.

Denise brings her own stories of crisis aftermath - students affected, curriculum chaos, administrative dysfunction. She's not simply a supportive spouse; she has her own professional world in turmoil. DeShawn occupies the margins of the scene, working on code, surfacing for meals, clearly present but not engaging. When Jerome tries to connect with his son - asking about his projects, trying to bridge their disagreement about tech - it goes awkwardly. The tension is not dramatic but chronic: a family existing in the same space while living in different worlds.

## Key Elements

- Domestic texture: dinner preparation, the choreography of a family evening
- Denise's perspective: high school teacher seeing crisis through students' eyes
- DeShawn's withdrawal: present but not engaged, the resistance passive rather than active
- Jerome's attempt to connect: reaching toward his son, being met with polite distance
- The classroom as different lens: Denise's students show the crisis's human scale differently
- Professional stress compounding: both Jerome and Denise exhausted by their work
- The tech disagreement: DeShawn sees opportunity where Jerome sees threat
- Food as grounding: the meal as moment of forced togetherness

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Exhausted from media performance; wanting family connection; struggling to reach his son
- **Denise Cole:** High school history teacher; her own crisis stories to tell; grounding presence stretched thin
- **DeShawn Cole (17):** Talented coder; physically present, emotionally distant; his relationship with tech is the unspoken tension

## Setting

- **Location:** The Washington-Cole house in Baltimore - kitchen, dining area, DeShawn's presence at the margins
- **Time of day:** 5:30 PM - 9:00 PM
- **Physical details:** A home that's comfortable but not wealthy; the signs of two working professionals and a teenage coder
- **Season/Weather:** January evening; early darkness; the warmth of the house against winter cold

## Timeline

- Duration: Approximately 3.5 hours
- Position in chapter: Second scene, domestic context established
- Pacing: Medium - the rhythm of an evening at home, with tension underlying the routine

## Page Target

- **Target:** 7 pages
- **Range:** 6-7 pages
- **Rationale:** Needs room for three characters, domestic detail, and the layered tensions of family life

## Stylistic Notes

- **Mode:** Predominantly Knausgaard - extended domestic sequences, attention to mundane routines
- **Dialogue:** Natural, interrupted, the way families actually talk around dinner
- **DeShawn's presence:** Should be felt even when he's not speaking - his body language, his screen glow
- **Denise's stories:** Specific, grounded in her classroom reality; a different window onto crisis
- **The attempted connection:** Should feel real - Jerome genuinely trying, DeShawn genuinely resisting, neither villainous

## Connections

- **Next scene:** Professional decision scene will show the external pressures (offers, threats) on Jerome's work
