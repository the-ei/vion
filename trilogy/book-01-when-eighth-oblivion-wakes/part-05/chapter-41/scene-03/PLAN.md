# Scene Plan: DeShawn Conversation

## Summary

The breakthrough talk that has been building since Chapter 37. A weekend afternoon, the house quiet, and Jerome and DeShawn actually speak - not the careful avoidance or the abbreviated exchanges of recent months, but a real conversation. DeShawn explains what he sees in tech: agency, possibility, building things that matter. He's not naive about the problems, but he doesn't see exposure as the solution his father sees it as. Jerome explains what he sees: power concentrating, agency illusory, building serving the builders more than the built-for.

They don't agree. But they finally hear each other. DeShawn says something that surprises Jerome - maybe both things can be true. The relationship shifts: not harmony, but honest disagreement, which is better than careful silence.

## Key Elements

- The conversation itself: finally happening, both sides present and listening
- DeShawn's perspective: tech as opportunity, building as agency, problems requiring solutions not just exposure
- Jerome's perspective: tech as power concentration, building serving builders, exposure as necessary first step
- The gap: genuine disagreement, not misunderstanding
- DeShawn's surprise: the acknowledgment that both views might hold truth
- The shift: from careful avoidance to honest disagreement
- Father-son dynamics: love beneath disagreement, respect emerging from honesty
- Neither converts: the conversation isn't about winning, it's about understanding

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Finally hearing his son; articulating his own view; receiving a surprise
- **DeShawn Cole (17):** Opening up about what he sees; not backing down but not dismissing either

## Setting

- **Location:** Washington-Cole house - living room or DeShawn's room, a space for private conversation
- **Time of day:** Saturday or Sunday afternoon; the house quiet; Denise out or elsewhere
- **Physical details:** Two people talking without the buffer of household activity; the intimacy of direct conversation
- **Season/Weather:** Early March; late afternoon light; something shifting in the season

## Timeline

- Duration: Approximately 2 hours
- Position in chapter: Third scene, the father-son breakthrough
- Pacing: Slow - extended conversation, with pauses and careful listening

## Stylistic Notes

- **Mode:** Predominantly Knausgaard - extended dialogue, attention to the difficulty of honest talk
- **Dialogue:** Should feel real - not speeches but conversation, with interruptions and misunderstandings worked through
- **DeShawn's voice:** Should feel authentically 17 and authentically smart - not precocious, not dismissive
- **The surprise:** DeShawn's acknowledgment that both might be true should land without being sentimental
- **No resolution:** They don't agree at the end; they just understand each other better
