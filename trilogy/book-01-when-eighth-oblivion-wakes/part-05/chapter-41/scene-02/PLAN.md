# Scene Plan: Home Evening

## Summary

A different evening from Chapter 37's tension - the Washington-Cole household in a gentler configuration. Denise home from school with stories about students who've internalized the crisis differently: some anxious, some indifferent, some angry, all processing in their own ways. She provides the long view Jerome needs but cannot generate himself: change happens slowly, across generations, through accumulated understanding. "You're teaching them," she says. "They don't know it yet."

Jerome's mother calls with a health update - her situation stable but present, the personal ground beneath the professional struggle. The conversation doesn't resolve Jerome's doubt about his work's impact but reframes it. Education, teaching, the slow work of changing how people understand their world - these might matter even when the immediate effect is invisible.

## Key Elements

- Domestic ease: different from Chapter 37's tension; the family in a better configuration
- Denise's school stories: students processing crisis in various ways - a different data set than metrics
- The long view: Denise's perspective as teacher, thinking in semesters and generations
- "You're teaching them": the reframe - journalism as education, impact measured differently
- Jerome's mother's call: health update, the personal stakes grounding the professional
- The stability: Jerome's mother not in crisis, but present as reminder of what matters
- Reframing without resolving: Jerome's doubt not erased but shifted

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Receiving Denise's perspective, processing the reframe
- **Denise Cole:** Home from teaching; has her own stories; provides the long view
- **Jerome's mother (phone):** Health update; personal ground beneath professional concerns
- **DeShawn (presence):** In the house but not central to this scene; setup for next scene's conversation

- **Season/Weather:** Late February/early March; evening darkness but something softer than deep winter
