# Scene Plan: Home Evening - Jessie's Table Read

## Summary

Delphine attends a table-read of Jessie's crisis-inspired TV pilot. Her wife has been working on this fictionalized version of the events - a drama that uses the crisis as backdrop, transforms real complexity into narrative arc. Delphine listens to dialogue that makes the crisis into entertainment, recognizes the bones of reality beneath the fictional flesh, and is unsettled by how effective it is - how much she's drawn in despite knowing better.

The scene holds a mirror up to Delphine's own work. Jessie is doing with television what Delphine does with content - taking reality and shaping it for consumption. But seeing it through her wife's work, in their shared home, with their son Theo needing bedtime attention in the middle of it all, forces Delphine to see her industry's logic applied by someone she loves. The conversation afterward navigates the line between documentation and exploitation, neither reaching conclusion.

## Key Elements

- The table-read: actors around a living room reading Jessie's pilot script
- Jessie's pilot: fictionalized crisis drama, compelling despite (because of?) its simplifications
- Delphine's dual response: drawn in as viewer, critical as professional
- The mirror effect: Jessie's work reflecting Delphine's own professional operations
- Theo's interruption: 4-year-old needs bedtime routine; crisis waits for no toddler
- Post-read conversation: Delphine and Jessie navigating the documentation/exploitation question
- Domestic context: their LA home, the intersection of two creative careers, parenting amidst work
- Unresolved tension: the conversation doesn't conclude; the question remains open

## Characters Present

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes (POV):** Watching her wife's work, seeing her own reflection
- **Jessie Barnes:** TV writer; excited about the project; not naive but differently positioned
- **Theo Barnes (4):** Needs bedtime; his demands interrupt and ground the adult conversation
