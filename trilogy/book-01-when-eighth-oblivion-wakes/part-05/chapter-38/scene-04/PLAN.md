# Scene Plan: Mother's Call

## Summary

Delphine's weekly video call with her mother Adaeze in London. These calls are both essential and inadequate - the connection across distance, the translation between worlds. Adaeze asks about the project, about Theo, about life. She mentions a BBC documentary about AI that she found confusing and asks Delphine to explain.

In explaining, Delphine hears herself simplify - the exact operation she's been questioning in her professional work. She watches herself translate complex reality into something her mother can grasp, and recognizes this as both necessary (communication requires translation) and potentially corrupting (something is always lost). Her mother's response cuts through the self-doubt: "Then make something better." The chapter ends with Delphine beginning to understand what that might mean - not perfection, but intention; not purity, but effort.

## Key Elements

- The video call ritual: weekly, scheduled, the structure of long-distance family connection
- Adaeze's questions: practical, grounded, from a perspective outside American media
- The explanation moment: Delphine simplifying AI concepts for her mother
- Self-recognition: hearing herself do what she's been criticizing
- Adaeze's response: practical wisdom cutting through analysis paralysis
- The father's memory: his absence two years later, his belief in making things that matter
- Nigerian-Welsh perspective: Adaeze's different relationship to British/American media culture
- Emerging clarity: not resolution, but the beginning of understanding what "better" might mean
