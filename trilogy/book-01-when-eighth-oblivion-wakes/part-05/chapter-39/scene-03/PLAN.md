# Scene Plan: Evening Alone

## Summary

Ruth in her hotel room after the day's meetings - room service, the particular loneliness of travel after loss, the space for reflection that Washington days rarely allow. She calls her son David, then her daughter Rebecca, and the two conversations illuminate different aspects of her thinking and different possible paths.

David is doing well - his fund positioned smartly during the crisis, profit extracted from disruption. Ruth feels the familiar disappointment she tries not to express: her son has become something she doesn't admire. Rebecca is exhausted, overworked, her social work clients in crisis, but also more alive than David. When Rebecca asks what Ruth thinks is really happening, Ruth finds herself being more honest than she was in the meetings. The conversation approaches something meaningful before Rebecca has to go. Ruth is left with the gap between her public consultations and her private understanding.

## Key Elements

- Hotel room solitude: the particular loneliness of travel, intensified by widowhood
- Room service dinner: the minor comforts and inadequacies of hotel living
- David call: son in finance, doing well in ways Ruth doesn't respect; love and disappointment intertwined
- Rebecca call: daughter as social worker, exhausted but aligned with Ruth's values
- The honesty differential: Ruth more candid with Rebecca than she was in meetings
- Susan's absence: the conversations Ruth would have had, now impossible
- The question of legacy: what did Ruth build in forty years? What will she leave?
- Private versus public understanding: what Ruth knows that she couldn't convey to institutions

## Characters Present

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Processing the day, connecting with children, reckoning with legacy
- **David Abramson (phone):** Son in finance; successful, disappointing; their conversation careful
- **Rebecca Abramson (phone):** Daughter as social worker; exhausted, values-aligned; genuine conversation possible
- **Susan (memory):** Absent partner for these reflections; her ghost in the room

## Setting

- **Location:** Ruth's hotel room near Capitol Hill
- **Time of day:** 6:00 PM - 10:00 PM
- **Physical details:** Hotel room anonymous but comfortable; room service tray; the view of DC at night
- **Season/Weather:** February evening; the city lit up outside; cold kept at bay by hotel warmth

- **Mode:** Predominantly Knausgaard - extended interiority, the texture of solitary evening
- **Phone call distinctions:** David conversation should feel guarded; Rebecca conversation should feel warmer
- **Susan's presence:** Not overwhelming but persistent - Ruth noticing her absence
- **The honesty with Rebecca:** Should feel like a release, saying what she couldn't say in meetings
- **Hotel details:** The specific textures of a good hotel - comfort that doesn't quite satisfy
