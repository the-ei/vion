# Scene Plan: The Meetings

## Summary

A day of consultations with congressional staff and officials - Ruth encountering institutional Washington's attempt to respond to the crisis. They want clear answers: Is this legal? What regulation would help? Can we stop it? Ruth tries to explain that the questions themselves are malformed, that legal frameworks designed for slower changes cannot simply be applied to accelerating technological transformation. She watches them not understand.

The scene captures the gap between institutional capacity and the problems institutions face. The staffers are young, earnest, overwhelmed - working in frameworks Ruth helped build, now recognizing those frameworks' inadequacy. One staffer asks a better question, and Ruth notes them as potentially useful - a seed for future development. But the overall impression is of institutional failure not through malice but through incapacity: the machinery of government unable to process what's happening.

## Key Elements

- Multiple meetings: committee staff, briefings, the rhythm of official Washington consultations
- The questions asked: seeking simple regulatory fixes to systemic transformations
- Ruth's explanations: trying to convey complexity, meeting incomprehension
- The staffers: young, earnest, overwhelmed - not villains but products of institutional limits
- One sharp staffer: asks a better question, recognized by Ruth as potentially valuable
- The constitutional framework: Ruth's expertise applied to questions it may not fit
- Physical experience: conference rooms, coffee, the exhaustion of explaining
- The gap between expertise and influence: Ruth knows things they don't, but knowing changes little

## Characters Present

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Explaining, meeting incomprehension, assessing the institutional landscape
- **Congressional staffers (various):** Young, serious, operating within frameworks that may be obsolete
- **Senior official/Senator (one encounter):** More interested in Ruth's credibility than her ideas
- **The sharp staffer:** One person who asks a better question - distinguished from the others
