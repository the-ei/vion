# Scene Plan: The Decision

## Summary

Daniel comes home. The relief of his physical presence - his body in the house, his voice, his particular way of moving through space - is immediate and real. Elena has been carrying Roberto's death and her investigation alone; having Daniel here doesn't fix anything, but it makes the weight bearable. The scene follows them through an evening together: dinner with the kids, the chaos and joy of family reunited, then the quiet after bedtime when adults can finally talk.

But Elena finds she can't tell Daniel everything. Not yet. She tries - starts to explain what happened at the clinic, what she's found - but the words feel dangerous, and she doesn't want to make him complicit in something that might have consequences. So she shares part of it: a patient died, the system failed, she's upset. Daniel listens with the patience of a man who has seen his wife carry impossible burdens before. He offers what he can: presence, touch, the solidarity of seventeen years of marriage.

After Daniel falls asleep, exhausted from travel, Elena is alone again. She reads on her phone, searching for ways to understand what she's found. She discovers Jerome Washington's newsletter - his pieces on algorithmic discrimination, his investigation into hiring software, his Pulitzer-winning history. He's looking at the same systems from a different angle. Elena writes an email to him, deletes it, writes it again. By morning, she's made her decision. She presses send. The chapter ends with that action - a nurse practitioner in Phoenix reaching out to a journalist in Baltimore, two strangers about to become allies.
