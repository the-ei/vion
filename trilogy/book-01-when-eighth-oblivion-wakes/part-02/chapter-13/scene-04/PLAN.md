# Scene Plan: The Warning

## Summary

Several days later. The family tension hasn't resolved, but it has settled into a familiar truce - Jerome trying to be more present, Denise accepting his efforts, DeShawn maintaining polite distance. The investigation continues, but Jerome is more careful now, more aware of time passing. His source "R" has been silent since the document dump; Jerome has sent careful messages that received no reply. He's begun to wonder if R is scared, compromised, or simply done.

Then: an email arrives from an address Jerome doesn't recognize. The sender identifies no name, no organization. The message contains no threats, no demands. Instead, it contains information: a summary of Jerome's recent activities. Who he's talked to. What documents he's accessed. The shape of his investigation. Not everything - there are gaps, places where Jerome's caution paid off - but enough to make clear that someone is watching.

The email's final line is not a warning to stop. That would be too direct, too actionable, too much like evidence. Instead, it's a statement: "We appreciate your interest in market efficiency." That's all. The corporate blandness of the language makes it more chilling. Jerome reads the email three times. He saves it, screenshots it, documents everything. He doesn't tell Denise. He sits in his office in the dark, understanding that the story he's chasing is no longer just a story. Something has noticed him noticing it. The chapter ends with Jerome at his window, dawn breaking over Baltimore, knowing that what comes next will be harder than everything before.

## Key Elements

- Days passing in tense domestic truce
- The source "R" gone silent - worry about their safety
- Jerome's investigation continuing but more careful
- The email arriving from unknown address
- Contents: a detailed summary of Jerome's activities
- The gaps in their knowledge - what they don't know
- The bland corporate language of the final line
- No explicit threat, which makes it worse
- Jerome's documentation instincts kicking in
- Not telling Denise - protection or isolation?
- Dawn at the window, the chapter's end

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Alone with knowledge of being watched, calculating what it means.
- **Denise Cole (background):** Present in the house, but Jerome doesn't share this with her.
- **The watchers (implied):** Present only through their knowledge, their message, their institutional patience.

## Setting

- **Location:** Jerome's home office, Cole-Washington house
- **Time:** Late evening / early morning, several days after the conference confrontation
- **Atmosphere:** The particular dread of being seen by something that shouldn't see you

## Timeline

- Scene compresses the days since the conference
- The email arrives late evening
