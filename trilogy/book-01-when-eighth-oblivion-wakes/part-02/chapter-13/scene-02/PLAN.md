# Scene Plan: Washington

## Summary

Jerome takes the Amtrak from Baltimore to DC, a familiar trip that feels different this time. He's carrying documents he couldn't risk emailing, meeting people who might help him understand what he's found. The train ride is an interlude - time to think, to watch the mid-Atlantic landscape slide past, to prepare for what's ahead.

The first meeting is with Jamie Okonkwo, a congressional staffer for the House Subcommittee on Technology. They meet in a coffee shop near the Capitol, two professionals in a city of professionals, their conversation careful and coded until it isn't. Jamie is young - late twenties - sharp, exhausted by the pace of technological change outrunning legislative response. She shares what her subcommittee has discovered: multiple investigations into algorithmic discrimination, none complete, political pressure to move slowly, an overwhelming sense that the problem is bigger than any single hearing could address. She confirms that Prometheus Systems appears repeatedly across the committee's radar but always indirectly, always one step removed from specific harms.

Later, Jerome calls Ruth Abramson from a quiet corner of Union Station. Ruth is a retired federal judge, now teaching at Georgetown, who has written on algorithmic accountability and due process in automated decision-making. Their conversation reframes Jerome's understanding: this isn't just corporate malfeasance, it's a constitutional question. When automated systems make decisions about jobs, healthcare, freedom - who is accountable? What process is due? Ruth's questions are sharper than her answers, which is her gift. Jerome boards the return train with more questions than he arrived with, but better questions.
