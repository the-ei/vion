# Scene Plan: The Conference

## Summary

Jerome comes home from DC to damage already done. While he was in Washington, absorbed in meetings that felt crucial, he missed DeShawn's parent-teacher conference. He'd put it in his calendar. Denise had reminded him. He forgot anyway - or rather, he didn't forget so much as he didn't remember, the investigation filling every available space in his mind until there was no room for the ordinary obligations of family life.

The scene unfolds as Jerome arrives home to a house that's too quiet. Denise is in the kitchen, grading papers, but her posture tells him something is wrong before she speaks. The conversation that follows is not a fight - they've been married too long for theatrical fighting - but something harder: a reckoning. Denise's anger is the kind that comes from exhaustion rather than surprise. She's seen this before. She's accommodated it before. The question she asks is whether this time is different, whether this story is worth what it costs, whether Jerome can even see what it costs.

DeShawn's reaction is worse than anger. He's in his room, door open, working on his laptop, and when Jerome tries to apologize, DeShawn is simply matter-of-fact: It's fine. I didn't expect you to be there. The resignation in his voice cuts deeper than any accusation. Jerome tries to explain why the story matters, but his words sound hollow even to himself. The scene ends without resolution. Denise will endure. DeShawn will adapt. Jerome will continue. But something has cracked, and everyone in the house knows it.

## Key Elements

- Jerome arriving home to aftermath
- The quiet house that signals trouble
- Denise's controlled anger - not shouting, just truth
- The missed conference as symbol of larger pattern
- Jerome's attempts to explain, to justify
- Denise's question: Is this story worth it? Will it change anything?
- The family's history of similar negotiations
- DeShawn's reaction: resignation worse than anger
- Jerome noticing DeShawn's coding project uses Prometheus APIs (dramatic irony)
- The unresolved ending - damage done, life continuing

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Facing the cost of his choices in the faces of people he loves.
- **Denise Cole:** Patient exhausted past patience. Her anger is earned, her questions real.
- **DeShawn Cole:** 17, adapting to disappointment by lowering expectations. His matter-of-factness is devastating.
