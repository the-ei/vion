# Scene Plan: The Weight of Home

## Summary

The days before the interview are shadowed by family urgency. Halima's health is visibly declining - she's hiding it, as she always does, but Yusuf sees through the performance. Her medications aren't as effective as they should be; she's missing work shifts she can't afford to miss; she's tired in a way that goes beyond exhaustion. Yusuf watches her with the attention of someone who has already lost one parent to systems that chew up bodies.

Amina, meanwhile, is deep in college preparation. She's building spreadsheets of applications, financial aid options, scholarship deadlines. The first in their family to have a real shot at a four-year university, she carries the weight of their collective hope alongside the weight of their collective fear: Can we afford it? What if the scholarships don't come through? What if she gets in and they can't make it work? Yusuf watches her work with a mixture of pride and terror. Everything he does is so she can escape what he's trapped in.

The scene shows Yusuf's music taking a turn. Alone at night, he's working on tracks that are different from before - explicit where they were implicit, political where they were personal. The beats carry lyrics now: about algorithms, about invisible bosses, about being reduced to a data point. He's not sure if the music is good or just angry, but it feels necessary, like something that had to be said.

## Key Elements

- Halima's declining health: visible despite her hiding
- The medications not working, the shifts missed
- Yusuf's fear: his father's death as precedent
- Amina's college preparation: spreadsheets, hope, pressure
- The financial anxiety underlying everything
- Yusuf's role as family earner, the weight he carries
- Night music: the political turn
- Lyrics emerging about algorithmic control
- The shift from personal to political in his art
- The family unit under strain, held together by love and necessity
