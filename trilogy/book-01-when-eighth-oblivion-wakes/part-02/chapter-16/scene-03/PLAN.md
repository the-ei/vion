# Scene Plan: The Interview

## Summary

A Minneapolis coffee shop, afternoon. Jerome arrived first, claiming a corner table, setting up his recording equipment with the subtle discretion of someone who's done this hundreds of times. When Yusuf walks in, he sees a man older than expected, Black, with the particular bearing of someone who has been disappointed by the world without being defeated by it. Jerome sees a young Somali-American man with guarded eyes and an athlete's alertness. They shake hands. The interview begins.

The conversation unfolds over two hours, rendered in extended dialogue with interior commentary. Jerome asks about daily gig work - the apps, the algorithms, the ratings systems. Yusuf explains what he's figured out through experience: surge pricing manipulation, the way acceptance rates affect algorithm favor, the ratings threats that keep workers docile. But Jerome is a better listener than Yusuf expected. He asks follow-up questions that go deeper: How do you know when the algorithm is testing you? What happens to workers who resist? How do you survive in a system designed to extract maximum labor for minimum compensation?

Yusuf finds himself talking about his father - the warehouse accident, the workers' comp that ran out, the way his death was another form of algorithmic logic: workers as replaceable inputs. He talks about Halima, her health declining under the pressure of multiple jobs and inadequate care. He talks about Amina, the hope that justifies his grinding. By interview's end, something has shifted. Yusuf expected to give quotes; instead, he gave testimony. Jerome expected a source; he found a teacher.
