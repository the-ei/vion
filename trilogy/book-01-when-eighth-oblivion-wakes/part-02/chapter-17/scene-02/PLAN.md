# Scene Plan: The Commitment

## Summary

Elena POV. Evening in Phoenix, the children asleep, Daniel gone again - this time to Tucson for a shorter job. Elena sits at the kitchen table with her tablet, making the final decision. She's packaged her documentation: the MedAssist logs, her analysis of Roberto's case, the pattern she's identified across patient files. Everything is ready. All she has to do is press send.

The act of sending is rendered in its full weight. Elena knows what she's risking - her job, her professional standing, potentially her family's stability. She also knows what she's witnessed: a patient who died because an algorithm decided he could wait, a system designed to sort humans by perceived value, an infrastructure of harm disguised as efficiency. The documentation is her testimony. Sending it to Jerome is stepping across a line she can't step back from.

After sending, Elena waits. Within an hour, her phone rings. It's Jerome. Their first direct conversation unfolds in the scene: he explains what her documents confirm, she explains what she's seen in bodies. They're strangers who share an understanding. The conversation is professional but weighted with mutual recognition - two people who have looked at the same machinery and reached the same conclusion. When Elena hangs up, she sits in the dark kitchen, understanding that whatever happens next, she's no longer alone.
