# Scene Plan: The Wait

## Summary

Yusuf POV. The clinic waiting room, this time in Minneapolis for Halima's follow-up appointment after her urgent care visit. The scene grounds the chapter's abstractions in bodily reality. While Elena documents algorithms and Jerome traces corporate structures and Kevin Zhou witnesses deployment meetings, Yusuf sits in a plastic chair, waiting for someone to tell him whether his mother's condition is manageable or deteriorating.

The waiting room is rendered as social document. Yusuf observes the other patients: gig workers like him, elderly people on fixed incomes, families clearly struggling. He sees the intake computer, the triage screen, the way staff interact with the system - deferring to its recommendations, trusting its sorting. He doesn't know what the system does, but he recognizes its effects. Certain people wait longer. Certain people get less attention. The sorting is invisible but real.

Halima's name is called. Yusuf waits alone, his phone in his hand, the exchange with Jerome still open. He thinks about what he said in the interview, what it means, whether anyone will listen. The scene holds the human stakes in suspension: while the investigation gathers force elsewhere, here is a son waiting to learn if his mother will be okay. The results - manageable, adjustments needed, follow-up required - are neither catastrophic nor reassuring. Life in the margins doesn't offer clean resolutions.
