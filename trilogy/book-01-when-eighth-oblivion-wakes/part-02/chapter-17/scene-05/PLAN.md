# Scene Plan: The Name

## Summary

Jerome POV. The chapter's final scene, the part's final scene, the revelation that names what has been building. Later that night, deep in documents, Jerome finds something he hadn't noticed before. In an internal Prometheus strategy document - part of the original source dump from "R" - there's a reference to "Eighth Oblivion scenarios."

The phrase stops him. He searches through the documents for other occurrences, finds a handful: risk modeling frameworks, strategic planning memos, investment presentations. "Eighth Oblivion" is Prometheus's internal term for catastrophic civilizational failure - the cascade of interconnected crises (climate, economic, technological, political) that their models predict with increasing probability. But what Jerome finds is not just prediction: it's justification. The documents argue that only comprehensive monitoring, prediction, and control can prevent the Eighth Oblivion. The tools of surveillance and sorting are framed as tools of salvation.

Jerome stares at the phrase. The thing he's investigating has a name. It's not just corporate malfeasance or algorithmic discrimination or surveillance capitalism. It's a philosophy: that humanity must be managed, sorted, optimized to survive, and that the managers should be the ones who can afford the managing. The chapter ends with Jerome at his window, dawn breaking over Baltimore, the documents behind him, the name in his mind. Part 2 ends at this threshold: the cracks have revealed the shape of what's cracking. Part 3 will bring the tremors.
