# Scene Plan: Morning After

## Summary

Morning, too early after too little sleep. Jerome is already in the kitchen when Denise comes down, which tells her something is wrong - or significant. He's made coffee, his hands slightly unsteady from exhaustion and adrenaline. The scene unfolds as a conversation between two people who know each other deeply, negotiating the impossible: how much of himself Jerome can give to a story, and what that leaving costs.

Jerome tries to explain what he's found - the connected systems, the scope of algorithmic control, the way sorting humans has become infrastructure. Denise listens, asks questions that cut to the heart: Is this story different from the others? Will telling it change anything? What happens to this family if you're right? The conversation is not a fight but something more difficult - two exhausted people trying to honor both the world's demands and their own needs.

The chapter ends without resolution. Jerome cannot promise the story is worth the cost because he doesn't know. He cannot promise he'll be present for his family because the story is already inside him. What he can do is be honest: he's going to pursue this. Denise's response is neither blessing nor refusal - it's the acknowledgment that this is who she married, that her love includes this burden, that she reserves the right to be angry about it later. The morning light is gray. The coffee is getting cold. The cursor in Jerome's office blinks on.

## Key Elements

- Kitchen at dawn - the intimacy of early morning shared space
- Jerome's physical exhaustion visible: rumpled, unshaved, eyes red
- Denise's concern shifting through recognition, resignation, acceptance
- Jerome's attempt to explain the story's significance without jargon
- Denise's questions exposing the core tension: truth vs. cost
- Their marriage's history present in how they argue - they know each other's patterns
- Brief interruption when DeShawn passes through, oblivious, getting breakfast
- Return to the conversation after DeShawn leaves for school
- The non-resolution: Jerome will pursue this; Denise will endure it; neither is happy
- Final image: Jerome heading back to his office, Denise heading to school, the gap between them

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Exhausted, committed, guilty about his commitment. He's trying to be honest about what he's asking of his family.
- **Denise Cole:** Awake, wary, loving despite wariness. She's seen Jerome disappear into stories before; she's wondering if this one will bring him back.
- **DeShawn Cole (brief):** Passes through, absorbed in his own morning, a reminder of what the family is supposed to be.
