# Scene Plan: The Contact

## Summary

Jerome Washington sits in his Baltimore home office on a gray February morning, surrounded by the artifacts of a career in measured retreat. The room is small, a converted sunporch off the kitchen, and everything about it speaks to the compromises of independent journalism - the secondhand desk, the aging monitor, the filing cabinets stuffed with stories that mattered to no one but him. He's working on a piece about algorithmic hiring bias when the encrypted message arrives from an unknown sender who calls themselves "R."

The scene follows Jerome through the next several hours as he applies his investigative methodology to the source and their documents. We see his meticulous process: checking metadata, cross-referencing claims, researching the sender's digital footprint. The documents describe something called "Project Sieve" - a system that connects algorithmic decision-making across hiring, insurance, and lending. Jerome's initial skepticism gives way to focused attention. By scene's end, he recognizes this might be the kind of story he built his career on - the kind that matters. The kind his family has paid for.

## Key Elements

- Jerome's home office described in detail - the space of diminished ambition
- The encrypted message arriving, breaking the morning routine
- Jerome's verification process rendered step by step
- First mention of "Project Sieve" and its cross-sector applications
- The documents: organizational charts, code references, internal communications
- Jerome's internal debate about whether to pursue this
- Baltimore winter visible through the windows - gray, cold, pressing
- Reference to his independent newsletter subscriber count, his reduced circumstances
- Brief mention of Denise teaching, DeShawn at school - the family living around him

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** 52, investigative journalist. This scene establishes his professional expertise through demonstration - we see him work. His righteousness is tempered by awareness of what his righteousness has cost.
- **"R" (Source):** Appears only through encrypted messages. Voice is clipped, urgent, specific. Claims to work at Vertex Analytics, a data firm Jerome has heard of.

## Setting

- **Location:** Jerome's home office, converted sunporch, Baltimore rowhouse
- **Time:** Late February 2033, morning (8:00 AM - 12:00 PM)
- **Atmosphere:** Gray winter light, the house settling around him, coffee going cold

## Timeline

- Duration: Approximately 4 hours
- Starts with Jerome's established morning routine
- The contact arrives at approximately 8:30 AM
- Verification and analysis through late morning
- Scene ends around noon as Jerome decides to respond

## Page Target

5-6 pages (~1,400-1,650 words)

## Stylistic Notes

- **Mode:** Predominantly Knausgaard - patient, accumulating detail that makes investigative work feel weighty
- **Prose rhythm:** Long sentences that follow Jerome's methodical thought process
- **Sensory focus:** The physical space, the cold, the sounds of an empty house
- **Interior access:** Deep but controlled - Jerome observing his own reactions
- **Contrast:** The mundane setting against the potentially significant discovery
