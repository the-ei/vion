# Scene Plan: Night Thoughts

## Summary

Evening at Elena's house. She's made it through dinner - Sofia's report on her day at school, Mateo's toddler chaos, Abuela's quiet presence keeping everything together. Daniel calls again, FaceTime this time, his face on screen while he sits on a motel bed in Flagstaff. The conversation is the ordinary intimacy of long-married people - he shows her the sunset from his window, she angles the phone so he can see the kids before bed. But Elena can't fully be present. Her mind keeps returning to the day, to the patterns she noticed, to a question she can't yet form.

After the kids are asleep and Abuela has retired to her room, Elena sits at the kitchen table with her tablet. She's not supposed to access patient records from home, but she has workarounds - notes she's taken, screenshots she's saved, the informal documentation of a clinician who's learned not to trust institutional memory. She reviews what MedAssist recommended today, cross-referencing with patient demographics she knows by heart. The pattern becomes clearer: patients with gig economy employment, with no insurance, with addresses in certain zip codes - their recommendations skew toward conservative management, fewer referrals, lower resource allocation. It looks like efficiency. Elena knows it's something else.

The chapter ends with Elena alone in the dark kitchen, the tablet's light on her face. She doesn't know what to do with what she's seeing. She doesn't know if she's right. She only knows she can't unsee it now.
