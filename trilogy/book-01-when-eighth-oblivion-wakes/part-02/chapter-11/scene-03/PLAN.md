# Scene Plan: The Empty Hours

## Summary

Night. Kevin Zhou's life outside work - which is barely a life at all. The scene follows him through an evening that reveals his isolation in concrete detail. After leaving the office (late, as always), he goes home to an apartment that feels like a waiting room for a life that hasn't started. He makes dinner from a meal delivery service, eats while reading technical papers, opens his gaming rig.

Gaming with James is Kevin Zhou's primary social outlet. They've played together for three years and never met in person. James knows Kevin Zhou only as "WeiCode," a handle that preserves anonymity while enabling connection. Their banter is the closest Kevin Zhou comes to friendship - competitive, joking, occasionally genuine. But tonight Kevin Zhou can't focus. His mind keeps returning to what he's found. He makes excuses, logs off early.

After gaming, Kevin Zhou tries to call his parents in Shenzhen. The first attempt fails - technical error. The second attempt fails differently - the call connects but drops immediately, a pattern he's seen before, a pattern that might be technical or might be political. He reads Chinese social media through a VPN, trying to understand what's happening in the country he left at 18. Everything feels distant - the language, the references, the home that isn't home anymore. He goes to bed in an apartment that might as well be on the moon, full of knowledge he can't share, in a city of eight million people, alone.

## Key Elements

- Evening routine in empty apartment - meal service, technical reading
- Gaming with James as simulated friendship
- "WeiCode" identity - anonymity as both protection and barrier
- The gaming session: banter, competition, the inability to focus
- Failed phone call to parents - technical or political interference
- The uncertainty: is the connection failing or being blocked?
- VPN access to Chinese social media - distance visible
- Kevin Zhou's complicated feelings about China and his departure
- The loneliness that feels chosen but isn't
- The knowledge he's carrying, unshared and unshareable

## Characters Present

- **Kevin Zhou (POV):** His isolation made concrete, his loneliness shown rather than stated.
- **James (voice):** Gaming friend, presence and absence both. Their relationship is real but limited.
- **Kevin Zhou's parents (absent):** The call that doesn't connect. They exist in Kevin Zhou's memory, in his guilt, in the gap where family should be.

## Setting

- **Location:** Kevin Zhou's apartment, gaming setup, the phone that won't connect
- **Time:** Evening to late night (8:00 PM - 12:00 AM)
- **Atmosphere:** The specific loneliness of expensive isolation

## Timeline

- Duration: Approximately 4 hours
- 8:00 PM: Home, dinner, solitary routine
- 9:00 PM: Gaming session with James
- 10:30 PM: Logs off, tries to call parents
- 11:00 PM - 12:00 AM: Failed calls, VPN browsing, the long slide toward sleep

## Page Target

4-5 pages (~1,100-1,375 words)

## Stylistic Notes

- **Mode:** Carson - fragmented, white space, the void made visible
- **Gaming dialogue:** Quick, overlapping, the rhythms of online friendship
- **The phone call:** Technical detail (connection errors) carrying emotional weight
- **Interior access:** Kevin Zhou's rarely-examined feelings about home, family, belonging
- **Contrast:** The day's technical mastery versus the night's human failure
