# Scene Plan: The House That Holds

## Summary

Midday break. Yusuf returns to the apartment for the brief window between morning and afternoon shifts. The Hassan home is small - two-bedroom in a building that has seen better decades - but it's full of life: Somali textiles on the walls, the smell of food that Halima has prepared despite Yusuf's protests that she should rest. Amina is getting ready for school, her junior year at a magnet school where she's thriving academically. The scene captures the family dynamic: love and obligation braided together, the weight of being the primary earner, the hope invested in Amina's future.

The conversation between Yusuf and Amina is the scene's heart. They banter like siblings do - she teases him about his music, he asks about her grades with the half-protective, half-desperate interest of someone who needs her to succeed. Their father's absence is present in what they don't say: the warehouse accident eight years ago, the workers' comp that ran out, the bills that never did. Halima, preparing for her own afternoon shift despite worsening health, represents what Yusuf is fighting against - the way this economy consumes bodies it no longer finds useful.

Brief but significant: Yusuf notices his mother's fatigue, her difficulty standing, the way she hides her pain. He suggests she skip work; she refuses with the dignity of someone who has survived worse. The scene ends with Yusuf switching apps, checking for afternoon surges, heading back out. The apartment door closes behind him, and everything he loves is on the other side of it.

## Key Elements

- The apartment as home: small, worn, filled with family presence
- Somali cultural elements - food, textiles, language mixed with English
- Amina getting ready for school - academically ambitious, aware of family pressure
- Sibling banter - loving, protective, real
- Halima's visible decline - the chronic illness she manages around work
- Memory of their father - the accident, the aftermath, the ongoing absence
- Yusuf's guilt about leaving school, his hope for Amina's future
- Economic calculations constant: can Halima skip work? Can they afford the medicine?
- The pivot back to work - checking apps, planning the afternoon
- The apartment door as threshold between love and labor

## Characters Present

- **Yusuf Hassan (POV):** Home for a brief interlude, his love for his family visible in attention and anxiety.
- **Amina Hassan:** 16, junior in high school. Smart, driven, aware of her family's circumstances, determined to transcend them.
- **Halima Hassan:** Mid-50s. Working despite illness, proud despite poverty, the center around which this family orbits.

- **Sensory focus:** Food smells, textiles, the textures of home
- **Interior access:** Yusuf's love and fear, his determination to protect what matters
- **Contrast:** Warmth of home vs. cold of work waiting outside
