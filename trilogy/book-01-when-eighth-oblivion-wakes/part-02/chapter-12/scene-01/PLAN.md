# Scene Plan: Before Dawn

## Summary

Yusuf Hassan's day begins at 4:45 AM when his phone alarm vibrates under his pillow - silently, so as not to wake Halima and Amina in their small Minneapolis apartment. March in Minnesota means darkness at this hour, means cold that makes the car reluctant to start, means the particular brutality of a climate designed to break the unhoused and the precarious. Yusuf has been doing delivery and rideshare gigs for three years, since he dropped out of community college to help support his family. He knows the morning rhythms like he knows his own heartbeat.

The scene follows Yusuf through pre-dawn delivery shifts, the specific mechanics of gig work rendered in granular detail. Which apps to check first, which orders to accept, which to decline. The algorithmic dance: surge pricing appears and disappears, ratings threaten and recede, the invisible hand of machine learning shapes his labor in ways he can feel but not see. Yusuf is good at this - efficient, strategic, experienced - but being good at an impossible game only means losing more slowly.

He encounters another delivery driver at a restaurant pickup - Darius, someone he sees regularly, their exchanges a compressed form of solidarity. They share information: which algorithms are behaving strangely, which restaurants are slow, which neighborhoods tip well. Their friendship is real but bounded, constrained by competition and exhaustion. By the time sunrise arrives, Yusuf has completed twelve deliveries, earned $47 before gas and wear, and is already thinking about the next shift.

## Key Elements

- Pre-dawn wake-up in shared apartment, silent to protect family sleep
- Minneapolis winter as antagonist - cold, dark, dangerous
- The car as workplace, as liability, as lifeline
- Gig app interfaces described in specific detail - notifications, ratings, surge maps
- Algorithmic management made visceral: which orders to take, the calculation of time/money/ratings
- Darius as fellow worker - brief encounter, real solidarity
- The economics made plain: hours worked vs. dollars earned vs. costs
- Yusuf's strategic thinking visible - he's skilled at a rigged game
- Memories of his father surface - the warehouse, the accident, the absence
- The morning light arriving as Yusuf's exhaustion accumulates

## Characters Present

- **Yusuf Hassan (POV):** 24, gig worker and aspiring musician. Smart, angry, disciplined in his survival. This scene shows his competence in a system designed to extract without compensating.
- **Darius Thompson:** Fellow gig worker, early 30s. A recurring presence at pickup locations, their relationship built on shared exploitation.
- **App interfaces:** Present almost as characters - demanding, opaque, constantly shifting.

## Setting

- **Location:** Minneapolis - apartment, streets, restaurants, the geography of delivery
- **Time:** Late March 2033, pre-dawn through morning (4:45 AM - 9:00 AM)
- **Atmosphere:** Cold, dark, the particular clarity of exhaustion before sunrise

## Timeline

- Duration: Approximately 4 hours
- 4:45 AM: Wake, check apps, prepare
- 5:00 AM: Leave apartment, first pickups
- 5:00 AM - 9:00 AM: Morning delivery grind
- Brief encounter with Darius around 7:00 AM
- Scene ends as morning shifts to midday demands

## Stylistic Notes

- **Mode:** Mix of Knausgaard patience (the long hours) and Carson compression (when anger spikes)
- **App language:** Specific, almost bureaucratic - the voice of algorithmic management
- **Physical detail:** Cold hands, breath visible, the car's heater struggling
- **Interior access:** Yusuf's running calculations, his suppressed anger, his survival instincts
- **Pacing:** The relentless rhythm of pickup-drive-deliver-repeat
