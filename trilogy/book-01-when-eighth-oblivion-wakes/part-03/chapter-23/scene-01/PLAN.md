# Scene Plan: The Climb

## Summary

Delphine sits at her desk at Nexus Digital, watching the engagement metrics for their "Eighth Oblivion" coverage climb in real-time. The dashboards that usually give her professional satisfaction now fill her with dread. She knows this pattern - she's engineered it before - but she's never felt it from inside this kind of content. The piece is crossing from trending to ubiquitous. Other outlets are picking it up, each adding their own frame: Newsweek's "What You Need to Know," Fox's "Liberal Elites Panic Over AI Doom," NPR's careful both-sides-ing. The algorithmic amplification is kicking in: the more people engage, the more the platforms promote it, the more people engage.

She watches the numbers with the expertise of a virologist watching an outbreak. She knows exactly what's happening, which makes it worse. Her recommendation - "the theory everyone is talking about" - has become the theory everyone IS talking about. The meta-coverage she designed to keep Nexus's hands clean has become fuel for the fire. She's not just observing the moment; she helped create it.

## Key Elements

- The engagement dashboard: real-time metrics, the visual language of virality
- The crossing point: from trending to ubiquitous
- Other outlets' coverage: each framing multiplies attention
- The algorithmic amplification pattern Delphine knows intimately
- Her expertise as curse: understanding exactly what's happening
- The meta-coverage becoming fuel
- Her complicity made visible in numbers
- Physical reactions: tension, nausea, the body knowing what the mind resists
