# Scene Plan: The Challenge

## Summary

Jerome comes home to find DeShawn energized from his coding camp - a week of intensive work on AI applications with other talented teenagers. Over dinner, DeShawn talks about what he's learning, the projects he's building, the future he sees. His enthusiasm is genuine and informed; he's not naive about the technology, he's excited about it. When Jerome shares his frustration with Paula's caution, DeShawn asks to read the article.

The conversation that follows cuts differently than Paula's. DeShawn reads quickly, then looks up with a question Jerome wasn't expecting: "What if the people positioning for catastrophe are just being rational?" He's not dismissing Jerome's work - he's engaging with it from a different framework. What if AI development does mean some systems have to fail for new ones to emerge? What if the wealthy positioning for disruption are just the ones who can see clearly? DeShawn isn't callous; he's genuinely trying to understand a world that doesn't work the way his father's models suggest. Jerome can't easily dismiss him - because DeShawn is brilliant, because he's Jerome's son, because his questions touch something Jerome has been avoiding.

## Key Elements

- DeShawn's return from coding camp: energized, informed, optimistic
- His request to read the article
- The unexpected question: what if positioning for catastrophe is rational?
- The different framework: evolution, not collapse; adaptation, not failure
- DeShawn's intelligence engaging with Jerome's work
- Jerome's inability to simply dismiss him
- The generational divide sharpening but not hostile
- DeShawn's perspective as genuine challenge, not dismissal
- Denise watching them, hoping for connection
