# Scene Plan: The Editor

## Summary

Jerome meets with Paula Henderson, his editor - one of the few who stuck with him when he went independent. They meet at a cafe near her DC office; she's read the draft he sent overnight. Paula is experienced enough to recognize the quality of what Jerome has written. She's also experienced enough to see the problems. The story implicates too many powerful interests simultaneously. Every major tech company, several investment firms, unnamed government facilities. The sourcing is solid, but the conclusions are systemic - and systemic claims invite systemic pushback.

Paula doesn't tell him not to publish. She asks him to narrow the focus, reduce the systemic claims, make it about one company or one pattern instead of the whole picture. "You can tell this story in pieces," she says. "You don't have to tell it all at once." Jerome resists. The story IS the whole picture. Telling it in pieces loses what makes it true. They don't reach agreement. Paula says she'll publish it - she believes him, she thinks it matters - but she wants him to understand the consequences. "This isn't like your other work. This one will follow you." Jerome leaves knowing she's right and unable to back down.

## Key Elements

- The meeting with Paula: experienced editor, careful ally
- Her recognition of the quality and the problems
- The concern: too many powerful interests, systemic claims
- Her suggestion: narrow the focus, tell it in pieces
- Jerome's resistance: the whole picture IS the story
- No agreement reached, but commitment to publish
- Her warning: "This one will follow you"
- Jerome's acknowledgment and refusal to back down
