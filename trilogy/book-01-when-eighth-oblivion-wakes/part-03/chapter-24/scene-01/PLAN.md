# Scene Plan: Outside

## Summary

Kevin Zhou leaves his apartment for the first time in weeks. The decision comes suddenly - he needs food that isn't delivered, needs to move his body, needs to confirm that the outside world still exists. The moment he steps through the door, the world feels hallucinatory. The San Francisco morning is too bright, the sounds too loud, the people too many. A simple walk to the corner store becomes an ordeal of sensory overload.

He manages to buy groceries - bread, eggs, orange juice, the basics his body has been denied. The cashier looks at him with mild concern; he realizes he probably looks as deteriorated as he feels. On his way back, he encounters a neighbor in the hallway - a woman he's nodded to for two years, whose name he's never learned. She doesn't recognize him at first. "Oh - Kevin? You've been... away?" He mumbles something about a project. Her polite smile doesn't hide her alarm. Back in his apartment, he stands in the kitchen holding the grocery bag, realizing how far he's drifted from normal existence. The question is whether to try to return or to keep drifting toward whatever is out there in the Nevada desert.

## Key Elements

- The decision to leave: sudden, almost physical necessity
- The hallway and building as first contact with outside
- Sensory overload: light, sound, people, the assault of normal life
- The corner store expedition: minimal but maximum effort
- The cashier's concerned look: social mirror
- The neighbor encounter: "You've been away?"
- Her failure to recognize him as measure of his change
- Return to apartment: standing with groceries, the question crystallizing
- The choice taking shape: return to normal or pursue the unknown
