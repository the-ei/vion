# Scene Plan: The Video

## Summary

Jerome Washington receives an encrypted message from Rachel Stern, a former colleague now working in tech journalism. The message contains a link: "You need to see this." Jerome clicks through to a video essay that has been spreading across platforms - a 47-minute AI-generated synthesis titled "The Eighth Oblivion: A Pattern Language for Collapse." He watches with his journalist's eye, initially skeptical of the conspiratorial aesthetic: the ominous music, the too-smooth narration, the rapid cuts between data visualizations and expert clips. But then he recognizes data points from his own investigations. Financial flows he's tracked. Corporate connections he's mapped. Predictions that match his unpublished findings. The video isn't quoting his work - it's arriving at the same conclusions through different routes.

By the end, Jerome is unsettled in a way he hasn't been in decades of investigative journalism. He doesn't know if he's watching the most sophisticated disinformation he's ever encountered or an AI-enabled synthesis that's seeing what humans can't.

## Key Elements

- The encrypted message from Rachel Stern with the link
- Jerome's home office in Baltimore: papers, screens, the organized chaos of an independent journalist
- The video essay itself: format, style, structure, the "Eighth Oblivion" concept explained
- His initial skepticism giving way to recognition
- Specific data points that match his own unpublished research
- The epistemological vertigo: is this truth or the most clever lie?
- His decision to investigate the video's origins rather than dismiss it
- The moment when he pauses the video on the phrase "eighth oblivion"
