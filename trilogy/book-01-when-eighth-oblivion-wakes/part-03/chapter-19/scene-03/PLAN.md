# Scene Plan: Dinner Table

## Summary

Jerome joins Denise and DeShawn for dinner, attempting to be present after days of obsessive investigation. The meal begins with family rhythms: Denise's stories from school, DeShawn's update on his coding projects. But Jerome is distracted, and Denise notices. When she asks what he's working on, he tries to explain the "Eighth Oblivion" video and his investigation into it. DeShawn, 17 and fluent in the information environment his father struggles to navigate, responds with unexpected challenge. "That video's been around for weeks, Dad. Everyone's doing their own take on it. It's a meme format now." Jerome asks if DeShawn believes it. DeShawn shrugs: "It's not about believing. It's about what you do with the frame."

The generational divide crystallizes. Jerome needs truth to be stable, verifiable, actionable. DeShawn lives in a world where truth is raw material, remixed and reframed. Denise watches them both, worried about the gulf opening in her family.

## Key Elements

- Domestic texture: the dinner table, the meal itself, the family ritual
- Denise's perceptive concern about Jerome's distraction
- DeShawn's casual expertise with the information environment
- The "Eighth Oblivion" video as meme: what happens when crisis becomes content
- Jerome's discomfort with DeShawn's epistemological fluidity
- The generational divide about what counts as knowledge
- Denise as mediator, increasingly concerned about both of them
- The unfinished meal, the conversation that doesn't resolve

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington** (POV): 52, trying to reconnect but failing
- **Denise Cole** (secondary): His wife, watching her family strain
- **DeShawn Cole** (secondary): 17, challenging his father without meaning to

## Setting

- **Location:** The Washington-Cole family home, Baltimore; dining room
- **Time of Day:** Evening, around 7:00 PM
- **Atmosphere:** Warm summer evening, windows open, the sounds of the neighborhood; domesticity as counterpoint to digital obsession

## Timeline

- Duration: Approximately 2 hours (dinner through aftermath)
- Date: Day 3 of the chapter
- Position: The personal cost emerging

5 pages (~1,375 words)

## Stylistic Notes

- **Knausgaard mode dominant:** Long sentences, domestic detail, the texture of family life, the weight of things unsaid
- The dinner should feel ordinary and freighted simultaneously
- DeShawn's perspective rendered without condescension: he's not naive, he's adapted
- Denise's worry communicated through glances, silences, the things she doesn't say
- The generational divide should feel real, not schematic
- Food as texture: what they eat, how they eat, who clears the table
