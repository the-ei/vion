# Scene Plan: The Meeting

## Summary

Delphine sits in an editorial meeting at Nexus Digital, the content company where she's creative director. The topic: what to do with the "Eighth Oblivion" content that's been spreading across platforms. Her colleagues argue different positions. Cameron, the head of growth, sees engagement opportunity: "This is trending harder than anything in months." Priya, the fact-check lead, urges caution: "We can't verify most of the claims." Raj, the editor-in-chief, wants a framework: "How do we cover this responsibly?" Delphine listens, realizing no one is asking the most important question: Is it true?

The meeting becomes a case study in how media companies process reality. The content is evaluated for engagement potential, legal risk, brand alignment, advertiser comfort. Truth is not on the agenda - not from negligence, but from the evolved nature of the enterprise. Delphine understands this better than anyone. She helped build this system. The meeting ends with the decision deferred for "more analysis" - meaning more data on engagement trajectories.

## Key Elements

- The editorial meeting room: glass walls, standing desks, the aesthetics of modern media
- Different positions: growth (Cameron), fact-check (Priya), editorial (Raj)
- The "Eighth Oblivion" video playing on the conference screen
- Delphine's internal monologue: seeing how the system processes content
- The absence of truth as a category in the discussion
- Her complicity made visible: she helped build the system now chewing on reality
- The deferred decision: waiting for engagement data
- Her realization that she'll have to make a personal choice about how to respond

## Stylistic Notes

- **Knausgaard mode:** The banal mechanics of consequential decisions; every detail of the room, the people, the silences
- The video on screen as intrusion of crisis into routine
- Each colleague's position rendered with sympathy: they're not villains, they're professionals
- Delphine's interiority split between participant and observer
- The meeting's language as subject: the vocabulary of modern media ("engagement," "reach," "sentiment")
