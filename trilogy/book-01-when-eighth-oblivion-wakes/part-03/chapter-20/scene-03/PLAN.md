# Scene Plan: Dinner

## Summary

Delphine comes home to Jessie and Theo. After putting Theo to bed - stories, negotiations about one more book, the rituals of parenting - the adults sit on the patio with wine. Delphine describes her dilemma: the video, the meeting, her analysis. Jessie listens as only another storyteller can. She writes for television; she understands narrative manipulation intimately. But Jessie's perspective is different: "Every story is manipulation. The question is what you're manipulating people toward."

Their conversation clarifies something for Delphine without offering resolution. Jessie asks what Delphine would do if the content is true. Delphine doesn't know. Jessie asks what she'd do if it's false. Delphine doesn't know that either. The dilemma isn't about truth or falsity - it's about her role in a system that processes both the same way. Jessie suggests: maybe the question isn't what the content is, but what Delphine wants to become.

## Key Elements

- Theo's bedtime routine: stories, negotiations, the love labor of parenting
- The patio: wine, night air, LA's strange combination of smog and stars
- Jessie's perspective as TV writer: narrative manipulation as craft
- Their conversation about what it means to be a storyteller in a manipulated world
- Jessie's question: "What do you want to become?"
- Delphine's inability to answer, and why that matters
- Their marriage as refuge: still solid, still a place where hard things can be discussed
- The stakes raised: this isn't just about content, it's about identity

## Characters Present

- **Theo** (brief): Their 4-year-old, innocence as moral weight
