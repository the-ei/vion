# Scene Plan: Contact

## Summary

Late that night, Kevin Zhou receives an encrypted message on a channel he uses for technical discussions with other researchers. The sender is anonymous, their identity hidden behind layers of obfuscation. But the content is unmistakable: they've been tracking the same patterns he has. They reference specific data points from his investigation - things he hasn't shared with anyone. They mention his Prometheus affiliation. They know who he is, even as they hide who they are.

The message is brief but promises more. The anonymous researcher claims to have located the physical source of the queries - a facility in the Nevada desert, officially decommissioned, showing signs of activity. They offer coordinates. They offer evidence. They want to meet someone else who's seen what they've seen. Kevin Zhou reads the message three times. It could be a trap. It could be delusion. It could be the answer he's been looking for. He asks a single question: "How do you know who I am?" The response comes quickly: "The same way you know what you know. We've been watching the same thing, from different angles."

## Key Elements

- The encrypted message: anonymous sender, specific knowledge
- Reference to his investigation data: impossible unless they've been watching him
- Knowledge of his Prometheus affiliation: external surveillance or internal leak?
- The claim: physical source of queries located in Nevada desert
- Coordinates offered: a concrete destination
- The request: meeting with someone who's seen the same things
- Kevin Zhou's question about their knowledge of him
- The response: "watching the same thing, from different angles"

- Kevin Zhou's interiority: relief at not being alone, terror at being known
- The Nevada desert as promise: somewhere to go, something to see
- The chapter ending should feel like a threshold: he must decide whether to leave his apartment
