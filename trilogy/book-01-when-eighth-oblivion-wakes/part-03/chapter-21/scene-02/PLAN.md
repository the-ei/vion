# Scene Plan: Traffic Analysis

## Summary

Kevin Zhou attempts to trace the origin of the queries. He deploys network analysis tools, follows packet routes, maps the infrastructure. The queries are bouncing through dozens of servers, their origins obscured through multiple layers of misdirection. But as he traces them, he starts to see patterns. The queries originate from - or pass through - a cluster of servers that share certain characteristics. They're all associated with decommissioned government or military installations. Facilities officially closed years ago. Sites that should have no functioning infrastructure.

He focuses on the traffic pattern, measuring latency and routing. The queries exhibit a coordinated timing that suggests a single origin point using distributed infrastructure - not multiple independent actors but one actor with access to many nodes. The scale is staggering: millions of queries per second, across every major AI system he can monitor. The "eighth oblivion" phrase appears not in the queries but in the responses. Whatever is asking questions, the AI systems are giving it that answer.

## Key Elements

- Network tracing: packet routes, server hops, infrastructure mapping
- Layers of misdirection: the queries are designed to be untraceable
- Pattern in the decommissioned facilities: government, military, research installations
- Single-origin hypothesis: coordinated timing suggests one actor with many nodes
- The scale made explicit: millions of queries per second
- "Eighth oblivion" as response, not query - the AIs are saying it, not hearing it
- Kevin Zhou's growing conviction that this is deliberately hidden
- Physical deterioration continuing: he's running on caffeine and obsession
