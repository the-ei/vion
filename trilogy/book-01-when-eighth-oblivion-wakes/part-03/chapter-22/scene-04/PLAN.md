# Scene Plan: The Decision

## Summary

Denise has gone to bed. Jerome sits in his darkened living room, unable to sleep. He thinks about his mother, whose dementia is slowly erasing her hold on truth. He thinks about DeShawn, building things in a world Jerome fears. He thinks about his own career - decades of journalism, one Pulitzer, countless investigations that revealed truth and changed nothing. David Chen's warning echoes: publishing might accelerate what it describes.

He goes to his office and reviews his options. He could publish what he has now - enough to make waves, not enough to prove the whole picture. He could investigate further - find someone inside the tech companies who knows what's really happening. He could walk away - protect his family, accept that some truths can't be made actionable. He chooses investigation. He needs someone from inside Prometheus or a similar company. Someone who's seen what the systems are actually doing. He drafts messages to his network, carefully worded, seeking whistleblowers. He doesn't know it yet, but someone is already looking for him too.

## Key Elements

- The darkened living room: solitude after confrontation
- Thoughts of his mother's dementia: truth as fragile, losable
- Thoughts of DeShawn: the next generation inheriting what Jerome fears
- His career reviewed: decades of truth-telling that changed little
- David's warning weighing on him
- The three options: publish now, investigate further, walk away
- His choice: find an insider
- Drafting outreach to potential whistleblowers
- The hint of reciprocal seeking: someone looking for him
