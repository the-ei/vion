# Scene Plan: The Confirmation

## Summary

Jerome meets David Chen - not a regular source but a contact from his financial crisis reporting days, now a senior analyst at a major hedge fund. They meet at a quiet restaurant in DC, both uncomfortable with the surroundings. David has agreed to meet because he's seen Jerome's earlier work and respects it. But what Jerome is asking about now makes David nervous in a way the financial crisis never did.

David confirms the patterns. "The smart money moved eighteen months ago. The really smart money moved three years ago. We're hedged." He explains: this isn't conspiracy, it's rational risk management. If the scenarios in the "Eighth Oblivion" video have even a 10% probability, positioning for them is just prudent investing. Jerome asks: if the wealthy are positioning for catastrophe, doesn't that make catastrophe more likely? David pauses. "That's not really our department." Before they part, David offers a warning: publishing this story might accelerate what it describes. Panic could become self-fulfilling prophecy. Jerome leaves the restaurant with confirmation and new ethical weight.

## Key Elements

- The meeting: a quiet DC restaurant, chosen for discretion
- David Chen as source: experienced, connected, ethically compromised
- Confirmation of the patterns: smart money has already moved
- The timeline: 18 months to 3 years of positioning
- The hedge fund's own position: "We're hedged"
- The logic: rational risk management, not conspiracy
- Jerome's question about self-fulfilling catastrophe
- David's deflection: "not our department"
- The warning about publishing

## Characters Present

- **Jerome Washington** (POV): 52, seeking confirmation and receiving more than he bargained for
- **David Chen** (secondary): 50s, hedge fund analyst, willing to talk but protecting himself

## Setting

- **Location:** Upscale restaurant in Washington DC
- **Time of Day:** Lunch, 12:30-2:30 PM
- **Atmosphere:** White tablecloths, muted conversations, the aesthetics of money and discretion

## Timeline

- Duration: 2 hours
- Date: Next day after Scene 1
- Position: Confirmation and warning

## Stylistic Notes

- **Knausgaard mode:** The texture of the restaurant, the food they eat, the silences between exchanges
- David Chen rendered with complexity: not a villain, not a hero, a professional navigating impossible terrain
- The confirmation should feel like validation and burden simultaneously
- The warning about publishing as genuine ethical concern, not threat
- DC setting as geography of power: where decisions are made that affect everyone
