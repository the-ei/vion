# Scene Plan: The Marriage

## Summary

Jerome comes home late from DC. Denise is waiting. She's not angry - she's worried in a way that's worse than anger. She's noticed the slide: the late nights, the distraction, the way Jerome looks through her instead of at her. She asks directly: is this story worth their marriage? Not as ultimatum, but as genuine question. She needs to understand what's consuming him.

Jerome tries to explain. He describes the overlay, the confirmation, what he's learned about how the wealthy are positioning for collapse while the rest of the world goes on normally. Denise listens - she's a history teacher, she understands systemic failure. But she also sees what Jerome doesn't: he's becoming consumed by a story he may not be able to tell, and the consumption is costing him what actually matters. "You can't save the world if you lose yourself," she says. He doesn't know how to respond. They don't resolve anything, but the conversation opens space for what he's been avoiding: the possibility that knowing isn't enough, that truth may not matter, that his crusade might be destroying what he loves without changing what he fears.

## Key Elements

- Jerome arriving home late, Denise waiting
- Her worry deeper than anger
- The direct question: is this worth their marriage?
- His attempt to explain what he's found
- Denise's understanding as history teacher: she gets systemic failure
- Her observation: he's being consumed
- The gap between knowing and acting
- No resolution, but honest confrontation
- Their marriage as real relationship under real strain
