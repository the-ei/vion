# Scene Plan: The Coalition

## Summary

A warehouse space converted for organizing meetings. Yusuf attends, more committed now than his first tentative presence in Chapter 21. Fatima facilitates as the room fills with people who shouldn't be allies: Jake, a libertarian small business owner whose opposition to corporate monopoly aligns with Yusuf's despite different ideology; Pastor Williams from a Black Baptist church whose dignity theology frames tech as dehumanizing; a young white woman named Sarah who quit her job at a major tech company out of conscience. Yusuf watches the improbable coalition negotiate shared action—protests, mutual aid, pressure campaigns. When discussion turns to gig work, Yusuf speaks publicly for the first time about algorithmic cruelty. People listen. He's surprised by how it feels to be heard.

## Key Elements

- The warehouse space: the aesthetic of organizing, found spaces repurposed
- Coalition diversity: libertarian, religious, tech worker, gig workers—unlikely allies
- Fatima as facilitator: skilled at bridging, holding space for difference
- Jake (libertarian): different ideology, same enemy, uncomfortable but necessary ally
- Pastor Williams: dignity theology, the moral framework that grounds economic critique
- Sarah (former tech worker): conscience-led defection, insider knowledge
- Yusuf speaking: the moment he moves from observer to participant
- Being heard: the surprise of mattering in a room
- Shared action: protests, mutual aid, what the coalition is actually doing

## Characters

- **Yusuf Hassan (POV):** Moving from suspicion to participation, finding voice
- **Fatima Osman:** Organizer, mentor, skilled facilitator, Somali-American like Yusuf
- **Jake Morrison:** Libertarian small business owner, late 40s, white, opposes corporate power from different angle
- **Pastor Williams:** Black Baptist minister, 60s, theology of human dignity
- **Sarah Reed:** Former tech worker, early 30s, white, quit major company out of conscience

## Setting

A warehouse space in Minneapolis, converted for meetings. Folding chairs, a makeshift podium, coffee and snacks, the aesthetic of grassroots organizing. The industrial setting meeting human community.
