# Scene Plan: The Hospital

## Summary

Halima's health worsens. Yusuf takes her to the ER at 3 AM, sits with her through the interminable wait. The hospital is overwhelmed; wait times stretch hours. Halima needs ongoing care that their insurance barely covers. A social worker mentions programs—most have waiting lists, most require documentation that complicates their family's immigration history. Yusuf watches his mother in pain while forms are filled. His anger crystallizes into something colder and clearer: this system is designed to fail people like them. In the waiting room, he pulls out his phone and composes a text to Fatima: "I'm in. Whatever needs doing." He makes a decision about Amina too: she should take the scholarship. They'll survive the irony. The personal has become political, the political personal.

## Key Elements

- 3 AM hospital trip: the terror and banality of health emergency
- The overwhelmed ER: wait times, understaffing, the reality of healthcare access
- Halima's pain: not dramatized but present, the body's vulnerability
- Insurance inadequacy: the bureaucratic nightmare of American healthcare
- Social worker's information: programs with waiting lists, documentation requirements
- Immigration complications: the family's history complicating access to help
- Yusuf's anger crystallizing: from hot to cold, from reactive to committed
- The text to Fatima: full commitment to organizing
- The Amina decision: accepting the scholarship, surviving the irony
- Personal becoming political: Yusuf's transformation complete

## Characters
