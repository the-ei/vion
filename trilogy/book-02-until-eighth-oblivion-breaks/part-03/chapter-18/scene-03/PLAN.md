# Scene Plan: The Long Night

## Summary

Jerome works through the night, chasing leads, reading technical papers he barely understands, texting sources across time zones. The house is dark around him; Denise went to bed hours ago. Some sources don't respond. Some respond with things that frighten him. He consumes information with desperate hunger—the ATLAS-7 announcement, the technical specs released, the economic projections already circulating, the think pieces proliferating. By 4 AM, he has the outline of a story but also a growing sense that the story is already too late. Dawn arrives with terrible clarity: the curve has revealed itself, and there's no going back to not seeing it.

## Key Elements

- The night as duration: hours of work, the house's darkness, the isolation of obsession
- Research methodology under stress: tabs, documents, sources, the failure to synthesize
- Sources responding (or not): the fragmentary communication of whistleblowers, insiders, colleagues
- Technical papers Jerome barely understands: reaching limits of comprehension
- The economic projections: first glimpses of displacement scale
- Physical toll: exhaustion, coffee, the body refusing to rest
- Dawn as revelation: light returning, the story taking shape, the horror of clarity
- The question: does any of this matter? Will anyone listen?
- Ending message to Ananya: reaching toward the inside view

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Journalist as night owl, obsessive, alone with the implications, his craft meeting its limits
- **Denise Washington (asleep):** Presence through absence; Jerome's awareness of her rest, his inability to join her
- **Sources (text/encrypted apps):** Fragmentary voices—a former Prometheus engineer who says "it's worse than they're saying"; a tech journalist who responds "I know, but what can we do?"; silence from two people who should have answered
- **Ananya Ramaswamy (message sent):** Jerome reaches out at chapter's end; she becomes relevant to his investigation

## Setting

Jerome's home office, then the kitchen, then the living room as he moves through the night. The house as container for his wakefulness. Screens glowing in darkness. Coffee cup after coffee cup. The slow gray arrival of dawn through windows.

## Timeline

- **Date:** March 2035, overnight
- **Time:** 11 PM to 6 AM
- **Duration:** Seven hours of work and worry
- **Context:** The same night as the announcement; the immediate aftermath extending into the next day

## Stylistic Notes

- Carson mode intensifies: fragmented thoughts, elliptical passages as exhaustion and information overload compound
- The night itself as character: darkness, silence, the texture of late hours
- Technical language encountered but not mastered: Jerome's glimpses of what he doesn't fully understand
- Source communications as fragments: the partial nature of truth-gathering
- Knausgaard's attention to the body: fatigue, caffeine, the physical experience of obsessive work
- Dawn as both literal and metaphorical: the light returning, the clarity terrible
