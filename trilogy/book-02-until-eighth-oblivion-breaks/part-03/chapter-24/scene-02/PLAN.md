# Scene Plan: Rebecca

## Summary

The weekend. Ruth's daughter Rebecca visits from New York, where she works as a social worker. They cook together, walk in Georgetown, talk about everything and nothing—the rhythms of a close parent-child relationship. Rebecca's work is directly affected by the economic displacement Ruth has been analyzing: she's already seeing the downstream effects in her caseload, the surge in people needing help. Ruth shares more than she should about her involvement with Jerome's investigation. Rebecca's support is immediate, unquestioning. A phone call from David, Ruth's son, interrupts—he's heard rumors about a Prometheus leak and defends the industry, argues that regulation kills innovation. Ruth is patient; Rebecca is not. The family fractures along political lines.

## Key Elements

- Rebecca's visit: the domestic comfort of close relationship
- Cooking together: the physical acts of care, mother and daughter
- Georgetown walk: the neighborhood, the city, shared space
- Rebecca's work: social services seeing displacement's downstream effects
- Ruth's disclosure: sharing involvement with Jerome, crossing professional boundaries
- Rebecca's support: immediate, grounding, the validation of her mother's choice
- David's call: the son defending tech, dismissing concern, representing opposition
- Family fracture: political alignment creating personal rupture
- The two children as spectrum: Rebecca's solidarity, David's opposition
