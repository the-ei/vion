# Scene Plan: After

## Summary

Evening in Ruth's hotel room after the testimony. She's exhausted in ways that go beyond the physical. A long phone call with her daughter Rebecca, who works as a social worker in New York, provides unexpected solace. Rebecca is dealing with a surge in social services cases as economic anxiety spreads—the ground-level view of what Ruth analyzed from above. They share something real: their perspectives converging on the same conclusion. Ruth thinks about what it would mean to say publicly what she really believes. She decides to write something—not testimony, not a legal opinion, but something true. The chapter ends with her opening a blank document, beginning the essay that will become her real response to the moment.

## Key Elements

- Post-testimony exhaustion: the physical and emotional crash after performance
- The call with Rebecca: genuine connection, shared understanding
- Rebecca's work: social services seeing downstream effects, the ground-level confirmation
- Convergent perspectives: institutional and ground-level views reaching same conclusion
- Ruth's decision to write: moving beyond professional caution toward truth
- The blank document: beginning the essay that will break her from institutional constraints
- Evening light: the hotel room after dark, the city visible through windows
- Ruth's health: fatigue that's more than circumstantial, the accumulation of years
- The shift from legal expertise to personal witness

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Recovering from testimony, making a decision to speak truthfully
- **Rebecca Abramson (phone):** Ruth's daughter, social worker, providing ground-level verification, representing Ruth's hope
- **Susan Abramson (remembered):** Ruth's late wife, present in Ruth's decision to write honestly

## Setting

Ruth's DC hotel room, evening. The same space as Scene 1 but transformed by exhaustion and the day's events. The desk where she'll write, the view of DC at night, the solitude that becomes productive rather than despairing.

## Timeline

- **Date:** April 2035
- **Time:** 6 PM to midnight
- **Duration:** Six hours of processing, talking, deciding, beginning
- **Context:** Same day as testimony; the immediate aftermath

## Page Target

6 pages (~1,650 words)

## Stylistic Notes

- Knausgaard interiority: the exhaustion that enables honesty, the archaeology of a decision
- Phone call as intimacy: the rhythm of mother-daughter conversation, the shorthand of family
- Rebecca's voice: practical, grounded, younger, carrying hope Ruth struggles to feel
- The blank document: the physical act of opening it, the cursor blinking
- Evening as transition: from performance to private, from theater to truth
- Susan's presence: not as ghost but as internalized voice, Ruth's conscience
