# Scene Plan: Preparing for Theater

## Summary

Ruth Abramson in her DC hotel room, the day before her Senate testimony on AI regulation. She reviews her notes, rehearses her talking points, talks with her aide about what questions to expect. But beneath the professional preparation is a corrosive awareness: she knows her testimony will be praised, quoted, and ignored. The preparation is meticulous anyway—forty years of professionalism cannot be set aside, even when professionalism seems futile. The scene interweaves this preparation with a phone call from her son David, who cheerfully dismisses her concerns about tech, and with memories of Susan, her late wife, who always saw clearly what Ruth tried to hedge.

## Key Elements

- Hotel room as temporary professional space: the aesthetic of government-adjacent work
- Testimony preparation: notes, talking points, the craft of legal persuasion
- Ruth's aide (Jennifer): young, competent, representing the next generation of institutional believers
- The corrosive knowledge: preparation for theater, the charade of democratic response
- Phone call with David: her son in finance, defending tech, dismissing her concerns
- Flashback to Susan: a conversation years ago about whether institutions can adapt
- Ruth's physical fatigue: 64 now, the body accumulating the weight of sustained clarity
- The gap between what she'll say and what she believes
- Washington DC as setting: the particular unreality of the capital during crisis

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Semi-retired judge, 64, preparing testimony she knows will be ignored, grappling with institutional faith
- **Jennifer Martinez:** Ruth's aide, early 30s, competent, still believes the system works
- **David Abramson (phone):** Ruth's son, finance, cheerfully dismissive, their relationship strained by politics
- **Susan Abramson (memory):** Ruth's late wife, doctor, always clearer-eyed, dead six years

## Setting

Ruth's hotel room in Washington DC. A respectable but impersonal space: desk, bed, window looking at other government buildings. The room where preparation happens, where Ruth is alone with her thoughts between public performances.

## Timeline

- **Date:** April 2035
- **Time:** Afternoon and evening before testimony
- **Duration:** Approximately six hours of preparation, calls, remembering
- **Context:** One month after the ATLAS-7 announcement; the Senate has scheduled hearings
