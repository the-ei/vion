# Scene Plan: The Hearing Room

## Summary

The Senate testimony itself. Ruth sits at the witness table in the Dirksen Building, facing the Judiciary Committee's Subcommittee on Technology. Her prepared statement is measured, legally precise—the framework she's been asked to provide, the expertise she's been summoned to perform. Senator Patricia Hawkins, the chair, is sincere but constrained. Senator Bradley Morrison grandstands for cameras. The lobbyists sit in the back row. Ruth has a moment of genuine connection with Hawkins, who asks off-script what Ruth actually thinks will happen. Ruth almost says the truth—that she doesn't know if democratic institutions can move fast enough—but retreats to professional caution. The testimony ends to applause that feels like a eulogy for something already dead.

## Key Elements

- The hearing room as stage: cameras, senators, staffers, the theater of oversight
- Ruth's testimony: measured, expert, the performance of competence
- Senator Hawkins: sincere but constrained, asking real questions within limits
- Senator Morrison: grandstanding, captured, representing the worst of the process
- Other senators: brief impressions, the range of engagement from earnest to absent
- The lobbyists: visible in back row, the actual power arrangement displayed
- The off-script moment: Hawkins asks what Ruth really thinks, Ruth almost answers
- The retreat to caution: professionalism as armor, the words unsaid
- The applause: hollow, the sound of democratic ritual divorced from power
- Ruth's body: the physical experience of testimony, exhaustion as presence

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Expert witness, performing competence while doubting its purpose
- **Senator Patricia Hawkins (D-CA):** Committee chair, sincere, limited by institutional constraints, genuinely curious
- **Senator Bradley Morrison (R-TX):** Ranking member, grandstanding, representing captured interests
- **Other senators:** Brief presences, the spectrum of engagement
- **Lobbyists:** Unnamed, visible in back row, the actual audience
- **Jennifer Martinez:** Ruth's aide, sitting behind her, believing in the process

## Setting

The Dirksen Senate Office Building, hearing room. Wood paneling, the elevated senators' dais, the witness table at lower level, cameras and spectators, the physical arrangement of democratic theater.
