# Scene Plan: The Shift, The Choice

## Summary

Evening deliveries. The surge pricing proves illusory—too many drivers, divided among too few orders. Between deliveries, Yusuf encounters Fatima, a fellow gig worker he's seen at the same pickup spots. She's organizing—not a union exactly, but something: collective action among workers the algorithm treats as independent contractors. Yusuf is suspicious (he distrusts all institutions, even nascent ones) but also desperate enough to listen. Their conversation is interrupted by work demands. His last delivery of the night is to a church basement where a community meeting is happening—people talking about automation, job loss, what comes next. Yusuf stays, anonymous in the back, listening. He doesn't speak. But something shifts.

## Key Elements

- Evening deliveries: the grind continuing, surge pricing disappointing
- Encounter with Fatima: fellow gig worker, organizer, offering something different
- The organizing effort: nascent, fragile, workers finding each other
- Yusuf's suspicion: his distrust of institutions meeting his desperation
- The interrupted conversation: work demands, the algorithm's interference
- The church basement delivery: accidental encounter with community organizing
- The meeting: working people discussing automation, mutual aid, collective response
- Yusuf listening: anonymous, skeptical, something stirring
- The shift: not conversion but opening, the beginning of political consciousness

## Characters

- **Yusuf Hassan (POV):** Worker at a crossroads, suspicion meeting need, listening for the first time
- **Fatima Osman:** Fellow gig worker, late 20s, Somali-American like Yusuf, organizing among workers
- **Community meeting attendees:** A range of affected workers—warehouse, delivery, rideshare, retail
- **Pastor Williams (brief):** Hosting the meeting, Black Baptist church, theology of dignity

## Setting

Minneapolis streets and a church basement. The geography of evening deliveries—parking lots, apartment buildings, commercial strips. Then the church: modest, welcoming, the basement set up for community gathering. A space outside the algorithm's reach.
