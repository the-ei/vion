# Scene Plan: Ground Game

## Summary

Jerome Washington arrives in Minneapolis to document the ground-level effects of automation acceleration. He checks into a modest hotel, rents a car, begins his reporting process. The city unfolds as different from the coastal world he usually covers—the Midwest's particular economic texture, the post-industrial landscape meeting tech disruption. He has contacts from his network, introductions to community organizations. A local organizer named Marcus—skeptical of media, tired of poverty porn—agrees to facilitate access but delivers a warning: "These people are exhausted from being studied." Jerome reviews his notes on ATLAS-7's projected impact, tries to translate statistics into stories, and feels the familiar tension between caring about his subjects and using them for narrative.

## Key Elements

- Arrival in Minneapolis: the airport, rental car, modest hotel—the infrastructure of reporting
- Minneapolis as different from coastal cities: industrial history, current struggles, specific geography
- Jerome's methodology: contacts, introductions, the craft of gaining access
- Marcus Oyelaran: local organizer, skeptical of journalists, gate-keeper figure
- The warning: "These people are exhausted from being studied"
- Statistics versus stories: Jerome's notes, the gap between numbers and humans
- The extractive nature of journalism: caring and using, documentation and exploitation
- Jerome's own economic precarity: the independent journalist's budget constraints
- Preparation for interviews: the questions he'll ask, the stories he seeks

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Journalist entering someone else's world, aware of his role's contradictions
- **Marcus Oyelaran:** Local organizer, late 30s, community leader, skeptical of media but willing to try
- **Hotel clerk, rental car agent (brief):** The minor characters who populate travel

## Setting

Minneapolis. The airport, a modest hotel in a neighborhood that shows economic stress, the streets Jerome drives through, a coffee shop where he meets Marcus. The physical texture of a city he's visiting to document.
