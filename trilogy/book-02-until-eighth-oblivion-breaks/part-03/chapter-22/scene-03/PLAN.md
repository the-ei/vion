# Scene Plan: The Hotel Room

## Summary

Night in Jerome's hotel room. He's alone with his recordings, notes, and the weight of what he's witnessed. A phone call with Denise reveals both intimacy and strain—she supports him, but the work takes a toll on their marriage. Jerome works on structuring the story but keeps returning to the young Somali-American man's voice: the distrust, the clarity, the refusal to be used. The question haunts him: does any of this matter? He's filed important stories before that changed nothing. But he keeps working—not because he believes in impact, but because he doesn't know what else to do. The chapter ends with Jerome sending a message to Ananya Ramaswamy, asking if she has time to talk. The investigation's threads are converging.

## Key Elements

- Hotel room solitude: the after-hours of journalism, processing alone
- Recordings and notes: the material of a story taking shape
- Call with Denise: intimacy under strain, love meeting work's costs
- The young man's voice: Yusuf (unnamed to Jerome) haunting his thoughts
- The question of impact: does truth-telling change anything?
- Jerome's doubt: not despair but persistent uncertainty
- Working anyway: the discipline of craft without faith in outcome
- The message to Ananya: reaching for the inside view, threads converging
- The story taking shape: not yet complete, but forming

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Journalist in doubt, working anyway, reaching toward connections
- **Denise Washington (phone):** Wife, supportive, strained, the cost of Jerome's work visible in their marriage
- **Yusuf Hassan (in memory):** The young man's voice that stays with Jerome
- **Ananya Ramaswamy (message recipient):** The inside source Jerome reaches toward

## Setting

Jerome's modest hotel room in Minneapolis. Night. The desk where he works, the bed he's not sleeping in, the view of a parking lot. The solitude of travel, the isolation of late-night work.
