# Scene Plan: The Interviews

## Summary

Jerome conducts interviews across Minneapolis: a warehouse worker just laid off, a nurse dealing with algorithmic scheduling, a rideshare driver whose income has dropped 40% in three months. He attends a community meeting in a church basement—the same meeting or similar to the one Yusuf attended. Jerome records, takes notes, asks questions, performs the rituals of journalism. He notices a young Somali-American man in the back, silent but present. After the meeting, he approaches for an interview. The exchange is brief but charged—Yusuf's distrust of media, his sharp analysis, his anger. Jerome asks if they can talk more; Yusuf says he'll think about it. The encounter stays with Jerome—this young man's clarity, his refusal to be a data point.

## Key Elements

- Multiple interviews: warehouse worker, nurse, rideshare driver—the catalog of displacement
- The church basement meeting: community organizing, collective witness
- Jerome's interview craft: the questions, the recording, the performance of listening
- Yusuf in the back: silent, observing, noticed by Jerome
- The approach: Jerome the journalist seeking a subject
- The brief exchange: Yusuf's distrust, his sharp analysis, Jerome's recognition
- "I'll think about it": the non-commitment that's also not rejection
- Jerome's recording: voices becoming data, stories becoming material
- The weight of witness: what it means to document suffering

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Journalist doing his work, recognizing its limits, drawn to Yusuf's voice
- **Yusuf Hassan:** Encountered from Jerome's perspective—unnamed to Jerome, observed
- **Interview subjects:** Maria (warehouse), David (rideshare), Nurse Sarah (algorithmic scheduling)
- **Marcus Oyelaran:** Present at meeting, facilitating, watching Jerome work
- **Community meeting attendees:** The range of affected workers

## Setting

Multiple locations in Minneapolis. Interview sites: a coffee shop, someone's living room, a car parked in a lot. The church basement meeting—folding chairs, coffee, the aesthetic of organizing. The spaces where working people gather.
