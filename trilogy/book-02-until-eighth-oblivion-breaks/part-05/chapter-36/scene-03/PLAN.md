# Scene Plan: Fragments

## Summary

While treating an elderly patient for dehydration, Elena's mind replays moments from the break - not in order, not complete. The memories arrive as shards: a moment of complete darkness when the power failed, someone screaming about contaminated water, the emergency generator's terrifying stutter before it restarted, the face of a patient she could not save. These fragments are not explained or contextualized but accumulated, building a sense of what was endured without narrativizing it. The scene represents Elena's inability to process, the way trauma resists chronology. Between fragments, she continues treating the patient - the contrast between her steady hands and fragmenting mind.

## Key Elements

- Memory as intrusion rather than narrative
- The contrast between competent physical action and psychological fragmentation
- Specific sensory fragments from the crisis (darkness, screaming, sounds)
- A patient who died during the break - their face returning unbidden
- The dehydration patient as anchor to present reality
- Elena's hands steady even as her mind splinters
- White space and compression in prose - Carson's influence strongest here
- The refusal of neat chronology or explanation

## Characters

- **Elena Varga (POV):** Treating a patient while her mind cycles through crisis memories; the split between professional function and private dissolution
- **Elderly dehydration patient (Mr. Gutierrez):** Kind-faced man in his seventies whose presence anchors Elena to the present; he reminds her of someone (she can't remember who)
- **Crisis ghosts:** The unnamed patient who died, the screaming voice, the shapes in darkness - presences in memory, not in the room
- **The colleague who failed:** A flash of someone else's breakdown during the crisis, someone Elena had to work around

## Setting

- **Location:** Treatment area of the Phoenix clinic - specifically a curtained examination space
- **Time:** Midday (approximately 11 AM - 1 PM)
- **Physical details:** IV drip catching light, the elderly patient's papery skin, the curtain's pattern that Elena has stared at thousands of times, medical equipment hum
- **Atmosphere:** Artificial calm in the treatment space; chaos just beyond the curtain; chaos inside Elena's head

## Timeline

- **Duration:** Approximately 2 hours of treatment
- **Period:** Late November or early December 2036, first day after crisis
- **Position in chapter:** Central scene, the chapter's most Carson-influenced section

## Page Target

5 pages (~1,375 words)

## Stylistic Notes

- This is the most formally experimental scene in the chapter
- Carson-mode: compressed, fragmented, white space as meaning
- Memory intrusions in italics or set apart visually
- Short, broken sentences for fragments; longer, steadier sentences for present action
- Sensory details dominate - what Elena sees, hears, smells, feels, not what she thinks
- No interpretation of the fragments; they simply arrive and depart
- The elderly patient's gentle presence as counterweight to internal chaos

## Connections

- **Parent (Chapter 36):** The chapter's most direct representation of shell shock and trauma's temporality
- **Siblings:** Follows the mounting anxiety of Scene 2 with internal collapse; the eventual call in Scene 4 will provide partial relief

- **Book:** This is the break seen in retrospect - we learn what happened to Elena through fragments, not exposition
