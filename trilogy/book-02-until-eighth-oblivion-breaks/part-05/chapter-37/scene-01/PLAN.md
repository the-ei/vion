# Scene Plan: The App Comes Back

## Summary

Yusuf's phone buzzes with delivery requests. The gig apps have returned, their algorithms recalibrated but still wrong - offering surge pricing in areas that were evacuation zones, delivery addresses for buildings that lost power and may still be uninhabitable. Yusuf takes a few requests anyway because he needs money, because motion is easier than stillness. The deliveries are strange: a pharmacy run for medications someone ran out of during the break, food for a family just returning to their home, supplies for someone who never left. Through these small errands, we glimpse the texture of recovery - the city resuming not all at once but in pockets, unevenly.

## Key Elements

- The gig apps' surreal return - capitalism's relentless continuity
- Algorithms behaving strangely: surge pricing in evacuation zones, nonexistent addresses
- Yusuf's need for income overriding his awareness of absurdity
- Motion as coping mechanism - easier to move than to sit with what happened
- Three deliveries showing different faces of recovery
- Minneapolis geography: neighborhoods damaged and undamaged side by side
- The particular loneliness of gig work in aftermath
- Economic anxiety returning now that immediate survival is secured

## Characters

- **Yusuf Hassan (POV):** Back on the apps because bills do not pause for crises; using work to avoid processing
- **Pharmacy customer:** Elderly man who ran out of blood pressure medication during the crisis; grateful, slightly disoriented
- **Returning family:** A mother and two children, back from shelter, accepting a grocery delivery at their damaged front door
- **The one who stayed:** A younger woman who never evacuated, accepting a delivery with the hollow look of someone who saw too much

## Setting

- **Location:** Minneapolis - multiple neighborhoods, seen from Yusuf's car
- **Time:** First week after crisis resolution, daytime hours
- **Physical details:** Yusuf's car interior (the familiar space), his phone mount, the app interface with its glitchy map, snow beginning to fall, boarded windows alternating with intact ones
- **Atmosphere:** The surreal quality of normalcy attempting to resume; the city's uneven recovery visible through windshield

## Timeline

- **Duration:** One afternoon of deliveries (4-5 hours)
- **Period:** Early December 2036, first week after crisis
- **Position in chapter:** Opening scene, establishing Yusuf's return to routine and its strangeness

## Page Target

4 pages (~1,100 words)

## Stylistic Notes

- Knausgaard's attention to mundane detail: the mechanics of gig work, the car, the phone, the deliveries
- The algorithm's glitches as dark comedy - absurdity recognized but not dwelt upon
- Each delivery as miniature portrait: brief encounters that reveal aftermath's texture
- Yusuf's internal commentary minimal but sharp - his observations rather than his feelings
- The city itself as character: Minneapolis recovering in fragments
- Motion and stillness in tension throughout
