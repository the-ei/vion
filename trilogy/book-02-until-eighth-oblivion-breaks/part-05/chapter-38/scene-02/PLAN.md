# Scene Plan: What It Did

## Summary

Kevin reviews documentation of how his technology was used during the crisis. The technical details reveal that his systems helped - but also that they were repurposed in ways that violated every principle he thought he held. Privacy was abandoned. Consent was ignored. His tools became instruments of emergency power. He can tell himself it was necessary, but the necessity does not erase what it means about what he built - dual-use from the start, whether he admitted it or not. The scene forces Kevin to confront the gap between intention and consequence.

## Key Elements

- Technical documentation of emergency deployment - what Kevin's systems actually did
- The revelation that his technology was dual-use all along
- Privacy violations: tracking, surveillance, identification without consent
- The "necessity" argument and its insufficiency
- Kevin's rationalizations confronting their limits
- Specific technical details rendered as moral indictment
- The violence of optimization when applied to people in crisis
- Kevin's self-image as ethical builder crumbling

## Characters

- **Kevin Zhou (POV):** Reading documentation, confronting what his creation became, unable to maintain previous self-understanding
- **The technology (conceptual):** What Kevin built, now seen through the lens of its emergency use
- **The users/victims:** Unnamed people affected by the technology's deployment - present through data

## Setting

- **Location:** Kevin's apartment - at his laptop, surrounded by screens
- **Time:** Same day as Scene 1, evening
- **Physical details:** The glow of multiple screens, documentation files open, Kevin's apartment's stark minimalism, coffee gone cold, the San Francisco skyline through windows
- **Atmosphere:** The cold light of reckoning; screens as judges; the loneliness of confronting one's own complicity

## Timeline

- **Duration:** Several hours of reading and processing
- **Period:** Mid-December 2036, second week after crisis
- **Position in chapter:** Second scene, deepening the confrontation begun in Scene 1

## Page Target

4 pages (~1,100 words)

## Stylistic Notes

- Carson influence strong here: fragmented technical language becoming poetry of complicity
- Short, declarative sentences for the facts; longer, more uncertain sentences for Kevin's reactions
- Technical details should be specific enough to be damning without requiring technical expertise to understand
- The gap between what Kevin intended and what happened rendered through juxtaposition
- Interior monologue as Kevin tries and fails to rationalize
- No easy absolution - Kevin sits with the weight
