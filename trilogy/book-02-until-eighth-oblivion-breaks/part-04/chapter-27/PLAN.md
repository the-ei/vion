# Chapter Plan: The First Dominoes

## Summary

The chapter opens on Elena Varga's shift at the Phoenix community health center on what seems like an ordinary Tuesday morning in March 2036. The break begins not with a bang but with accumulating anomalies: the electronic health records system glitching, then failing entirely; patients arriving with stories of ATMs rejecting cards, rideshare apps frozen mid-route, pharmacy systems unable to verify prescriptions. Elena initially treats these as isolated technical failures, the kind her clinic has weathered before. But by midday, the failures aren't resolving. They're spreading.

The chapter charts the first six hours of what will become the largest cascade failure in American history. Through Elena's ground-level perspective, we experience the crisis as it actually unfolds for ordinary people: not through news anchors or expert analysis but through the accumulation of small impossibilities. The chapter ends with Elena trying to reach Daniel on a phone network that keeps dropping calls, the clinic's backup generator humming to life, and a line of patients stretching out the door with no system to process them.

## Key Elements

- The unremarkable beginning: ordinary morning routines disrupted by small glitches
- Cascade failure mechanics: how interconnected systems amplify single points of failure
- The gap between "technical difficulties" and civilizational rupture
- Elena's professional competence tested against unprecedented circumstances
- The clinic as microcosm: healthcare's dependence on digital infrastructure exposed
- First moments of the "before/after" divide that will define this crisis
- Knausgaard-style attention to mundane details that become significant in retrospect
- Carson-esque fragments as systems fail: broken sentences, incomplete information

## Characters Present

- **Elena Varga (POV):** Nurse practitioner whose clinical training becomes her anchor as systems fail around her
- **Daniel Varga (phone/absent):** Elena's husband, working a construction job three hours away
- **Dr. Samira Okonkwo:** Elena's supervising physician, initially skeptical of the scope
- **Rosario Mendez:** Longtime clinic receptionist who has seen every kind of crisis except this one
- **Abuela Varga:** At home with the children, unreachable by phone
- **Patients:** A cross-section of Phoenix's vulnerable population confronting the failure

## Scene Breakdown

### Scene 1: Before the Day Changed (~5 pages)
Elena's morning routine: waking before the children, checking on Abuela's blood sugar, the drive to the clinic in pre-dawn quiet. Small details that will become precious in retrospect. The world as it was.

### Scene 2: First Glitches (~5 pages)
The EHR system freezes during Elena's first patient encounter. She switches to paper backup, a procedure they've drilled but rarely used. More systems fail. The mood shifts from annoyance to unease.

### Scene 3: The Pattern Emerges (~5 pages)
Stories from arriving patients paint a wider picture. This isn't the clinic's system - it's everything. Elena tries to contact Daniel. Network congestion. Dropped calls. The first real fear.

### Scene 4: Institutional Paralysis (~6 pages)
Dr. Okonkwo tries to reach hospital administration. Radio silence. The clinic must decide: turn away patients they can't properly triage, or practice medicine without the infrastructure that makes modern medicine possible? Elena's training in resource-scarce environments becomes unexpectedly relevant.
