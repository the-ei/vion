# Scene Plan: Institutional Paralysis

## Summary

By noon, the clinic faces an impossible choice. They have a waiting room full of patients, no working systems to triage or treat them, no ability to reach the hospital for referrals, no way to verify prescriptions or access medical histories. Dr. Okonkwo tries to reach hospital administration, the health department, anyone with authority - radio silence. The systems that make modern medicine possible have vanished. Elena's training in resource-scarce environments - rotations in underfunded clinics, volunteer work after natural disasters - becomes unexpectedly relevant. She proposes they return to fundamentals: take vitals, take histories by hand, triage based on presentation, treat what they can with what they have. It's medicine as it was practiced a century ago. The chapter ends with Elena still unable to reach Daniel or home, the line of patients stretching out the door, and the generator humming in a building that has become an island.

## Key Elements

- The institutional decision point: continue or close?
- Dr. Okonkwo's futile attempts to reach authority: the hierarchy has gone silent
- Elena's resource-scarce training: experience that prepared her for exactly this
- Return to fundamentals: paper records, manual triage, physical examination
- The waiting line: people with nowhere else to go, depending on the clinic
- The generator as lifeline: the only thing keeping them partially functional
- Elena's unresolved family anxiety: still can't reach Daniel, Abuela, the children
- The before/after divide: by noon on March 17, the world has changed

## Stylistic Notes

The scene carries the most weight in the chapter, warranting slightly more length. Knausgaard mode for the internal deliberation - Elena's thought process as she proposes the return to fundamentals, her specific memories of resource-scarce medicine. Carson mode for the fragments of failed communication, the institutional silence. End on an image: Elena looking at the line of patients, feeling her phone's silence in her pocket, hearing the generator's hum - a Carsonesque moment of compression where the situation's enormity is present in sensory detail.
