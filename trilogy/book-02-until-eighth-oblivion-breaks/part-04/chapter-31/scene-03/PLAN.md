# Scene Plan: Mrs. Gutierrez

## Summary

Elena sits with Maria Gutierrez, an elderly diabetic patient she has treated for six years. The insulin is gone. There is no more to give. Mrs. Gutierrez's blood sugar has been climbing for two days; she is now slipping into diabetic ketoacidosis, and without insulin, she will die. Elena cannot save her. What Elena can do is sit with her. She holds the old woman's hand. She listens to her talk about her husband, dead ten years. Her grandchildren in Tucson, unreachable. The garden she hasn't been able to tend. Elena bears witness because that's what remains when medicine fails. Mrs. Gutierrez dies in the late afternoon, Elena still holding her hand. This is the chapter's most devastating sequence.

## Key Elements

- Mrs. Gutierrez: six years of care, now beyond saving
- The insulin gone: the specific absence that kills
- Diabetic ketoacidosis: the medical progression rendered clearly
- Elena's choice to stay: she cannot treat, but she can witness
- Mrs. Gutierrez's stories: the husband, the grandchildren, the garden
- The death: quiet, witnessed, grieved in the moment
- Elena bearing witness as the only remaining care
- Knausgaard for Mrs. Gutierrez's stories; Carson for the death

## Stylistic Notes

This is the emotional core of the chapter and must not be rushed. Knausgaard long sentences for Mrs. Gutierrez's stories - the specificity of a life being recounted. The medical details rendered clinically but not coldly: Elena noting the symptoms she cannot treat. Carson compression for the death itself: a few sentences, white space, the absence that follows. Elena's grief should be present but professional - she has been trained for this, but training does not erase feeling. The scene ends with silence.
