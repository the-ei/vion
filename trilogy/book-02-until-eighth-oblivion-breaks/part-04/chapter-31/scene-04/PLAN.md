# Scene Plan: The Warehouse

## Summary

Evening. Word reaches the clinic: a medical supply warehouse on the edge of Phoenix was abandoned by its corporate owner when the crisis hit. The company evacuated their managers and left the warehouse locked, full of supplies that could save lives. Some community members have already broken in, are taking what they can. Elena makes a decision. She will go. She will take what's needed. The law has already failed these patients - the law that protected the corporation's right to abandon supplies rather than distribute them. She organizes a group: Daniel, two volunteers, a community member who knows the location. This is the line she didn't know she had. She is about to cross it.

## Key Elements

- The abandoned warehouse: corporation evacuated, supplies locked inside
- Community members already acting: some have broken in
- Elena's decision: she will take what's needed
- The legal/ethical line: property rights vs. lives
- Organizing the group: Daniel, volunteers, a guide
- Mrs. Gutierrez's death as precipitating event: this is why
- The shift from caregiver to something else
- Radicalization through action, not ideology

## Characters

## Stylistic Notes

The scene should move: after Mrs. Gutierrez's death, Elena is energized by anger. Short sentences, decisive action. The ethical reasoning rendered not as philosophical debate but as simple clarity: people are dying, supplies exist, the owners left. Daniel's support is wordless - he understands. The departure into the Phoenix evening has a sense of crossing over: Elena leaving behind one version of herself. Carson's compression for the moral clarity that feels obvious now.
