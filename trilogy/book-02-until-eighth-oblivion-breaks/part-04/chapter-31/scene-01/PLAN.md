# Scene Plan: The List

## Summary

One week into the crisis. Elena Varga starts her morning as she has every morning since the break: reviewing the triage list. On paper, in her handwriting, the names of everyone who needs medication they're running out of. Insulin. Blood pressure medication. Anticonvulsants. Dialysis supplies. Cardiac drugs. Each name is a person Elena has treated, sometimes for years. Each name is a calculation: how much do we have, who needs it most, who can survive without. She has become what medical ethics courses describe in hypotheticals. She is choosing who lives. This scene follows her through the morning review, the faces attached to names, the impossible arithmetic of care when resources are finite.

## Key Elements

- The triage list: handwritten names, medication needs, supplies remaining
- Elena as moral actor: making decisions that will result in deaths
- One week of crisis: the clinic transformed, procedures established
- Specific patients: names and faces, histories Elena knows
- The arithmetic of care: who needs it most, who can survive without
- Medical ethics made material: this is what shortage means
- Knausgaard exhaustive detail for the list, the process, the weight
- The specific burden of knowing the people you're rationing

## Stylistic Notes

Full Knausgaard: the physical details of the list, the handwriting, the specific names. Each patient should feel real - brief but vivid details that give them individuality. The moral weight carried through specific imagery rather than abstract reflection. Elena's internal state rendered through her attention to the list, not through explicit anguish. The exhaustion of a week of this work present in sentence rhythm: sentences that start with purpose and trail into weariness.
