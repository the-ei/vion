# Scene Plan: Daniel's Return

## Summary

Mid-morning. Elena looks up from a patient and sees her husband in the doorway. Daniel has made it back from the construction site three hours away - it took him four days, walking the last forty miles when the fuel ran out. He's thinner, sunburned, exhausted, changed. They embrace in the clinic hallway, the first time they've touched in over a week. The reunion is brief because the clinic is drowning, but it is real: they are both alive, the children are safe with Abuela. Daniel doesn't wait to rest; he starts helping where he can - carrying supplies, organizing intake, doing the physical work that Elena's depleted staff can no longer manage. They cannot process what has happened, not yet. They can only work.

## Key Elements

- Daniel's arrival: the physical shock of seeing him
- Forty miles on foot: what it took to get home
- Brief reunion: the embrace, the relief, then back to work
- Daniel changed: thinner, sunburned, something in his eyes
- Immediate action: Daniel stepping into helping
- The children confirmed safe: Abuela holding things together
- Marriage under crisis: no time for words, only presence
- Carson compression for the reunion; body language over dialogue

## Stylistic Notes

Shorter scene - the brevity is the point. They don't have time for reunion. The embrace rendered in Knausgaard physical detail: the specific feel of his body, how he smells, the way his hands have changed. Then Carson compression: they separate, they work, the conversation about his journey happens in fragments between tasks. Daniel's account of the forty miles should be spare - he doesn't have words for it yet. The scene's power is in what isn't said: the marriage that works because both people understand priorities.
