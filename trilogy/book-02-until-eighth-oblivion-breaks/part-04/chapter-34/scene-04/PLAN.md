# Scene Plan: The Signal

## Summary

Near midnight, a working radio crackles with news. Someone had a solar-charged battery; someone else had a shortwave receiver. The signal is fragmented but clear enough: partial grid restoration in some areas, government coordination finally functioning, the acute phase ending. Other cities are coming back online. Emergency services are reestablishing. Help is coming - though what "help" means, after everything, is uncertain. The news doesn't feel like rescue. It feels like transition. The question that crystallizes: back to what existed before, or toward something new?

## Key Elements

- The radio signal: solar-charged, fragmented but clear
- Partial grid restoration: the acute crisis ending
- Government coordination returning: the institutions functioning again
- Not rescue but transition: the crisis ending differently than expected
- The question: back to before, or toward something new?
- What "help" means: uncertain, possibly unwanted
- The network's response: will this dissolve what they've built?
- Carson fragments for the radio news

## Stylistic Notes

The radio news should be rendered as fragments: incomplete sentences, static between phrases, the specific limited-bandwidth quality of emergency broadcasts. The community's reaction is not celebration but something more complex - relief mixed with uncertainty, even reluctance. Elena's internal response: what does "return to normal" mean when normal was the problem? Carson for the news fragments; Knausgaard for the community's processing.
