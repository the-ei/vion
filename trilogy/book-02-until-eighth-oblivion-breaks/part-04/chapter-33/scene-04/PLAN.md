# Scene Plan: Amina's Promise

## Summary

Yusuf tells his sister she must care for their mother while he's gone. Amina is sixteen. She has already been doing more than any sixteen-year-old should do. Now Yusuf is asking her to do the impossible: keep their mother alive, alone, for however long it takes him to reach the enclave and return with help. Amina doesn't argue. She doesn't cry. She accepts. The siblings' conversation is brief, enormous, inadequate. Yusuf tries to give her instructions, but what instructions can cover this? They hold each other for a moment. Then Yusuf packs what little he can carry and prepares to leave with Nathaniel. The scene ends with Amina watching him go.

## Key Elements

- The impossible ask: care for a dying mother alone at sixteen
- Amina's strength: she accepts without argument
- The siblings' bond: what they don't say matters
- Inadequate instructions: there is no guide for this
- The embrace: the only thing that can be given
- Yusuf packing: what do you carry to find salvation?
- Amina watching him go: the image that will haunt
- Carson compression for the conversation; every word weighted

## Stylistic Notes

Dialogue minimal but essential. What the siblings say to each other should be simple, almost formal - they are beyond casual conversation. The embrace described in Knausgaard physical detail. Amina's face, her hands, the way she stands straight despite everything. Habiba's awareness that she is the reason her son must leave and her daughter must suffer - a moment of her perspective, briefly. End on the image of Amina at the window, watching Yusuf walk away with Nathaniel.
