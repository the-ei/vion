# Scene Plan: Counting Breaths

## Summary

Yusuf Hassan sits with his mother in their Minneapolis apartment, watching her breathe. Habiba's kidney failure is progressing; without dialysis, her body is poisoning itself. He counts her breaths the way he counted days of medication. Each breath is one more she has. Each breath is one closer to the last. The scene is quiet, intimate, terrible. Yusuf has not slept. He has been working to find solutions, reaching out to Nathaniel Burke's contacts, following rumors of functioning medical facilities. But right now, in the early morning of Day 10, he sits with his mother and watches her breathe. The scene establishes the stakes in the most immediate terms: this woman will die without help.

## Key Elements

- Counting breaths: the physical reality of kidney failure
- Habiba's deteriorating condition: the body without dialysis
- Yusuf's sleeplessness: days of searching, no solution yet
- The intimacy of watching death approach
- The mother-son relationship: what they have meant to each other
- The apartment as confinement: nowhere to go, no one to help
- Knausgaard's attention to the body, the specific symptoms
- The helplessness made physical in the count

## Stylistic Notes

Quiet Knausgaard: long, slow sentences matching the vigil's rhythm. The physical details of kidney failure rendered clinically but lovingly: Yusuf knows what the symptoms mean. Habiba's breathing as structural element - it should recur throughout the scene, each description slightly different. The silence of the apartment is part of the texture. Very little dialogue; what can be said? Carson compression only for the moments when Yusuf's grief surfaces unexpectedly.
