# Scene Plan: Seventeen Percent

## Summary

Yusuf Hassan is mid-delivery when everything stops. He's riding his electric scooter through Minneapolis, a package for someone who ordered something that seemed urgent enough to pay for rush delivery, when his phone goes blank. The delivery app crashes. The scooter's GPS navigation vanishes. He checks his battery: seventeen percent. This number becomes everything - it is distance, it is time, it is whether he can get home. He is somewhere in the suburbs, unfamiliar territory, holding a package for an address he can no longer see. His first thought is his mother, who needs her dialysis medication refilled today. His second thought is Amina, alone at school. His third thought is the seventeen percent blinking on his scooter display. The scene captures the specific terror of total app dependency revealed in its absence.

## Key Elements

- The moment of failure: mid-delivery, mid-motion, suddenly alone
- Seventeen percent battery: the number that becomes fate
- Geographic disorientation: suburbs without GPS, no way to navigate
- Immediate family fear: mother's medication, sister at school
- The undelivered package: absurd artifact of a world that just ended
- Gig economy's total dependence revealed: Yusuf has nothing without the app
- The scooter as both lifeline and trap: mobility dependent on battery
- Carson compression for the crisis moment; visceral, immediate

## Characters

- **Yusuf Hassan (POV):** Gig worker stranded mid-delivery
- **Habiba Hassan (distant):** His mother, needing medication, now unreachable
- **Amina Hassan (distant):** His sister, at school, now unreachable

- **The scooter:** Display showing seventeen percent, navigation gone
- **The phone:** Blank screen, unresponsive

## Stylistic Notes

Carson mode dominates: short sentences, high tension, the present moment stretched. The number seventeen percent should recur like a pulse. Yusuf's internal calculus rendered as near-math: distance, speed, time, charge. Fragments of thoughts about family intercut with practical survival assessment. The undelivered package as absurd object - Yusuf considers just dropping it, but his work reflexes persist even as the work itself has ended.
