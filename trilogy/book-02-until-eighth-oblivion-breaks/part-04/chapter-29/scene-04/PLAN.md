# Scene Plan: Habiba's Medication

## Summary

Yusuf reaches home to find his mother stable but her medication supply finite. Habiba's dialysis requires regular prescriptions; she was due for a refill today. Amina made it home from school - the building went into lockdown, then released students to walk home, and she walked three miles because the buses weren't running. She is shaken but composed, already taking care of their mother. Yusuf checks the medication: three days, maybe four if they're careful with dosing. The pharmacy's systems are down. The hospital, when Yusuf walks there, is overwhelmed with emergencies and has no capacity for chronic care. The timeline becomes concrete. They have days, not weeks.

## Key Elements

- Reunion with family: relief and then immediate new fear
- Habiba's dialysis needs: chronic care requiring infrastructure
- Amina's strength: sixteen, walked three miles, caring for mother
- Medication count: three to four days, maybe
- The pharmacy: systems down, staff unable to help
- The hospital: overwhelmed, no capacity for non-emergency
- The timeline crystallized: days, not weeks
- The body as clock: kidney failure doesn't wait for systems to recover

## Stylistic Notes

The reunion should be warm but brief - Yusuf and Amina don't have time for emotion. Practical concerns take over. The medication count rendered with clinical precision: pill bottles, dosage requirements, days remaining. The pharmacy and hospital visits are Carson-compressed: short, frustrating, futile. The scene ends with the family together but the timeline ticking. Knausgaard for Yusuf's internal experience of watching his mother and calculating how long she can survive.
