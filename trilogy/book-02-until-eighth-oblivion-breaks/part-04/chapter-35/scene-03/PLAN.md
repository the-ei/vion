# Scene Plan: DeShawn at the Door (Jerome)

## Summary

A knock at the apartment door. Jerome opens it and sees his son. DeShawn is alive. He evacuated early when he saw the cascade beginning - his engineering knowledge let him recognize what was happening before it fully manifested. He got out, made his way across states, arrived home on foot. The relief that overwhelms everything: DeShawn is alive, is here, is touching his father's arms. But later - not now, but in days to come - there will be conversations. DeShawn knew something. He saw the failure patterns. He tried to warn people internally and was dismissed. Now he carries knowledge about what his company did, and didn't do, and could have done. Jerome's journalism may finally have a subject he can't bear to investigate.

## Key Elements

- The knock: the moment before knowledge
- DeShawn alive: the overwhelming relief
- Physical reunion: father and son touching
- Early evacuation: DeShawn saw it coming
- Engineering knowledge: he recognized the cascade
- Future conversations (foreshadowed): what DeShawn knew
- The company's culpability: what didn't happen that should have
- The subject Jerome can't investigate: his son's knowledge

## Stylistic Notes

The reunion should be physically rendered: the door opening, DeShawn's face, the embrace. Knausgaard for the specific sensation of holding your child who was maybe dead. Brief account of DeShawn's journey - spare, factual, focused on movement. The foreshadowing of harder conversations should be present but not dominant; this scene is about relief. Jerome notices that DeShawn carries something heavy, but doesn't push. Not yet. The chapter's work is return, not reckoning.
