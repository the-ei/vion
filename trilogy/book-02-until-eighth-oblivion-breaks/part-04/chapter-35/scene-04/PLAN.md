# Scene Plan: The Story Worth Telling (Delphine)

## Summary

Delphine reunites with Jessie and Theo. They drove back from Oregon when the roads reopened, when the news said the acute crisis was ending. The family is together again: Theo running into Delphine's arms, Jessie's face worn but present, the house that still stands. In the days since she sent them away, Delphine has been broadcasting what she could: honest accounts of mutual aid, stories of communities self-organizing, the truth about who helped and who abandoned. Now, with her family home, she knows what she'll do next. A documentary. Not about the crisis as spectacle, but about the crisis as revelation - what it showed about connection, abandonment, possibility. The story worth telling.

## Key Elements

- The reunion: Jessie and Theo returning from Oregon
- Theo running: the child's body in her arms
- Jessie's face: worn, grateful, home
- The house still standing: survival's simple gift
- Delphine's broadcasts: what she did while they were gone
- Honest accounts: mutual aid, communities, truth
- The documentary project: what comes next
- The story worth telling: not spectacle but revelation

## Stylistic Notes

Warm Knausgaard reunion: Theo's weight in Delphine's arms, the specific way Jessie looks. Don't rush the physical reunion; let it breathe. Then pivot to Delphine's clarity about what comes next - the documentary project described in its shape, not its details. The scene should feel like both ending and beginning: the crisis resolving, the work starting. Carson for Delphine's vision of the project - fragments of what she wants to capture.
