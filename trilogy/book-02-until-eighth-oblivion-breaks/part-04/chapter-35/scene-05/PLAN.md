# Scene Plan: Voices from the Break (Documentary Interlude)

## Summary

A formal experiment: the chapter's final scene is not a POV narrative but a compilation of voices. Short fragments from across the crisis, multiple unnamed speakers, a collective processing of what happened. A mother describing the moment the lights came back. A gig worker describing the informal network that formed. A nurse describing what she had to choose. A child asking when the iPad will work. A radio operator describing the silence before the signals returned. The voices compile into something larger than any individual perspective: the break as lived experience, processed not yet into history but into shared memory. The reader is positioned as documentary audience. The characters' individual stories set in the context of the whole.

## Key Elements

- Formal experiment: voices rather than POV narrative
- Multiple unnamed speakers: mother, gig worker, nurse, child, radio operator
- Fragments of experience: specific, vivid, brief
- Collective processing: the crisis becoming shared memory
- Carson-style compression: every fragment weighted
- Documentary positioning: the reader as audience
- The break as lived experience: not yet history
- The characters' stories in context: individual within collective

## Stylistic Notes

Typographic experiment: each voice a short paragraph, no attribution beyond the briefest identifier ("A mother in Phoenix:"). Carson at fullest compression: every word essential. Some voices should echo scenes we've witnessed; others should expand the story to people we haven't followed. End the chapter and Part 4 on a voice that captures the break's meaning without stating it - something specific and small that contains everything. Leave white space between fragments.
