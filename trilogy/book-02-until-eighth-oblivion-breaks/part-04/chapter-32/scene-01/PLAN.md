# Scene Plan: The Hub

## Summary

Day 10 of the crisis. Jerome Washington is at a community center in Baltimore that has become an information hub - one of the only places where news still aggregates. Ham radio operators have set up stations, returning travelers share what they've seen, printed bulletins are posted on the walls, and a handful of working internet nodes provide fragments of the larger picture. Jerome sits in the corner with his legal pad, doing what he knows how to do: gathering information, cross-referencing accounts, building a picture. But he cannot publish. He cannot verify. He is a journalist without an outlet, collecting facts that may never reach anyone beyond this room. The scene establishes the hub as a space of both hope and despair.

## Key Elements

- The community center as information hub: ham radio, printed bulletins, travelers' reports
- Jerome in journalist mode: gathering, cross-referencing, analyzing
- The impotence of journalism now: he can learn but not transmit
- The hub's population: volunteers, refugees, information-seekers
- The patchwork information environment: fragments assembling into picture
- Baltimore geography: the specific community center, its history
- Knausgaard detail for the hub's operations; the specific equipment, procedures
- The journalist's reflex persisting without outlet

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Journalist working without a platform
- **Ham radio operators:** Maintaining the communication links
- **Returning travelers:** People who have seen other places
- **Information hub volunteers:** Ordinary citizens organizing knowledge
- **Other information-seekers:** People trying to understand what's happening

## Setting

- **Community center, Baltimore:** The hub's physical space
- **The information environment:** Radio crackling, bulletins posted, conversations
- **Baltimore beyond:** The city in crisis, glimpsed through reports

## Timeline

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard attention to the hub's specific details: the equipment, the volunteers' routines, the physical arrangement of the space. The information fragments can appear as typographic elements: radio transcripts, bulletin text, overheard dialogue. Jerome's professional mode is both comfort and prison - he knows how to do this, but it may be pointless. The scene should feel industrious but uncertain: people working hard to know things that may not help.
