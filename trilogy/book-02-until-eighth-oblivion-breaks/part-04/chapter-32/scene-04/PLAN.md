# Scene Plan: The Spiral

## Summary

Jerome's psychological breaking. Walking home from the hub through darkening Baltimore streets, he is caught in a recursive loop of thought he cannot escape. His journalism warned of this. His son was inside it. Everything he knew was useless. The thoughts circle: blame, guilt, helplessness, rage, grief. He argued with DeShawn about the tech industry; DeShawn didn't listen. Was that rebellion, or was Jerome wrong to push? The company that may have caused this employed his son. Does that make DeShawn complicit? Does it make Jerome responsible for not preventing it? The spiral has no bottom. He walks until he doesn't know where he is, then stands in the dark, lost in his own city.

## Key Elements

- The psychological spiral: recursive, inescapable thoughts
- Walking as failed escape: moving physically, stuck mentally
- The blame loop: self, son, industry, systems
- Baltimore streets: the familiar become strange in crisis and darkness
- Jerome lost: literally and metaphorically
- The impotence of knowing: being right changed nothing
- Full Knausgaard interiority: the specific texture of breakdown
- The body walking while the mind circles

## Stylistic Notes

Peak Knausgaard: the stream of consciousness that cannot stop. Long sentences that circle back on themselves. Thoughts repeating with slight variations, the way anxiety actually works. External reality (the streets, the city) only intermittently present - Jerome keeps emerging from thought to find himself somewhere he doesn't recognize. Carson elements for the intrusions of memory: short, sharp fragments from the past. The scene should feel like drowning in thought.
