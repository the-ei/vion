# Scene Plan: Denise's Hands

## Summary

Denise finds him. She has been searching for hours, asking neighbors, tracing his possible routes from the hub. She finds him standing on a corner three blocks from their building, staring at nothing. She doesn't ask what happened. She doesn't offer optimism or argument. She takes his hands. The physical contact breaks the spiral - something outside his mind, something real. They walk home together, her hand in his. At home, she sits with him in the dark. She says: we cannot know about DeShawn. We can only survive and wait. The chapter ends in darkness, but a darkness shared. Jerome is not better, but he is not alone.

## Key Elements

- Denise searching: her practical love in action
- Finding Jerome: the corner, the staring, the lostness
- Touch as rescue: her hands breaking the spiral
- Walking home together: physical accompaniment
- Sitting in the dark: no light, no false comfort
- "We can only survive and wait": Denise's wisdom
- Shared darkness: not hope, but presence
- Marriage as anchor: the chapter's resolution through connection

## Stylistic Notes

Shorter scene after the long spiral - the brevity is part of the healing. Denise's approach should be rendered simply, without drama: she finds him, she takes his hands. Physical description matters: the warmth of her hands, the texture of walking together. Little dialogue - what can be said? Carson's compression for the few words that matter. End in darkness but shared darkness: an image that carries hope without claiming it. The chapter's resolution is not resolution - it's accompaniment.
