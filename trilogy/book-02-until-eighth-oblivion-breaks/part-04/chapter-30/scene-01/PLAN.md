# Scene Plan: The Satellite Window

## Summary

Day 4 of the crisis. Delphine Okafor-Barnes is in her media company's office, one of the few places in Los Angeles still partially operational. They have generators. They have a satellite uplink that works in windows - forty-five minutes at a time, twice a day. They have what everyone desperately wants: a platform. The scene opens with Delphine watching the uplink timer count down, knowing that in forty-five minutes she has to decide what to broadcast to whatever audience can receive it. Her team is exhausted, running on caffeine and adrenaline. Her boss Lawrence Kim sees this as their moment. Delphine sees it as their test. What story do they tell when story might be all that's holding anything together?

## Key Elements

- The satellite window: limited uplink time, forced editorial choices
- Technical constraints as moral constraints: what fits in 45 minutes?
- Delphine's media company still functioning: generators, dedicated staff
- The weight of being heard when few can speak
- Lawrence Kim's opportunism: he sees ratings, legacy, influence
- Delphine's ethical hesitation: what does responsible broadcasting look like now?
- LA as media capital in crisis: the industry that shaped narrative now tested
- Carson compression for the countdown; Knausgaard for Delphine's internal debate
