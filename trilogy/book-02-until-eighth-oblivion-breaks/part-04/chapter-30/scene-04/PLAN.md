# Scene Plan: Lawrence's Opportunity

## Summary

The next morning. Delphine returns to the office for the second satellite window. Lawrence Kim is waiting with a vision: they will become THE voice of the crisis. He has graphics prepared, a naming convention ("The Collapse Report"), a plan for serialized coverage that will make them the definitive source when systems recover. His analysis is brilliant - he understands audience psychology, information scarcity, the hunger for narrative. And his plan is ethically compromised: it prioritizes attention capture over truth, fear over calm, their brand over public good. Delphine recognizes the playbook because she's run versions of it her whole career. Lawrence wants her to lead the content strategy. She must decide whether to follow or resist.

## Key Elements

- Lawrence's vision: THE voice of the crisis, brand dominance
- Commercial brilliance, ethical compromise: he's right about what will work
- "The Collapse Report": branded disaster coverage
- Delphine recognizing the playbook: she's done this before
- The offer: lead content strategy, significant role
- The choice crystallizing: serve the brand or serve the public
- Lawrence as antagonist but not villain: he believes this is good work
- Delphine's career at a crossroads

## Characters

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes (POV):** Facing the choice her career has led to
- **Lawrence Kim:** Boss, visionary, ethically compromised but not evil
- **Production team (background):** Watching, waiting for direction

## Setting

- **Media company office:** The same space, the satellite timer counting again
- **Conference room or Lawrence's office:** Where the vision is presented

## Timeline

4 pages (~1,100 words)

## Stylistic Notes

Lawrence should be compelling, not cartoonishly villainous. His pitch rendered with its genuine appeal - Delphine (and the reader) should feel the pull of what he's offering. Knausgaard psychological depth for Delphine's internal response: the part of her that wants to say yes, the part that recognizes the corruption. Dialogue carries weight here - Lawrence's words should be quotable, memorable, seductive. The scene is a temptation narrative.
