# Scene Plan: Theo's Question

## Summary

Delphine goes home for a few hours - the satellite window won't reopen until tomorrow morning. Jessie and Theo are in their Silverlake house, which still has power through some quirk of the grid. Theo, four years old, asks when the iPad will work again. He doesn't understand crisis, collapse, systemic failure. He understands that his favorite game won't load. Delphine and Jessie talk around him about what to do. Jessie's parents have a rural property in Oregon; they could evacuate, get out of LA before things get worse. But Delphine feels pulled to stay, to use the platform she has. The conversation is their marriage under pressure: two competent women with different risk assessments, a child who doesn't understand the stakes.

## Key Elements

- Theo's question: the child's perspective, the iPad as synecdoche
- Domestic intimacy during crisis: the home as shelter and trap
- Jessie's evacuation plan: rural Oregon, her parents' property
- Delphine's pull to stay: professional responsibility vs. family safety
- Marriage under pressure: two valid positions, no right answer
- The child who doesn't understand: Theo's needs as clarifying lens
- Parenting during apocalypse: what do you tell a four-year-old?
- Knausgaard domestic detail; Carson for the impossible choices

## Stylistic Notes

Warm domestic Knausgaard: the specific details of their home, Theo's mannerisms, the married couple's shorthand. The tension should be loving, not hostile - these are two people who agree on values and disagree on tactics. Theo's presence keeps the abstract stakes concrete: whatever they decide affects this small person who cannot consent. Carson compression for the unanswerable question - "When will the iPad work again?" becomes something neither parent can answer.
