# Scene Plan: What Story to Tell

## Summary

Delphine makes her choice. She will broadcast, but not Lawrence's version. In the remaining satellite window, she crafts something different: a message that acknowledges uncertainty rather than pretending to know. That foregrounds mutual aid rather than fear. That names the information chaos without claiming to have escaped it. She speaks directly to whatever audience can receive: we don't know everything, but here is what we do know; here is what might help; here is how to find each other. It's not dramatic. It won't go viral. It's true. After the broadcast, she tells Jessie to take Theo to Oregon. She will stay, use whatever windows remain, tell what stories she can. The family separates. Delphine watches them go.

## Key Elements

- The broadcast Delphine crafts: honest, humble, helpful
- Rejecting Lawrence's vision: the story that serves vs. the story that captures
- Uncertainty acknowledged: not pretending to have answers
- Mutual aid foregrounded: practical information, connection resources
- The audience unknown: whoever can receive this, wherever they are
- Sending Jessie and Theo to safety: the personal sacrifice
- Family separation: watching them drive away
- Carson's elliptical register for the broadcast; Knausgaard for the farewell

## Characters

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes (POV):** Making her choice, paying the cost
- **Jessie Barnes:** Accepting the plan, taking Theo to safety
- **Theo Okafor-Barnes:** In the car, not fully understanding
- **Lawrence Kim (implied):** His reaction to Delphine's choice off-screen
- **The audience:** Unknown, receiving whatever reaches them

## Setting

- **The parking lot or front of the house:** Where she watches Jessie and Theo leave
- **The road to Oregon (implied):** Where her family is going

## Stylistic Notes

Two modes in this scene. The broadcast itself could appear as typescript, formatted differently, Delphine's voice rendered with Carson's compression - the careful words that have to carry so much. The farewell to Jessie and Theo in full Knausgaard warmth: the specific details of the goodbye, the car driving away, the emptiness after. End on Delphine alone in the city, having chosen work that matters over safety. The choice is not heroic, not tragic - just chosen.
