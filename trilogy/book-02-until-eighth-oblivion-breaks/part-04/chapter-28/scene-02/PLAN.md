# Scene Plan: The Empty Feed

## Summary

Jerome's professional instincts take over: if something is happening, he needs to document it. But his tools are gone. He cannot check sources. He cannot verify reports. He cannot publish. He cannot even record voice memos to his phone - it won't save without cloud backup. He finds a legal pad and starts taking notes by hand - the radio reports, his observations, the timeline. He tries to structure what he knows, but what he knows is almost nothing. For the first time in decades, Jerome Washington, Pulitzer-winning investigative journalist, has no more information than anyone else on the street. Less, perhaps, because his neighbors might be talking to each other while he's trapped in professional reflexes that no longer function. The scene tracks his psychological response to sudden information deprivation.

## Key Elements

- Professional identity crisis: Jerome's skills rendered useless
- The attempt to document: handwritten notes as regression to earlier journalism
- Information deprivation as almost physical pain for someone like Jerome
- The legal pad: inadequate tool for a digital professional
- What the radio reports: fragmentary, contradictory, unhelpful
- Jerome's recursive analysis: he can't stop trying to understand
- The gap between the man who predicted this and the man experiencing it
- Knausgaard psychological interiority: the texture of Jerome's frustration and fear

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Journalist whose tools have vanished
- **Denise Washington:** Moving through the apartment, preparing for a day that may not happen normally
- **Radio voices:** The only external information, unsatisfying and incomplete

## Setting

- **Baltimore apartment:** Jerome at the kitchen table with legal pad and radio
- **The legal pad:** Handwritten notes, a return to pre-digital journalism
- **The silent devices:** Phones, laptops, tablets - all dead weight

## Stylistic Notes

Deep Knausgaard interiority: Jerome's thoughts circling, unable to find purchase. The specific frustration of an expert rendered helpless. Long sentences that follow his recursive attempts to make sense of fragmentary data. Carson elements in the radio fragments themselves - quoted in short bursts that don't cohere. The legal pad notes could appear as typographic elements: handwritten-style fragments within the prose.
