# Scene Plan: Maya Patterson's Oxygen

## Summary

Jerome goes to check on Maya Patterson, the elderly widow three floors up. Her apartment door is open; she's called out for help, and another neighbor has propped it open but left. Mrs. Patterson's oxygen concentrator requires electricity. The building still has power - the grid is holding here, for now - but she's terrified. What if the power goes? Jerome sits with her. He listens to her talk about her late husband, about the neighborhood, about how things have changed. He helps her locate her backup oxygen tanks. He calls Denise to bring food. For the first time since the crisis began, Jerome is not trying to understand or document or analyze. He is simply present with another human being who needs him. The scene ends with Jerome choosing: he will not try to report this crisis. He will try to survive it. The journalist puts down his legal pad.

## Key Elements

- Mrs. Patterson's oxygen dependency: concrete stakes, not abstract systems
- Jerome's shift from analyst to helper: the chapter's central transformation
- The simple act of presence: sitting with someone afraid
- Her stories: the past as anchor, Knausgaard attention to memory
- The backup oxygen tanks: practical problem-solving
- Denise bringing food: the marriage working as partnership
- The choice: stop documenting, start surviving
- The legal pad put down: symbolic action

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Making the choice to stop being a journalist and start being a neighbor
- **Maya Patterson:** Elderly widow, depends on oxygen, afraid and alone
- **Denise Washington:** Bringing food, completing the caregiving circuit
- **The absent neighbor:** Who opened the door but couldn't stay - the limits of community

## Setting

- **Maya Patterson's apartment:** Small, full of history, the oxygen concentrator humming
- **The building's hallway:** Jerome walking up three flights
- **Baltimore evening:** The city outside, unknowable now

## Stylistic Notes

The prose should settle and warm. Mrs. Patterson's stories told in Knausgaard's long, flowing sentences - the past as refuge. Jerome listening, truly listening, is rendered not as dialogue but as attention. The choice to stop documenting could be understated: Jerome puts the legal pad in a drawer. The scene ends quietly, with darkness outside and the oxygen concentrator humming - a machine that still works, a life that still continues.
