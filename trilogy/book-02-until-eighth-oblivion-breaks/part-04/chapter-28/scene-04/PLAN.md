# Scene Plan: The Son Who Isn't There

## Summary

Jerome tries to reach DeShawn. His son works at a tech startup that interfaces with the very systems now failing - a company Jerome has criticized publicly, a career choice that has strained their relationship. The phone won't connect. Text messages fail. Jerome knows, rationally, that network congestion means nothing about DeShawn's safety. But he also knows that DeShawn is inside the very systems that have just broken. His son, who admired the tech world Jerome distrusted. His son, who took a job his father saw as compromised. His son, who might know things about this crisis, or might be caught in it, or might be complicit in it. The scene traces Jerome's spiraling fear and the history of the father-son rift that makes this silence unbearable.

## Key Elements

- Failed attempts to reach DeShawn: the silence that could mean anything
- Father-son relationship history: the tech divide between them
- Jerome's ambivalence about DeShawn's career: criticism now mixed with terror
- The company: Kevin Zhou's startup, mentioned in news clips Jerome has seen
- The possibility of complicity: what if DeShawn knew something?
- The possibility of danger: what if DeShawn is inside the failure?
- Memory fragments: moments of connection and disconnection with his son
- Knausgaard psychological depth: the specific texture of parental fear

## Characters

- **DeShawn Washington (absent):** The son who represents everything Jerome fears about tech
- **Denise Washington:** Sharing Jerome's fear, less able to analyze it away
- **Kevin Zhou (mentioned):** DeShawn works at his startup; Jerome has seen news coverage

## Setting

- **Memory:** Flashbacks to Jerome and DeShawn's conversations about tech, career, values
- **The startup (imagined):** Jerome's mental picture of where his son might be

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard at fullest expression: the recursive worry, the memories that intrude, the specific quality of parental terror. The prose should spiral, returning to the same fears from different angles. Carson fragments for attempted contacts - the failed texts, the dropped calls, the silence. Memory sections can be more flowing, past tense, warmer in tone - the relationship that existed before the rift. The contrast between remembered connection and present silence.
