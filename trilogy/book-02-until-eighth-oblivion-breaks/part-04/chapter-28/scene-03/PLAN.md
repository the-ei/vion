# Scene Plan: Denise's Pragmatism

## Summary

Denise Washington returns from the cancelled school - the automated system failed mid-notification, teachers learned of closure through text chains that barely got through. She finds Jerome at the kitchen table, surrounded by legal pad notes, radio crackling. She doesn't ask what's happening; she starts making lists. Food in the house. Water stored. Medication levels. The elderly neighbor, Maya Patterson, who depends on oxygen equipment. Jerome watches his wife shift into practical mode and feels both grateful and shamed - he's been trying to understand the crisis while she's been preparing to survive it. The tension between his need to know and her need to act crystallizes. She asks him to check on Mrs. Patterson. The ask is both practical and pointed: do something useful.

## Key Elements

- Denise as grounding presence: practical vs. Jerome's abstract
- The cancelled school: another system failure, absorbed into domestic response
- The list-making: supplies, needs, vulnerabilities
- The neighbor Mrs. Patterson: concrete responsibility vs. abstract journalism
- Marriage under stress: different response modes creating friction
- Denise's competence: high school history teacher accustomed to managing crises
- Jerome's shame: recognizing his response has been all analysis, no action
- The ask: check on Mrs. Patterson - first movement toward helping

## Stylistic Notes

The prose settles slightly as Denise enters - she brings structure. Her dialogue should be direct, short, action-oriented. Jerome's internal response remains Knausgaard-rich: the specific texture of feeling inadequate, the self-criticism that is also deflection. The scene pivots from abstract crisis to concrete need when Mrs. Patterson is mentioned. Carson compression for Denise's lists - they could appear as formatted elements.
