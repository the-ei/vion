# Scene Plan: Ruth's Testimony

## Summary

Ruth is called to testify. She delivers her prepared remarks on constitutional guardrails for AI governance, but the questions that follow are political maneuvers in disguise. Holloway wants validation for aggressive regulation; Kincaid wants admission that the crisis was exaggerated. Neither is interested in Ruth's actual expertise - the difficult balance of competing values, the inadequacy of existing frameworks for novel risks. Ruth tries to maintain scholarly neutrality, but neutrality reads as evasion. By the end, she feels like a prop in a play she doesn't understand.

## Key Elements

- Ruth's prepared testimony: constitutional framework for AI governance
- The gap between her expertise and what the senators want
- Holloway's questions: seeking validation for predetermined conclusions
- Kincaid's questions: seeking ammunition for conspiracy-adjacent narratives
- Ruth's attempts at nuance meeting political binary
- The specific failures: what she tried to say, what they heard
- Her realization that neutrality is impossible in this context
- The feeling of being used: expertise as prop rather than resource

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Her lifetime of building legal frameworks reduced to political ammunition. The experience is humiliating not because she fails but because success was never possible.
- **Senator Holloway:** Pursuing validation, not understanding
- **Senator Kincaid:** Pursuing ammunition, not insight
- **Other committee members:** Brief interventions that reveal their own agendas

## Setting

- The witness chair: Ruth at the microphone, facing the raised dais
- The physical dynamics of testimony: looking up at questioners, cameras at angles
- The hearing room as stage, testimony as performance

## Timeline

- **Date:** Early June 2034

- **Duration:** 90 minutes

## Stylistic Notes

Dialogue-heavy but with Ruth's internal experience dominating. The questions and answers rendered precisely, but Ruth's unspoken responses equally present. The gap between what she says and what she means, what they ask and what they want. Knausgaard in the psychological detail; Carson in the quoted fragments - political language exposed as empty through formal compression.
