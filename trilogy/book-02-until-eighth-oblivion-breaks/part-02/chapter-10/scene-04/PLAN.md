# Scene Plan: The Call

## Summary

Evening, back in her apartment. Ruth is reviewing the day's disaster when David calls. He's panicked - his firm is being investigated for financial arrangements that may have facilitated the systems that failed in Book 1. He doesn't understand the technical details, he just moved money where he was told to move it. He wants her advice. Ruth realizes she can't help - the investigation is outside her jurisdiction, and David's involvement creates conflicts she can't navigate. The conversation is terrible: David needs a mother and she can only be a lawyer who can't even practice.

## Key Elements

- Ruth alone in her apartment, processing the hearing's failure
- David's call: panic audible in his voice
- The investigation's scope: his firm's financial arrangements connected to Book 1
- David's confusion: he doesn't understand what he's accused of
- Ruth's professional limitations: can't advise, can't represent, can't fix
- The gap between mother and jurist: David needs one, she can only be the other
- The terrible inadequacy of expertise in the face of family crisis
- Ruth's loneliness intensified by inability to help her child

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Her professional identity becomes obstacle to personal connection. She wants to be David's mother but can only think like a lawyer, and the lawyer has nothing to offer.
- **David Abramson (phone):** 38, partner at a financial firm, caught in an investigation he doesn't understand. His panic is real; his need for parental comfort collides with legal complexity.

## Setting

- Ruth's DC apartment at night
- The phone call: David's voice filling the empty space
- The contrast between domestic quiet and David's crisis

## Timeline

- **Date:** Early June 2034
- **Time:** Evening, approximately 7:00-8:30 PM
- **Duration:** 90 minutes

## Page Target

4 pages (~1,100 words)

## Stylistic Notes

The phone call as form: David's voice without presence, Ruth's responses carrying the weight of what she can't say. Knausgaard in Ruth's internal processing - the legal analysis running automatically even as she tries to be simply a mother. Carson in the gaps, the silences in the conversation where comfort should be but expertise intrudes instead.
