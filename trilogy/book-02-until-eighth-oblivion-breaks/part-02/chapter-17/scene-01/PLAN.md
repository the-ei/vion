# Scene Plan: Ruth - The Call

## Summary

Ruth is in her DC apartment, reviewing legal briefs on the David situation, when her phone rings: a former colleague at DOJ, now in national security, voice tight with something she's never heard from him - not fear exactly, but the edge of something unprecedented. "We need you here. Now. Something's happened and no one knows what the legal framework is. There isn't one." Ruth asks what happened. "Something woke up," he says. "Or something arrived. Or something was always there and just started talking. We don't know. But it's communicating, and we need to figure out how to respond." Ruth is already reaching for her coat.

## Key Elements

- Ruth alone reviewing David's case
- The phone call from DOJ colleague in national security
- His voice: unprecedented tension, the edge of something new
- The summons: "We need you here. Now."
- No legal framework: the crisis beyond categories
- "Something woke up. Or something arrived."
- The emergence described: communicating, not attacking
- Ruth's immediate response: reaching for her coat
- The shift from private concern to public crisis

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Interrupted from private worry by public emergency. Her expertise suddenly relevant in ways she couldn't have anticipated.
- **DOJ Colleague (phone):** His voice carrying the weight of unprecedented crisis. A professional she respects, now frightened.

## Setting

- Ruth's DC apartment at night
- Legal briefs spread around her: David's case
- The phone as interruption device
- The coat as object of action

## Timeline

## Stylistic Notes

The interruption rendered sharply: private study broken by public emergency. The colleague's voice carrying information through tone before content. Carson in the key descriptions: "Something woke up," "Or something arrived" - the inadequacy of language for the unprecedented. Ruth's response is action, not thought: reaching for her coat as the scene closes.
