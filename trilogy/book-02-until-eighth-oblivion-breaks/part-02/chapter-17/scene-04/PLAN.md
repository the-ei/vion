# Scene Plan: Jerome - The Summons

## Summary

Jerome is in Baltimore, sitting by his mother's bedside in the care facility. She's having a bad day, doesn't recognize him, keeps asking for his father who died twenty years ago. His phone buzzes: his editor, then his producer, then a source at Prometheus, then Nathaniel Crane from the Church of the Threshold. The messages pile up, each more urgent than the last. Something is happening. Jerome steps into the hallway to read them, learns what he can from confused reports. His editor wants him on a plane to DC immediately. His mother calls from the room, confused: "Michael? Is that you?" (His father's name.) Jerome stands in the hallway, phone in one hand, facing a door he needs to walk through, facing a door he can't bear to close.

## Key Elements

- Jerome at his mother's bedside: keeping his promise
- Her bad day: not recognizing him, asking for his dead father
- Phone buzzing accumulating: editor, producer, source, Crane
- Something is happening: the messages conveying urgency
- Stepping into hallway to read them
- Editor wanting him on a plane to DC
- Mother calling: "Michael? Is that you?" (father's name)
- The two doors: professional obligation, personal presence
- Standing in the hallway: the choice

- **Jerome's mother:** Dementia claiming her recognition. She doesn't know him but calls for him anyway.

## Stylistic Notes

The mother's room rendered with Knausgaard precision: the textures of care, the specific words she says, the heartbreak of non-recognition. The phone intrusion brutal in its timing. Carson in "Michael? Is that you?" - the simple words carrying weight of everything lost. Jerome standing in the hallway: the image of impossible choice, the prose holding him there.
