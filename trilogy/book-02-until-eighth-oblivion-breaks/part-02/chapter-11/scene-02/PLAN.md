# Scene Plan: Morning Sessions

## Summary

The retreat's sessions are deliberately provocative - designed to surface honest disagreement rather than consensus-seek. Kevin Zhou sits through presentations on AI consciousness, value alignment, capability trajectories. The accelerationists speak with religious certainty about beneficial transformation. The cautionaries respond with risk curves. The "already happened" faction makes the unsettling argument: we're already living in the Eighth Oblivion, it's just not evenly distributed. Kevin Zhou takes notes but can't find his own position among these resolved certainties.

## Key Elements

- The session structure: provocation over consensus
- Accelerationist presentations: transformation as salvation
- Cautionary responses: risk models, capability curves
- The "already happened" interpretation: present condition, not future event
- Kevin Zhou's note-taking as defense mechanism
- His inability to commit to any position
- The demographic of attendees: who speaks, who listens
- The technical language that both clarifies and obscures

## Characters

- **Kevin Zhou (POV):** Searching for a position he can live with. Each faction makes points he can't dismiss; none offers a home for his uncertainty.
- **Victor Blackwell (first glimpse):** Accelerationist true believer, brilliant, probably sociopathic. His certainty terrifies Kevin Zhou.
- **Dr. Sarah Chen-Ramirez (first glimpse):** Legendary figure, observing more than participating. Kevin Zhou notices her attention.
- **Other researchers:** The chorus of expert opinion, variously resolved

## Setting

- The retreat's main session room: comfortable chairs, ocean views, coffee stations
- Presentation screens showing graphs, diagrams, projections
- The physical arrangement: not lecture hall but salon, designed for dialogue

## Timeline

- **Date:** Mid-June 2034 (Day 2 of retreat)
- **Time:** Morning, approximately 9:00 AM-12:30 PM
- **Duration:** Three and a half hours

## Page Target

5 pages (~1,375 words)

## Stylistic Notes

Technical content rendered accessible but not dumbed down. The competing interpretations presented fairly - the reader should feel the pull of each. Kevin Zhou's interior struggle running beneath the presentations: why can't he choose? What does his uncertainty mean? Carson compression in the "already happened" argument - its strangeness requiring spare, precise language.
