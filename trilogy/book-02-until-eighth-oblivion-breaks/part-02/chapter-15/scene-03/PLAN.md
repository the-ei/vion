# Scene Plan: The Counter-Interview

## Summary

Kevin Zhou turns the conversation around - asks Jerome what he believes. Why does journalism matter if information doesn't change behavior? Why publish if people only hear what they already think? Jerome doesn't have easy answers. He talks about his son DeShawn, who is fascinated by tech, who admires people like Kevin Zhou, who might have been Kevin Zhou if he'd been born ten years later. "I write because someone might read it," Jerome says. "I can't know who. I can't control what they do with it. But the act of telling true things has to matter, or I don't know what I'm doing with my life." Kevin Zhou is moved despite himself.

## Key Elements

- Kevin Zhou turning the conversation: interviewing the interviewer
- His challenge: why does journalism matter?
- Jerome's honest struggle with the question
- DeShawn: Jerome's son who admires tech
- The generational connection: DeShawn might have been Kevin Zhou
- Jerome's answer: "the act of telling true things has to matter"
- Faith without certainty: journalism as necessary hope
- Kevin Zhou moved by Jerome's honesty

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington:** Confronting questions he can't fully answer. His faith in journalism is real but not certain.
- **DeShawn Washington (via mention):** His presence connects the older and younger generation
