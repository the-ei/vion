# Scene Plan: The Lobby

## Summary

Kevin Zhou arrives at the Fairmont, spots Jerome at a corner table. They've exchanged messages but never met in person. Jerome is older than Kevin Zhou expected, more weathered but also more present - none of the performative intensity Kevin Zhou associates with journalists. They order coffee. The preliminary exchange: how Jerome found him, what he's working on, ground rules for the interview. Kevin Zhou is guarded at first, offering the same careful non-answers he's given other journalists. But Jerome doesn't take notes, just listens, and Kevin Zhou finds the silence drawing out honesty he didn't plan to offer.

## Key Elements

- Kevin Zhou arriving: the physical approach, spotting Jerome
- First impressions: Jerome older, more present than expected
- The preliminary exchange: how they connected, ground rules
- Kevin Zhou's initial guardedness: practiced non-answers
- Jerome's method: listening without notes
- The silence drawing out honesty
- The hotel lobby as neutral territory
- Two men assessing each other

## Characters

- **Kevin Zhou (POV):** Meeting the journalist he's been warily corresponding with. His defenses encounter an unexpected approach.
- **Jerome Washington:** His interview method: presence, patience, silence. He treats Kevin Zhou as person, not source.

## Setting

- The Fairmont hotel lobby in San Francisco
- A corner table: private but public, safe for both
- Morning coffee, the rituals of meeting

## Stylistic Notes

Kevin Zhou's POV: seeing Jerome through tech-world eyes, surprised by what he finds. The dialogue sparse at first, then deepening as trust builds. The hotel lobby rendered with precision - the specific textures of neutral ground. Knausgaard in the accumulated detail of observation; Carson in the silences that do more work than words.
