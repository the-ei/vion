# Scene Plan: The Call with Rebecca

## Summary

Ruth calls her daughter from the hotel that evening. Rebecca is less sympathetic - she's always thought David made moral compromises for money, always thought the finance world was parasitic. "He's not a criminal," Ruth says. "He's complicit in systems that create criminals," Rebecca responds. The conversation is painful but important: Rebecca articulates the critique Ruth has been suppressing. Maybe David isn't innocent in the way Ruth wants him to be. Maybe the system Ruth defended enabled exactly this kind of shielded culpability.

## Key Elements

- Ruth calling Rebecca for her perspective
- Rebecca's existing critique of David and finance
- The sibling dynamic: Rebecca the social worker vs. David the finance partner
- Rebecca's argument: complicity in systems that create criminals
- Ruth caught between children, between perspectives
- The critique Ruth has been suppressing: her own institutions enabled this
- The pain of hearing truth she doesn't want to hear
- Family as site of ideological conflict

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Seeking Rebecca's perspective and receiving more than she wanted. Her daughter articulates the critique she's been avoiding.
- **Rebecca Abramson (phone):** Social worker, the family's moral conscience. She loves her brother but won't pretend his choices were innocent.

## Setting

Carson in Rebecca's key lines - "complicit in systems that create criminals" as compressed moral argument. The conversation hurts because Rebecca isn't wrong.
