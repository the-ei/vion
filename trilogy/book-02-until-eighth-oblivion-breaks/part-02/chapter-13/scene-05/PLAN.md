# Scene Plan: Departure

## Summary

Morning, saying goodbye. David is calmer now - Ruth's presence helped, even if she can't fix anything. He asks her what she honestly thinks. Ruth struggles: as a jurist, she sees prosecutorial overreach and political exploitation. As a moral person, she sees how easy it was for David to participate in harm without knowing or caring. "I think," she finally says, "that the law wasn't built for this. And neither were we." She leaves for DC, her faith in institutional justice further eroded, but her love for her flawed son unchanged.

## Key Elements

- Morning goodbye: the ritual of departure after difficult visit
- David's request for honest assessment
- Ruth's struggle to integrate roles: jurist and mother, legal analyst and moral being
- Her final statement: law's inadequacy for novel harms
- "Neither were we" - Ruth including herself in the indictment
- Departure for DC: the physical journey as emotional transition
- Institutional faith eroded further
- Love for flawed son unchanged

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Struggling to give David an honest answer that encompasses her multiple perspectives. Her final statement is hard-won truth.
- **David Abramson:** Calmer but still afraid. Her presence helped even without fixing anything.

## Setting

- David's apartment entrance: the threshold of departure
- The car waiting: return journey beginning
- Morning light: a new day but no new answers

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard in the physical detail of goodbye: the embrace less awkward now, the specific gestures of departure. Ruth's internal struggle rendered through hesitation, false starts, the difficulty of finding words. Carson in her final statement - compressed, honest, inadequate to the complexity it addresses but the best she can offer. End on the departure, the physical movement carrying emotional weight.
