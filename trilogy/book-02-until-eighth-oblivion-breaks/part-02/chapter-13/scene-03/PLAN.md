# Scene Plan: The Attorney's Assessment

## Summary

Ruth meets with David's defense attorney, a woman she's known professionally for years. Over lunch, the attorney delivers the hard assessment: the legal merits are defensible, but this isn't about legal merits. David's firm has been chosen as a symbolic target. The conspiracy theory that has attached to the case - that financial interests orchestrated Book 1's crisis for profit - is nonsense, but it has momentum. The prosecution is riding that momentum even while officially rejecting the conspiracy framing. "They're prosecuting the narrative," the attorney says, "not the crime."

## Key Elements

- Ruth meeting the defense attorney: professional respect, institutional knowledge
- The hard assessment: defensible but irrelevant to political reality
- David's firm as symbolic target: not the guiltiest but the most useful
- The conspiracy theory attached: financial orchestration of Book 1
- The prosecution's strategy: riding momentum while disavowing its source
- "Prosecuting the narrative" - the legal system weaponized for story
- Ruth's institutional faith further eroded
- The gap between legal merit and legal outcome

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Hearing institutional failure described by someone she respects. The attorney's analysis confirms her worst fears about the system she spent her career building.
- **Defense Attorney:** Professional, competent, clear-eyed. She's not angry at the system, just describing its dysfunction with clinical precision.

## Setting

- A restaurant near David's apartment: neutral, professional, private enough
- Lunch as meeting: the rituals of professional consultation
- Manhattan outside: the city of legal power

## Timeline

- **Time:** Lunch, approximately 12:00-2:00 PM

## Stylistic Notes

Dialogue-heavy, the attorney's analysis delivered in precise legal language that nevertheless condemns the legal system. Ruth's internal response: recognition, grief, the death of something she believed. The lunch setting providing necessary contrast - civilized ritual containing uncivilized truth. Carson in the key phrases: "prosecuting the narrative" as compressed indictment.
