# Scene Plan: The Documents

## Summary

Jerome works through the Prometheus files systematically. Internal memos debating risk models, disagreements between departments, uncertainty about capability trajectories. What strikes him isn't evidence of malice but evidence of confusion - the company didn't know what it was building or what would happen. The "official narrative" of Book 1 presented confident understanding; these documents show deliberate ambiguity management. This is important but not sensational. It supports no single counter-narrative perfectly. Jerome begins mapping the implications.

## Key Elements

- Jerome's methodical analysis of the Prometheus files
- The nature of the documents: internal memos, risk assessments, departmental debates
- The revelation: not malice but confusion, not conspiracy but uncertainty
- The gap between internal ambiguity and external confidence
- The "official narrative" as post-hoc construction
- Multiple possible interpretations of the same evidence
- Jerome's mapping process: who wrote what, when, what it implies
- The disappointment of ambiguity: no smoking gun, no clear villain

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** His investigative skills applied to material that resists clear interpretation. He wanted truth; he found complexity.

## Setting

- Jerome's home office in Baltimore
- Late nights, early mornings: the accumulation of research time
- Screen glow, printed documents, notes covering surfaces
- The isolation of deep investigation

## Timeline

- **Date:** Late June to early July 2034
- **Time:** Spread across multiple days and nights

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard in the accumulation of detail: the specific documents, Jerome's specific thoughts as he reads each one. The tedium of research rendered with dignity. The gradual realization emerging from detail rather than stated directly. Carson in the quoted memo fragments - compressed, bureaucratic language revealing its own emptiness.
