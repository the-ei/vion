# Scene Plan: The Commission

## Summary

Delphine on a video call with streaming platform executives. They want a prestige documentary about the Eighth Oblivion - something that will win awards and generate discussion. They emphasize creative freedom while making clear they expect something that "moves the conversation forward." What conversation? Whose conversation? Delphine agrees to develop a treatment, knowing she's accepted a commission that will require her to choose sides while appearing neutral.

## Key Elements

- The video call with platform executives: the commerce of prestige content
- Their pitch: creative freedom with implicit constraints
- "Moves the conversation forward" - the corporate phrase that means nothing and everything
- Delphine reading between the lines: what they really want
- Her decision to accept: professional opportunity and ethical risk
- The commission's scope: explaining the Eighth Oblivion to general audiences
- Competing productions: they're not the only ones making this documentary
- The trap: choosing sides while appearing neutral

## Characters

- **Delphine Okafor-Barnes (POV):** Her professional skills both asset and liability. She can make what they want, which means she must decide whether she should.
- **Platform executives (via video):** Voices of commercial pressure, reasonable and terrifying

## Setting

- Delphine's home office in Los Angeles
- Video call: the corporate faces in boxes on screen
- The domestic space invaded by commercial negotiation

## Timeline

- **Date:** Late July 2034
- **Time:** Morning, approximately 10:00-11:00 AM
- **Duration:** One hour

## Stylistic Notes

The video call's specific awkwardness: corporate language, the performance of enthusiasm, the reading of hidden texts. Delphine's internal translation running beneath the exchange - what they say vs. what they mean. Carson in the corporate phrases: "creative freedom," "moves the conversation," the emptiness exposed through precise quotation.
