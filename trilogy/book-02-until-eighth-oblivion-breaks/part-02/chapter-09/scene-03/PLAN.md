# Scene Plan: The Prophet

## Summary

After the gathering disperses, Jerome secures a private interview with Nathaniel Crane. The former tech executive tells his conversion story: what he saw at Prometheus, why he left before Book 1's crisis, the experience that transformed his understanding. Crane doesn't claim certainty - he claims that the official narrative requires more faith than his interpretation. He names the anomalies Jerome has been privately tracking, offers explanations that fit disturbingly well. The interview becomes a philosophical sparring match between two intelligent men with different relationships to truth.

## Key Elements

- The transition from public gathering to private conversation
- Crane's backstory: VP at Prometheus, left six months before Book 1's crisis
- His conversion experience: described with precision rather than mysticism
- The critique of the official narrative: internal contradictions, suppressed data
- Crane addressing Jerome's specific concerns: the anomalies he's noticed
- The epistemological challenge: what counts as evidence when frameworks differ?
- Crane's intellectual humility combined with conviction
- The question Jerome can't dismiss: what if he's partially right?

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** His professional armor tested by an adversary who won't play the expected role. Crane isn't a charlatan he can expose; he's a serious thinker with a different interpretation.
- **Nathaniel Crane:** Charismatic but not manipulative. His technical background gives him credibility; his spiritual turn could be breakdown or breakthrough. He respects Jerome's skepticism while challenging its premises.

## Setting

- A small office adjacent to the main warehouse space
- Modest furnishings: desk, chairs, whiteboards with diagrams
- The sounds of the departing congregation audible through walls
- Late evening light through industrial windows

## Timeline

- **Date:** Late May 2034
- **Time:** Approximately 9:30-10:30 PM
- **Duration:** One hour

5 pages (~1,375 words)

## Stylistic Notes

Dialogue-heavy but with Knausgaard's psychological interiority running beneath the exchanges. Jerome's internal reactions to Crane's arguments, the micro-shifts in his certainty. Carson compression in Crane's descriptions of his transformative experience - language that gestures toward something unsayable rather than trying to capture it completely. The scene should feel like intellectual combat where both fighters earn respect.
