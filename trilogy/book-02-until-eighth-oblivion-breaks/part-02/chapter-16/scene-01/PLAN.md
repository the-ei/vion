# Scene Plan: Homecoming

## Summary

Jerome arrives home late, still processing the Kevin Zhou conversation. Denise is awake but distant - a different quality of silence than he's used to. He tries to share what he's learned, the excitement of the investigation, but she doesn't engage. "I can't," she finally says. "I can't keep being an audience for your work while you're absent from your life." The fight begins.

## Key Elements

- Jerome's late arrival: the physical return
- Still processing Kevin Zhou: his mind elsewhere
- Denise awake but distant: a different silence
- Jerome trying to share his excitement
- Her refusal to engage
- "Absent from your life" - the accusation
- The fight beginning: years of tension surfacing
- The house at night: familiar becoming strange

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Arriving home with professional excitement, confronting personal crisis he didn't see coming.
- **Denise Washington:** Her distance is new quality, accumulated frustration finally expressed. She's not angry at journalism but at absence.

## Setting

- The Washington home in Baltimore
- Late night: the specific quiet of after-midnight
- The entryway, then living room: spaces of confrontation

## Timeline

- **Date:** Mid-August 2034
- **Time:** Late night, approximately 11:30 PM-12:30 AM
- **Duration:** One hour

4 pages (~1,100 words)

## Stylistic Notes

Knausgaard in the domestic detail: the specific sounds of late arrival, the texture of the house at night. Denise's distance rendered through physical observation before it's named. The transition from professional excitement to personal crisis - Jerome's confusion as he reads a situation he missed. Carson in Denise's key line: "absent from your life" compressed to its accusation.
