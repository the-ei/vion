# Scene Plan: The Argument

## Summary

The confrontation that follows is years in the making. Denise articulates everything: the missed dinners, the distracted conversations, the way Jerome's work always comes first. She's not asking him to stop being a journalist - she fell in love with a journalist. She's asking him to be present in the hours between stories. Jerome's defenses are real but sound hollow: the work matters, the truth matters, someone has to do this. "And someone has to be married to you," she says. "Someone has to raise your son. Someone has to hold the space you keep leaving." They reach an impasse, both in separate rooms.

## Key Elements

- The accumulated weight of years emerging
- Denise's specific grievances: missed dinners, distraction, absence
- Her distinction: not journalism but presence
- Jerome's defenses: work matters, truth matters
- The hollow sound of true things used as excuses
- "Someone has to hold the space you keep leaving"
- The impasse: neither yielding
- Physical separation: different rooms

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** His defenses genuine but inadequate. He believes in his work and can't deny its cost.
- **Denise Washington:** Not asking him to change careers but to be present. Her years of accommodation reaching their limit.

## Setting

- Then separate rooms: the physical expression of impasse
- Night deepening around them
