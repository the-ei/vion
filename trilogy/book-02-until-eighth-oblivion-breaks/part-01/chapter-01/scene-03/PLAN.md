# Scene Plan: What Falls Through

## Summary

Ruth has lunch with her daughter Rebecca at a cafe near Rebecca's social work office in Oakland. Rebecca describes her caseload - families destabilized by the economic disruptions following the crisis, the health consequences Elena sees from the medical side manifesting as housing instability, child welfare concerns, addiction. Ruth listens, asks questions, offers what she can: professional connections, advice, a check for Rebecca's discretionary fund. The scene makes the abstract "aftermath" concrete through Rebecca's stories. Mother and daughter genuinely love each other but inhabit different worlds - Ruth's constitutional abstractions versus Rebecca's daily triage. When Rebecca mentions she's seeing someone new, Ruth is happy for her but feels her own loneliness sharpen. The scene grounds Ruth's institutional concerns in human reality.

## Key Elements

- Oakland cafe near social services office: the geography of care work
- Rebecca's exhaustion: visible, physical, accepted as normal
- Specific case stories: families falling through cracks the crisis widened
- Ruth as listener and helper: the limits of what connections and money can do
- Mother-daughter dynamics: love across different relationships to suffering
- Ruth's offer of money: complicated by class dynamics even within family
- Rebecca's new relationship: life continuing despite crisis
- Ruth's loneliness surfacing: Susan's absence made present by Rebecca's coupling
- The downstream effects of the crisis Ruth testified about: made personal
