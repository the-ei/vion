# Scene Plan: The Morning's Argument

## Summary

Ruth Abramson rises before dawn in her Berkeley home, eight months after Book 1's crisis. The scene establishes her daily solitude since Susan's death, the rituals of early morning that have become her defense against grief. She drinks coffee, reads the print newspaper that still arrives, checks headlines on her tablet. The crisis has migrated from front page to business section to occasional opinion pieces. Ruth attempts to draft an op-ed arguing for new regulatory frameworks to address AI governance - work she's been circling for months - but each sentence feels inadequate. Her legal mind keeps finding the gap between how law imagines technology and how technology actually functions. The scene builds her interiority through the specific textures of her morning, ending when she abandons the op-ed and looks at a photograph of Susan.

## Key Elements

- Ruth's early waking as response to Susan's absence (the bed feels wrong)
- The physical details of her Berkeley home: books everywhere, Susan's garden visible through windows
- The ritual of coffee preparation, described with Knausgaard specificity
- Print newspaper as generational marker and deliberate choice
- Tablet headlines showing crisis normalized into background news
- The op-ed draft: sophisticated arguments that feel hollow
- Legal frameworks versus technological reality - Ruth's expertise becoming obsolete
- Memory of Susan interwoven with present loneliness
- First hints of Ruth's doubt about her congressional testimony's impact

## Characters

- **Ruth Abramson (POV):** Central consciousness; alone with her thoughts and doubts
- **Susan Abramson (memory/photograph):** Ruth's late wife, present through absence

## Setting

- **Location:** Ruth's home in Berkeley, California - specifically the kitchen and study
- **Physical details:** Victorian house, book-lined rooms, Susan's garden going slightly to seed, morning light through fog
- **Atmosphere:** Solitary, intellectual, tinged with grief

## Timeline

- **Date:** Late February 2034
- **Time:** Approximately 5:30 AM to 8:00 AM
- **Duration:** 2.5 hours
- **Relation to chapter:** Opens the chapter, establishes Ruth's post-crisis isolation

## Page Target

6 pages (~1,650 words)

## Stylistic Notes

- **Mode:** Primarily Knausgaard - exhaustive detail of morning routine elevated to meditation
- **Sentence structure:** Long, flowing sentences for the routine; shorter when doubt intrudes
- **Interiority emphasis:** Ruth's legal mind as organizing principle
- **Key techniques:** The mundane made profound through attention; grief as texture not event

## Connections

- **To Scene 2:** Ruth's morning doubt about her expertise carries into the seminar
- **To Chapter:** Establishes the emotional and intellectual state Ruth carries through the day
- **To Part:** Opens Book 2 with institutional disillusionment embodied
- **Cross-references:** Newspaper mentions of investigations that will be revealed as deliberately buried (Chapter 7)
