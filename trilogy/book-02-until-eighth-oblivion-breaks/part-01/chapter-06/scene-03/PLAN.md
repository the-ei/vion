# Scene Plan: The Tasks

## Summary

Afternoon task work: furniture assembly at a suburban house, mystery shopping at a retail store. The scenes are compressed, showing the variety and indignity of task labor. The furniture assembly pays $50, takes three hours, requires tools Yusuf bought himself. The customer barely looks at him. The mystery shopping pays less, requires him to pretend to be a customer and report back - a kind of surveillance labor. Yusuf's interiority captures the ways this work makes him feel invisible, the ways he has developed skills that are not recognized as skills. A moment of anger when a task is canceled after he has already arrived. The app offers no explanation, no recourse. He drives home.

## Key Elements

- Furniture assembly: skilled labor, inadequate pay
- The suburban customer: barely looks at him, class dynamics
- Tools bought himself: the hidden costs of gig work
- Three hours for $50: the math that does not work
- Mystery shopping: surveillance labor, pretending to be a customer
- Reporting back: the layers of monitoring
- Skills unrecognized: Yusuf's competence invisible
- The canceled task: already arrived, no compensation
- No explanation, no recourse: algorithmic injustice
- Driving home: the day's accumulation
