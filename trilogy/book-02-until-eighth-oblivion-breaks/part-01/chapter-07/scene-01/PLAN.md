# Scene Plan: The Call

## Summary

Ruth is grading papers in her Berkeley study when Elena Park calls. Elena was one of Ruth's best clerks, now a mid-level attorney at the Department of Justice. The conversation is careful - Elena knows she is taking a risk. She describes what she has seen: the Prometheus investigation files, the way they have been moved between departments, the personnel changes, the pattern that adds up to deliberate derailing. Ruth listens, asks precise questions, knows how to receive information that cannot be officially acknowledged. After the call, she sits in her study, the implications sinking in. This was not bureaucratic failure - this was active protection of power. Everything she told the Senate Judiciary Committee was absorbed into a system designed to produce the appearance of action without the reality.

- The study setting: grading papers, Ruth's ordinary life interrupted
- Elena Park's call: the former clerk, now DOJ, taking a risk
- Careful conversation: what can and cannot be said directly

- Ruth's precise questions: the judge knowing how to receive sensitive information

- After the call: Ruth alone with implications
- Not bureaucratic failure: active protection of power
- Everything she testified: absorbed, neutralized, performance
- The Senate Judiciary Committee: her faith in the process, betrayed
