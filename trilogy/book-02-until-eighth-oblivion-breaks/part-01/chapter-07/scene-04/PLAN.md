# Scene Plan: What Rebecca Sees

## Summary

Dinner with Rebecca at Ruth's house. Rebecca looks exhausted; her work in social services is becoming impossible. Budget cuts have arrived, staffing is decimated, cases are being closed or transferred without proper resolution. Rebecca describes specific families she can no longer help, children falling through cracks that used to have safety nets. The scene connects Ruth's institutional concerns to human consequences - what Elena revealed is not abstract corruption, it is the active protection of systems that produce suffering. Mother and daughter are both tired, both angry, both unsure what to do. Rebecca asks about Ruth's health; Ruth minimizes. They talk about Susan, briefly - how she would have responded to this moment. The evening ends with love but no solutions.

## Key Elements

- Dinner at Ruth's house: mother and daughter, the routine of care
- Rebecca's exhaustion: visible, chronic, the toll of social work
- Budget cuts: the political becoming personal
- Specific families: Rebecca's cases, children falling through cracks
- The cracks widening: safety nets disappearing
- Connecting institutional concerns to human consequences
- Both tired, both angry: the shared exhaustion of caring
- Ruth minimizing her health: protecting Rebecca from worry
- Talking about Susan: what she would have done
- Love without solutions: the evening's ending note
