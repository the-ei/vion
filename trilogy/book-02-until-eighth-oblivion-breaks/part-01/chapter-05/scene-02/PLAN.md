# Scene Plan: The House on Edmondson

## Summary

Jerome visits his mother Lorraine in Baltimore. Her dementia has progressed - she knows him sometimes, does not other times. His sister Sandra manages the day-to-day care. The scene is Knausgaard mode at its deepest: the physical reality of caring for a parent with dementia, the routines, the moments of clarity and confusion. Jerome helps with lunch, sits with his mother while she watches television, has a conversation where she thinks he is his father. The emotional weight is immense but not performed - Jerome has been doing this for years, has made his peace (sort of). He and Sandra talk about logistics, money, the aide who comes three times a week. In caring for his mother, Jerome is reminded of what patience actually means, what limits actually feel like. This is different from journalism's impatience, its demand for resolution.

## Key Elements

- The Baltimore house: where Jerome grew up, now transformed by illness
- Lorraine's dementia: the long goodbye, the person who is and is not present
- Sandra as primary caregiver: the sibling who stayed close
- The physical reality of care: lunch, television, the routines
- Moments of clarity: Lorraine knowing him
- Moments of confusion: Lorraine thinking he is his father
- Jerome and Sandra's conversation: logistics, money, the aide
- The years of this: the adjustment already made
- Patience as lesson: different from journalism's demands
- Limits as lived reality: what cannot be fixed or exposed

## Characters
