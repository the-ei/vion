# Scene Plan: The Essay

## Summary

Carson mode - compressed, late night. Jerome in his hotel room, surrounded by notes from meetings that led nowhere. He starts writing - not the reported piece he came to D.C. to research, but an essay. About journalism and truth and what happens when exposure changes nothing. About his mother losing her memories and his son building new ones. About the year since the crisis: the year of our lord 2034, and what lordship means now. The writing is tentative, personal, unlike his published work. It may never see print. The chapter ends with Jerome still writing, past midnight, the city sleeping around him, truth-telling as prayer into void.

## Key Elements

- The hotel room: generic space, notes scattered everywhere
- The failed research: meetings that produced nothing publishable
- The essay beginning: not reporting but reflection
- Journalism and truth: what happens when exposure changes nothing
- His mother losing memories: the personal interwoven
- His son building the future: the generational counterpoint
- "The year of our lord 2034": the title, the question of power
- Tentative, personal writing: different from his professional voice
- May never see print: the privacy of the essay
- Past midnight, still writing: the act itself as meaning

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Central consciousness; writing as reckoning

- **DeShawn (thought):** His son, the future being built
