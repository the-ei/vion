# Scene Plan: The Source Who Wasn't

## Summary

Jerome meets Martin Reyes, a congressional aide who was a crucial source during the Book 1 crisis. The meeting is brief and frustrating: Martin has "moved on," does not want to revisit the story, offers nothing new. His body language suggests fear or exhaustion or both. Jerome pushes; Martin deflects. The scene shows how sources close up, how stories die - not through suppression but through the simple attrition of human reluctance to keep being brave. Jerome walks away angry, then just tired. He calls Denise from a park bench; her voice anchors him. She tells him about DeShawn's latest coding project, something Jerome does not fully understand. He does not mention that the world his son is building is the world he has been trying to expose.

## Key Elements

- The meeting with Martin Reyes: brief, frustrating, fruitless
- Martin's "moving on": the euphemism for fear or exhaustion
- Body language: what Martin communicates without words
- Jerome pushing, Martin deflecting: the dynamics of source management
- How sources close up: not suppression but attrition
- Jerome's anger becoming exhaustion: the limits of investigative persistence
- The park bench: liminal space for phone call
- Denise's voice: anchor, home, love across distance
- DeShawn's coding project: the son building the future Jerome fears
- The unspoken tension: the world his son is building

## Characters

- **Denise Washington (phone):** Jerome's wife, anchoring voice
