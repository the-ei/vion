# Scene Plan: The Hill

## Summary

Jerome Washington's meetings on Capitol Hill. The scene establishes Washington D.C.'s texture: the buildings that embody democratic ideals, the people who navigate democratic realities. He meets with staffers on the Senate Judiciary Committee - the same committee Ruth advised during the crisis. They speak in careful language: concerns were raised, recommendations were made, the process continues. Jerome pushes for specifics and receives none. His interiority captures the particular frustration of knowing more than he can print, of watching power absorb critique without changing. A moment of connection with one staffer who seems genuinely frustrated - but she cannot go on record. Jerome leaves the meetings with a notebook full of nothing usable, the story still out of reach.

## Key Elements

- Capitol Hill: the architecture of democracy, the halls of power
- Senate Judiciary staffers: careful language, institutional protection
- The same committee Ruth advised: institutional continuity, no change
- Pushing for specifics: Jerome's method, their evasion
- Concerns raised, recommendations made: the vocabulary of inaction
- Knowing more than he can print: the journalist's frustration
- The one frustrated staffer: genuine understanding, no record
- Power absorbing critique: the system's immune response
- The notebook full of nothing: documentation without usability
- Cherry blossom season: D.C.'s beauty as ironic backdrop

## Characters

- **Jerome Washington (POV):** Central consciousness; investigative journalist hitting walls
- **Senate staffers (several):** Unnamed, careful, speaking for the system
- **The frustrated staffer (female):** Moment of genuine connection, cannot go on record
- **Capitol environment (background):** Security, tourists, the machinery of democracy
