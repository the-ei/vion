# Scene Plan: The Book Event

## Summary

Back in D.C., Jerome attends a book launch at Politics and Prose - a colleague's book on constitutional reform. He sees Ruth Abramson across the room and they end up in conversation. They have never met in person, but Jerome interviewed her by phone during the crisis and cited her opinions in his reporting. Now they discover a kinship: two people who believed in their institutions and watched those institutions fail to respond. Ruth is more philosophical, Jerome more aggrieved. She mentions the op-ed she never finished (from Chapter 1). He mentions the story he cannot complete. Neither has answers. The scene ends with an exchange of contact information and a sense that this connection might matter later - two truth-tellers finding each other.

## Key Elements

- Politics and Prose: the D.C. bookstore, intellectual gathering place
- The book launch: colleague's work on constitutional reform
- Seeing Ruth across the room: recognition before introduction
- Their phone interview history: the connection already established
- The kinship discovered: two institutional believers watching institutions fail
- Ruth's philosophical approach: the long view, doubt without despair
- Jerome's aggrieved approach: anger at what exposure did not change
- The unfinished op-ed (Ruth): mentioned, connecting to Chapter 1
- The incomplete story (Jerome): his ongoing frustration
- Exchange of contact information: the relationship seeded
