# Scene Plan: The Waiting Room

## Summary

The first half of Elena's shift at the Phoenix community health center. The scene moves through multiple patient encounters, each briefly but specifically rendered: the diabetic grandmother negotiating medication costs that have tripled since the crisis, the construction worker with an untreated fracture because he cannot afford to miss work, the teenager whose anxiety has become debilitating. Elena's clinical competence is clear - she's genuinely skilled at reading bodies and establishing rapport. But the scenes accumulate weight: each patient carries a story she can only partially help with. The administrative burden appears throughout: electronic health records that demand constant attention, insurance pre-authorizations, the bureaucracy of care. Dr. Amara Osei appears as ally and mentor - they share a moment of dark humor about the gap between what they're trained to do and what they're actually able to do.

## Key Elements

- The clinic waiting room: overcrowded, under-resourced, the architecture of unmet need
- Multiple patient encounters: each a window into systemic damage
- The diabetic grandmother: medication costs as life-or-death math
- The construction worker: injury plus fear of losing work
- The teenager with anxiety: mental health crisis in young people
- Elena's diagnostic skills: reading bodies, establishing trust quickly
- Electronic health records: the time tax of documentation
- Insurance pre-authorizations: bureaucracy as barrier to care
- Dr. Amara Osei: Ghanaian-American physician, pragmatic ally, dark humor as survival
- The accumulating weight: each patient a story with no good ending

## Characters

- **Key techniques:** Accumulation creating weight; the particular made universal
