# Chapter Plan: The Verdict

## Summary

Jerome witnesses DeShawn presenting at a tech conference - his son fully inhabiting the world Jerome spent his career scrutinizing. The chapter explores intergenerational judgment from both directions: Jerome judging DeShawn's choices while DeShawn judges his father's relevance. But the conference itself becomes the occasion for unexpected complexity when DeShawn's presentation includes elements that reveal he's absorbed more of Jerome's values than either of them acknowledged.

The chapter wrestles with whether inheritance is rejection or transformation - whether DeShawn's tech career is a betrayal of Jerome's journalism or a continuation of it by other means.

## Scene Breakdown

### Scene 1: Arrival (Pages 1-7)
Jerome enters the conference center. Everything signals a world he doesn't belong to - the branding, the vocabulary, the young people networking with fierce casualness. He's here because DeShawn invited him, a peace offering after their previous conflict. Jerome watches from the margins, remembering his own professional confidence in rooms where he belonged. He sees journalists present - not investigators like him but content producers, indistinguishable from the PR people. Knausgaard-mode: the texture of a tech conference, Jerome's alienation in detail.

### Scene 2: The Presentation (Pages 8-14)
DeShawn takes the stage. His presentation is about building accountability systems into AI development - not quite ethics (which the industry has learned to perform) but something structural. Jerome is surprised. DeShawn cites historical precedents, systems journalism, investigative methods. He never names his father but Jerome hears his own principles translated into different language. Afterward, DeShawn is surrounded by admirers. Jerome waits. Carson-mode: fragments of the presentation, Jerome's interior response, the gap between what he expected and what he witnessed.

### Scene 3: Reckoning (Pages 15-21)
Father and son finally talk, in a quiet corner away from the networking. DeShawn explains what he's trying to build - accountability that doesn't depend on exposure but is embedded in systems. Jerome asks questions, genuine ones, not challenges. DeShawn admits that Jerome's work shaped him even as he rejected its methods. "I'm not trying to tell the truth to people. I'm trying to make it harder for systems to lie." Jerome calls Denise that night, trying to articulate what shifted. The chapter ends with a tentative new understanding - not resolution but possibility.
